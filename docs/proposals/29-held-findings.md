# Held findings: a guard holds back the request, not the finding — a decision record

**Status: decided on 2026-11-17, built and tested against fakes, not deployed. Changed on 2026-11-18: an automation is no longer a guard (the last section).** Nothing here changes Azure ([rule 29](../../AGENTS.md)), and nothing runs until the function app that carries it is published.

## What was wrong

In the real runs of 16 and 17 November the Investigator found things that Meternaut then held back, and a person saw only counts on the start page: "held back: managed by an automation" for ten findings (the dearest worth hundreds of dollars a month) and "held back: not checked who changed it" for six. The guards were right. A change by hand on a resource that something rewrites by itself is undone, a deletion is not asked on a log that was not read, and an impact that was not checked to the end is not asked. They were wrong to make the finding invisible: the waste is real, and the person who can act is not an approver of a click but whoever looks after what rewrites the resource, or after the facts that are missing. A finding that nobody sees is a saving that does not happen.

## What existed

A held finding was a case (status `needs_human` or `proposed`) with no request. The History said why (`case.held`, with `held back: …` words that stay as they are), the run counted it, and the start page said "Held back: N findings" in one sentence. Savings found counted its figure in "Waiting for a person", which says that a person has to answer, though nobody could. No letter, card or page showed its figure, its reason or who may know. The Investigator was given it again as an open question and could judge it again ([proposal 15](15-learning.md)).

## What is decided

**The guards stay** (all but one: since 2026-11-18 an automation is a warning on a request and no guard; see the last section). `brief.held_reason` (who changed it was not looked up; what depends on it was not checked), a finding that cannot say what to look at, and a request that was taken back because the rules changed keep deciding what Meternaut may ask a person to approve.

**A held finding is shown as a request of its own.** `Approvals.settle`, which the Watch calls at its end, gives each such finding a request in the new state `held`. It is the existing request that asks a person whether a finding is worth acting on, so the answers that exist work and no second answer system was made: a question, "This is not mine", "Decide later" and "Not worth it" with its reason ("it is still needed", "the figure is wrong", "it is already handled", "another reason"). It has **no Approve**: there is nothing to approve, a sign-in to Azure is never begun for it, and nothing changes in Azure.

**It is no open request.** The state `held` is not `open`, so everything that lists the open requests does not see it: the Decisions page has no row for it, no card is made for it, no letter is sent for it as a request, and `ADVICE_OPEN_MAX` does not count it (a test fills the places and still shows the held findings). It is also no unanswered request in the lessons.

**Its words are written once** (`brief.HOLD_WORDS`, used by the page, the letter and this record):

| Reason | Heading | The guard's sentence | What it still needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| automation (until 2026-11-18; its words stay for the requests made before) | Something that runs by itself changes it | Something that runs by itself changed it N days ago and would change it back, so a change by hand would not last. | Whoever looks after what runs by itself can change it there. Meternaut asks about it as usual when nothing has changed it for 14 days. |
| unchecked | Nobody has looked up who changed it | Nobody has looked up who changed it, so Meternaut does not ask anyone to delete it. | Meternaut looks it up at its next search. Nothing is asked of you. |
| impact | What depends on it was not checked to the end | What depends on it was not checked to the end, so Meternaut does not ask anyone to remove it. | Meternaut checks again at its next search. You can look at what uses it in the meantime. |
| nothing | Meternaut has nothing to say about it yet | Meternaut has no figure, no name of a thing and no words of Azure Advisor for it, so it cannot say what to look at. | Nothing is asked of you. Meternaut asks again when it can say what to look at. |
| rules | Meternaut's rules changed | Meternaut's rules changed, and it had asked about this on less evidence than it asks for now. | Nothing is asked of you. Meternaut looks at it again at its next search. |
| waiting | Waiting for a free place | Nothing holds it back any more, but 30 requests are open already, each worth at least as much as this one, so Meternaut has not asked about it yet. | Nothing is asked of you. Meternaut asks about it when a place is free. |

`waiting` is no guard: it is what a held finding becomes when its guard has ended and no request can be made for it yet (see "When a guard ends").

Who may know is the owner line that every page has (the tag, who made the resource, who changed it last): the people that the finding would have gone to are the same list as for any request.

## Where a person sees it

- **Savings found.** A row "Found, and held back from a request" after "Waiting for a person", and below the table a section with one heading for each reason and one line for each finding: a link to its page, the figure with its level and its basis, how sure the AIs are, the guard's sentence and who may know. A finding is in one row only: it leaves "Waiting for a person" when it has its request, so no figure is added twice.
- **"Found in all" counts a held finding's figure.** It is found waste that a person still has to act on, outside Meternaut. The tile says how many of the findings that it counts are held back ("Of which held back: 3"), and the level is the finding's own (Expected or Scenario), as for any other.
- **The page of the finding** (`/why/<id>`, and `/ok/<id>` for a person who is named) has a box with the heading, the sentence and what is needed, the same brief as any request, and the form without Approve. It says that the request ends when nothing holds the finding back, and not on a date; the words after "Ask or tell", "Decide later" and "This is not mine" say where the finding is ("Held back" on the Decisions page, and Savings found) and name no date.
- **The start page** lists, under "Held back", each held finding that the person is named for, with a link and its figure.
- **The weekly letter** has one short block after the decisions: at most three lines (the largest first), each with the title, the figure with its level, the sentence and what is needed, and "N more are held back" for the rest. A person who has only such findings gets a letter that says so in its subject. It has no button of its own and one link, as every letter has; it goes once a week, as the weekly summary does, and is not counted by the weekly budget. A person who has answered the finding does not get it again.
- **No card, ever.** The round of cards looks at open requests only, and a test shows a person who chose Teams getting nothing for a held finding.

## When a guard ends

The automation stops changing the resource, the Activity Log is read, or the impact check completes. The finding is an open question for the Investigator, so at an investigation it is judged again by the normal path (`advise`), with the facts of that day. If nothing holds it back then, it becomes a normal request, and at the end of that Watch `settle` closes the held request, with `takenBack = released` (the page says "No longer held back") and one line in the History, `case.released`, with the words `no longer held back` and the words of the reason that it was held for. The line has no money and no id. The same happens when the finding is dropped, answered, or gets a request of any kind, and a reason that changed is kept as the new reason. **A guard that ends by itself, with no new judgment, does not close it:** the facts of that day were not read, so only a judgment decides.

**It is closed only when a real request exists, or when nobody is to be asked.** If the guard has ended but the normal path makes no request (the places for requests are full), the held request stays, with the reason `waiting`, so that the finding does not lose the one place where a person can see it; it is closed as above once a real request exists. If nobody is to be asked (little is at stake, or a person answered it lately) it is closed at once. A resource that a person marked do-not-touch after the request was made closes it too, with `takenBack = left-alone`, the page word "Left alone" and the History word `closed: a person marked it do-not-touch`.

## Limits

At most 50 held requests are made in one Watch (the rest at the next one); none is made when nobody is on the list of approvers or when what is at stake is not worth a person's time (the rule `why_not_ask` of any request); a finding that a person answered in the last 30 days, or marked do-not-touch, or that has a request open, gets none. A held request never runs out by the clock, so a person may answer it weeks after it was made (a test keeps it open and listed 400 days after it was made): the time of the answer is written into its `created_at`, and the 30 days count from the answer. The figure of the case is copied onto a held request at every Watch, so that the letter, the start page and Savings found say the same after a new price. Held requests have a query of their own (`plans_held`, the state `held`), and `plans_recent` leaves them out: the Table store reads the first thousand rows in the order of the key, not of time, so a held request found by a scan of the recent plans would be lost past a thousand plans, and a bulk of them would crowd the newest.

## What is not proved

- It has not run against the real store or the function app. The first Watch after it is published makes the held requests of every held finding at once, up to 50.
- The ten and the six findings of the real runs were not replayed through it, and no stranger has read the five sentences.
- A held finding that the Investigator never cites again stays held until a person answers it or it stops being open: there is no age limit, and the open-questions list gives each its turn, not every investigation.
- The answers of the last 30 days are read from `plans_recent`, as `advise` always did, which the Table store builds from the first thousand rows by key and not by time: past a thousand plans an old answer may be missed. That limit is the normal path's own; the held requests do not depend on it.
- "N days ago" is as old as the last judgment's reading of the Activity Log.
- The people who may know are not always on the list of people (the owner of an automation is often somebody else): the page shows what the tags and the log say, and Meternaut writes to nobody who is not on the list ([rule 15 (g)](../../AGENTS.md)).
- A weekly mention of a finding that asks nothing of a person may be unwelcome; the only mutes are the settings that exist (the weekly budget, "Pause messages").
- Whether the tile should count a held figure at all is a judgment: the page says what it counts.

## 2026-11-18: an automation no longer holds a finding back; it warns

**Decided on 2026-11-18** by the orchestrator, under the owner's rule 38 and his words "the app should find waste, ask the people, and chase them until they act". Built and tested against fakes, not deployed. It changes nothing in Azure ([rule 29](../../AGENTS.md)).

**Why.** In the real runs 3 and 4 every finding that the Investigator kept ended "held back: managed by an automation": the dearest was a Bastion at about 200 US dollars a month, then an SRE agent at about 317, a search service at about 73 and storage accounts. In an estate that pipelines deploy, almost every resource was written by something that is no person within 14 days. A held finding asks nobody anything (no card, no letter as a request, no escalation), so the best findings of the owner were the same passive list that Azure Advisor gives and that people ignore. The guard was right about one thing only: a change by hand on a resource that something rewrites would be undone, so Meternaut must never offer a click that changes it. A request of advice changes nothing in Azure, and the right advice for a resource that something rewrites is to change it where it is defined.

**What changed.**

- `brief.held_reason` no longer says `automation`. The other reasons (`unchecked`, `impact`, `nothing`, `rules`, `waiting`) are about what Meternaut has not read or decided itself, and stay holds. `AUTOMATION_DAYS` (14) and `_automated_at` stay.
- `brief.automation_note(case, now)` is the warning: the sentence of the old guard with how long ago ("N days ago") and where to act: "Something that runs by itself changed it 2 days ago and would change it back, so a change by hand would not last. Change it where it is defined (the template or pipeline that deploys it)." It is code's text, with no name and no id, and it is empty when nothing that is no person wrote to the resource within 14 days. What Meternaut planted in a group of its own is exempt, as before.
- An ordinary request of advice (`Approvals.advise`, `build_advice`) keeps the time of that change (`preconditions["automated"]`), so every channel says the same words from `brief`: the page of the request (`/why`, `/ok`), the Teams card, the letter of the ladder, the approval letter and the weekly desk show it as one line, "Heads-up", under the figure. It keeps all its answers (a question, "This is not mine", "Decide later", "Not worth it", "Worth acting on"), changes nothing in Azure, and has no Approve of a change.
- **No change plan is made for such a resource, by any path.** A plan of a change needs a group of Meternaut's own, the tag and the planted list ([the gates](../../src/case-functions/cases/families/gates.py)), and nothing else makes one (`build_plan` has one caller). A test runs every detector (the investigation, Advisor, three older ones) and every recommendation against three settings (no group where a change may be made, another group, the group itself without the planted resource) and gets advice each time.
- **The migration.** The first Watch after the deploy finds held requests with the reason `automation` (the real store has about ten). `Approvals.settle` turns each into an ordinary open request of advice **in place**: the same request and case, state `open`, the held mark gone, the warning kept, its time of making kept as the start of its wait (`waiting_since`, which the ladder, the cost of waiting and the pages count from), sealed again. They go in the order that the pages rank by: the largest figure first (a saving, else what the finding costs; a finding with neither last), then the finding that has waited longest, at most `PROMOTE_NEW` (5) in one Watch, and none while the places for requests are full (`places_full`, below). The rest are held with the reason `waiting` ("Waiting for a free place") and the next Watch goes on, five more: a held request that waits for a place is made an ordinary request in place as soon as a place is free, in the same order, whatever held it. A finding that nobody is to be asked about (little is at stake, or a person answered it in the last 30 days) is closed as released, and a held request with another reason is never promoted. A second Watch changes nothing.
- **The places are 30 now (`ADVICE_OPEN_MAX`, from 10), and a finding that is worth more is never held behind them.** Measured from the History of the last 14 days: 92 requests were made (79 of advice, 11 to delete a disk, 2 to stop a machine) and 12 ended by the clock, which the escalation branch opens again at its first Watch. With rule 38 nothing expires, so a place is freed only by an answer, and the old requests that come back could fill ten places and hold the fresh, dearest findings (a firewall at about 616 US dollars a month, a Bastion at about 411) as "waiting for a free place". 30 is no flood for one person: the page, the letters and the cards rank by the figure, the largest first, and what they say is bounded by rules that exist (one letter a day from the ladder, three cards a week, one desk letter a week). `places_full` holds a finding back only when 30 requests for findings that the AIs did not settle are open and the finding's stated saving is no larger than the smallest of theirs; a larger one gets its request (so more than 30 can be open, the largest ones), and a finding with no saving never takes the place of one that has. A finding that waits is shown, in the plain words "30 requests are open already, each worth at least as much as this one" (`waiting`): `settle` makes its held request, and the Savings page lists the held ones under "Waiting for a free place" with their figures.
- **Every list ranks the same way.** The Decisions page (the start page) and the weekly desk, which uses its code, rank by the figure of the request (a saving, else the cost of what a change could save; a request with none last) and then by how long ago the finding was first seen (the page's own tie-break; the page of a request shows the start of its wait besides); the migration uses the same order. The places compare the stated saving only. The weekly letters of the ladder list what waits longest first, on purpose.
- The pages and counts: the History no longer writes `case.held` with `held back: managed by an automation` and its vocabulary stays valid for the old lines; each promotion is one `plan.created` line with `was: held`. The start page, Savings found and the weekly letter list these findings as waiting requests, not as held, and "Held back: N" no longer counts them. The page of what the AI looked at says "a request was made; an automation manages the resource". `HOLD_WORDS["automation"]` and `TAKEN_BACK["automation"]` stay: old held and taken-back requests are shown as they were.

**What a person receives on the first day.** Measured against fakes with five held requests of one person that were made five days before (`tests/test_managed_resources.py`, class `TheFirstDay`); the timer sends cards, then letters, then the ladder, each minute, and the desk goes at the end of the Watch.

| The person | Cards | Letters | In this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chose e-mail (the default) | none | the reminder of the ladder: **one** letter for all five ("5 decisions have waited without an answer", one line each, each with its Heads-up), because each has waited three days; never more than one a day. On the desk day (Tuesday by default, once a week) the weekly desk too: one letter, one line for each row of the Decisions page | the Watch ends (the desk letter, on the desk day), then the next minute of the timer sends the reminder |
| Chose Teams | the card that says who writes (if the person was never written to), then **three** advice cards (the weekly budget; the other two wait for Monday), each with its Heads-up | the same reminder letter (escalation goes by letter only) and the same desk letter | the Watch ends (the desk letter), then in the next minute the cards, then the reminder |

The second Watch promotes five more, so a person with ten requests is written to the next day by one letter again, and a request that was held less than three days before gets its reminder on its third day. **Nothing here is a burst**: five requests at most from the Watch, one letter a day from the ladder, three cards a week, one desk letter a week.

**What is not proved.**

- Nobody has read the words. A person may find the warning obvious (the team that owns the pipeline knows that it rewrites the resource), or may read it as "not mine". The only answers are the ones that exist.
- The warning keeps the time when the request was made, and its "N days ago" grows with the request: it says what was true then. It is not made again when the automation stops, and a request for several resources of one kind shows no warning on its line of a letter (the page of each request has it).
- The cards of the weekly budget go in the order that the store lists the requests (the newest start first), not by figure, so the three cards are not always the three dearest. The order of the promotion is by figure; the order of the cards is not.
- A finding that an automation manages and that the AIs did not settle waits for a place when 30 requests are open and it is worth no more than the least of them. It is shown as held ("Waiting for a free place", with its figure) and asked about when a place is free, in the order of the pages. The cap compares the stated saving: a finding with no saving is worth nothing to it, whatever it costs, so it waits behind priced ones; and more than 30 requests can be open, because a finding that is worth more than the smallest of them gets its request.
- The count of ten that the first run showed is replaced by 30 on a measurement of 14 days (92 requests made, 12 closed by the clock and now opened again), not on how people answer: whether 30 open requests are too many for one person is not proved.
- It has not run against the real store or the function app. The real held requests were not replayed; whether five of them are promoted, one letter and three cards go out and the people answer is not tried. "N days ago" is as old as the last judgment's reading of the Activity Log. The person who looks after an automation is often not on the list of people ([rule 15 (g)](../../AGENTS.md)).
