# The loop — a proposal

**Status: approved on 2026-11-05 (the owner: "GO", for the whole solution), built and deployed the same day. The way a change is made was replaced on 2026-11-05 by [run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md), which is built, deployed and proved once on the real estate. A page below is true of the real environment only as far as the changelog says that it was checked there: Watches made plans for planted disks, the approval letters reached the owner, the function app answered on an always-on Basic plan (it was on Flex Consumption for a short time from 2026-11-07, two measurements of 2026-11-08 found that the platform held its requests for 50 to 60 seconds, and it was on a Basic plan for part of that day; the owner ordered Flex Consumption again on 2026-11-08, the constant says Flex, and the move ran the same day (the page answers, after a setting removed the hold): [the web page on Flex Consumption](07-private-by-default.md#the-web-page-on-flex-consumption)), and the owner approved three plans on the page. The first two were deleted by a separate identity, the writer (`id-meternaut-runner`), now retired and removed; the third was deleted as the approver, and the Activity Log names the owner as the caller. The check two days later is next.** This page says how a case that the Watch finds becomes a person's answer and, for the one kind of resource that Meternaut may touch, a checked change to Azure: the function app, the approval, the change that the approver makes, the mail, the resources that Meternaut plants itself, and what stays with a person. It follows the [first cases](02-first-cases.md), whose "Not built, and next" this is, and it builds on the [access requirements](03-access-and-onboarding.md). The pages are designed in [the web pages](../experience/06-web-pages.md).

## What it is for

Until now Meternaut found leads, two AI roles judged them and the History recorded the work, and nothing reached a person except by opening a portal workbook. A workbook is a poor first contact: it reads its data from the browser, so a network that resolves the Azure Monitor query addresses to a private IP address (a corporate VPN, for instance) shows "Query failed", and the person has nobody to answer to. The loop closes that gap:

1. a case that is proposed for deletion becomes a plan that **code** writes, with its evidence, what could go wrong and how to undo it;
2. the person who may approve it gets a letter with a link;
3. the person signs in, reads the plan on a page and answers it;
4. right after the person presses "Approve", the function app makes the change **as that person**, with that person's own rights, after it has read the resource again;
5. later the result is checked against Azure and against the cost lines, and the person is told.

Only a resource that Meternaut planted itself, in its own resource group, can reach step 4. Everything else is still reported and never changed; when it needs a person, the person is asked whether it is worth acting on, and the answer is recorded and changes nothing in Azure ([the decision brief](09-the-decision-brief.md)): no other project's resource is a target ([rule 26 of AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)). That is a choice and not a need of the design ([run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md)), and relaxing it is a later decision of the owner: the pilot keeps the guard so that the first real approvals touch only resources that nobody else owns.

## The path of one case

| Step | What happens | Who or what does it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Find | The Watch (daily, or when a message is put in the control queue) lists leads, puts the waste that Meternaut planted itself first (it is the one kind that a plan is made for), drops the ones judged in the last seven days so that the slots of a run go to new leads, and takes up to three. Since 2026-11-06 an Investigator agent in Foundry also looks through the estate by itself, with generic read-only tools, and each resource of its verified findings becomes a case ([the Foundry agents](06-foundry-agents.md)) | The Watch, as before |
| 2. Judge | Two AI roles on two different models, then the guards in code (lock, keep tag, too new, managed group). A planted resource is not held back for being new, and the models are told so: they see the typed fact `planted` and no age, because a disk of age zero made the Skeptic ask whether it was only just made (the first real run on a planted disk ended as "needs a person" for that reason, and no plan was made). Every other lead is shown as before, and every other guard still holds. The findings of the Foundry Investigator pass the same guards, a Skeptic agent that tries to break them, and the same decision in code | The Watch |
| 3. Record | Each case is written to the store with its steps, models and facts. A case that is proposed for deletion of an unattached managed disk or an unassigned public IP address, with the planted tag, in the project's own resource group (the setting `CHANGE_SCOPES`, which replaced `CHANGE_RESOURCE_GROUP` on 2026-11-08; the changes that a plan can hold are in [families of change](14-families-of-change.md)), gets a **plan** | The Watch, then the approvals code |
| 4. Tell | A letter to each person who may approve: the first letter to a person says who Meternaut is and what it will and will not do; the approval letter has the link `https://<function app>/ok/<plan>` | The mailer, as the shared mailbox |
| 5. Answer | The person signs in with Microsoft Entra and opens the page. It shows the exact change, the evidence level, what could go wrong, how to undo it and the saving. The answers: approve, decline, ask a question, or "not mine". The form carries the plan's 8-character code and a one-use token, so an answer to a plan that has changed, or a form that was used already, is refused. Declining, asking and "not mine" are recorded at once. **Approve** records nothing yet: it sends the person to Microsoft Entra to sign in to Azure, and the change runs right after that. The button never says so; the page says once that the change runs with the person's own permissions. A change that cannot be undone asks once more first ([the decision brief](09-the-decision-brief.md)) | The person |
| 6. Sign in to Azure | Approve sends the person to Microsoft Entra to sign in for Azure management ([run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md)). With a session at Entra the sign-in is silent; whether Entra asks again is the tenant's choice (Conditional Access, Azure's own multifactor rule), not Meternaut's. Entra asks for consent once where the tenant needs it, and returns the person to the function app with a one-use code. The function app checks the `state` of the request, redeems the code with the app registration's federated credential, and checks the ID token: issuer, expiry, tenant, this client, the nonce, and that its object id (`oid`) is the one of the person who is signed in to the function app | The person, Microsoft Entra and the function app |
| 7. Change | Only now the function app claims the plan's action (a second approver at the same moment records nothing), reads the switches again and records the approval. With the person's own token it re-reads the resource and the locks, checks every precondition again, reads the switches once more, deletes the resource once and reads until Azure says that it is gone. Azure checks the person's own rights: if the person may not delete the resource, Azure refuses and nothing changes. The token is held in memory for this one request | The function app, as the approver |
| 8. Tell again | A letter says what ran and what the outcome was | The mailer |
| 9. Check | Two days later the timer reads the resource again (it must still be gone) and the cost lines of the resource's cost scope, with an identity that only reads; the case becomes `verified`, and a last letter says so | The timer |

The plan's words (title, what changes, what could go wrong, how to undo it, the evidence level) are written by code from the lead's typed facts. A model contributes only the objections that the Skeptic raised, shown on the page in a labelled box, and never a sentence of the plan. The plan's code is the first 8 characters of the SHA-256 of the plan id, the family, the resource id and the expiry time: a changed plan has a new code, so an old approval cannot be reused for it.

## What is built

The app's list of allowed calls holds the account's table address and nothing else for this part. A one-use token (of a form, or the `state` of an approval's sign-in) is stored as a hash, never as the token. The record of a `state` also holds the PKCE verifier and the nonce of that sign-in for ten minutes at most, and it is deleted when the `state` is used | The cases app's system-assigned identity, with Storage Table Data Contributor on the account (a role that the workflow already gives to a function app) |

People must be assigned to the app, and the platform lets in only the object ids of the list of people | `id-meternaut-console-auth` (no Azure role) |
| The people | One setting, a list of the people of the function app: object id, name, roles (`owner`, `approver`, `ops`) and the address that letters go to. A person who is not on the list sees nothing, and no letter goes to an address that is not on it | None |
| The approvals | The code that makes a plan, takes an answer and records it. It is the one `Decider` that the pages call | The cases app's system-assigned identity |
| The change, as the approver | The only code of the app that changes Azure, and only with the approving person's own token. `delegated.py` takes an approval in two steps (Approve starts it, `GET /arm/return` finishes it), and `runner.py` makes the change with its own HTTP client and its own list of calls: read and delete of a managed disk and of a public IP address in one resource group, and a read of management locks. It cannot change Azure: its list of calls holds three reads | The cases app's system-assigned identity, the identity that only reads |

An outside job strips the tags from unattached disks every night at about 22:56 UTC, so `plant` also puts the tags back on every planted disk and public IP address that is there already: approve a planted disk the same day it is planted | The deployment identity, as for any other resource of the project |

## Who may do what

- A person is known only by the object id that the platform's sign-in puts in a header after it has signed the person in. A form field, a link or a cookie never says who someone is. The app trusts the header only because it refuses to serve anything when the platform's sign-in is off.
- **owner**, **approver** and **ops** are the roles. An approval needs the role *approver* and the person's object id in the plan's list of approvers, which the code takes from the list of people when the plan is made. The change itself needs one more thing that no list gives: the person's own right in Azure to make it. Azure checks that, and Meternaut cannot supply it. The pause page needs *ops*; the permissions page needs *ops* or *owner*. Every person on the list can read the History and manage their own settings, notes and decisions.
- A person cannot approve for somebody else, and cannot approve a plan that is not open, has expired, or has a changed code. A person who signs in to Azure with another account than the one signed in to the function app is refused.
- A person can say "not mine": the plan is closed and the case goes back to the people who decide by hand. A person can ask or tell something: it is recorded, the plan stays open and nothing is approved. An answer to advice (a request that changes nothing in Azure) needs the role *owner* or *approver* and the person's name on the plan ([the decision brief](09-the-decision-brief.md)).

## Safety

- **Nothing of Meternaut's writes to Azure.** The Watch, the function app, the mailer and the timer hold no right to change Azure. The test of the app's packaging allows a PUT or a DELETE in the store (Table Storage) and in the change code (Resource Manager) and in no other file; the web package makes no call at all. The change's list holds five calls (read and delete of a disk, read and delete of a public IP address, read of locks), and the timer's list holds the three reads. Azure checks the person's own rights on top of the list.
- **What the change checks again, right before it deletes.** The plan's state, expiry and code and the switches were checked at the press and again at the return from the sign-in, and an approval by a named approver is recorded for the exact plan. Then the code reads the resource again and stops unless the resource is in the one group, subscription and kind that a change may be made on, still exists, is still unattached or unassigned, still has the planted tag, is not marked do-not-touch and has no management lock (read at the resource and at its resource group). A check that says no leaves the request open: nothing is changed, the approval is taken back, the action is kept as `withdrawn` with the reason in plain words, and a later approval is a new one, with a new sign-in and new one-time tokens. Two things still end the action as `failed` and close the plan: a plan that is not the one that was approved (its digest), and a refusal by Azure itself. It deletes once; it never retries a delete. It then reads the resource until Azure answers 404 (up to six reads, five seconds apart).
- **The page looks first.** Before a page offers Approve for a change, it runs the same checks (the family's own re-check) with the app's own identity and a list of calls that holds reads only. If one would refuse the change, the page says so in a box at the top, in plain words, and has no Approve; if the look cannot be made, one line says so and Approve stays, because the runner looks again with the approver's own rights and changes nothing when a check fails. The look is advice and writes no record.
- **There is no delay and no cancel, on purpose.** A delay needs a token that is stored until it ends, and this design stores none. The press of "Approve" is the confirmation: the page says that the change runs right after it and cannot be undone.
- **Switches.** `all`, `changes` and `messages` can be pulled on the pause page by a person with the role *ops*. A pulled `changes` switch stops an approval at the press, at the return from the sign-in and right before the delete, so an approval that is in the middle of its sign-in when changes are paused stops; `messages` stops letters. A switch that cannot be read counts as pulled.
- **One approval at a time.** The action of a plan is claimed once, so two approvers who answer at the same moment cannot both record an approval or both delete: the second sees that the plan is being approved at this moment and records nothing. A HEAD request to `/arm/return` is refused, so a prefetch cannot use up a sign-in. The `state` holds the resource that was approved, and the return refuses a plan that names another. The function app redirects only to the Microsoft Entra sign-in address, and the change's HTTP client follows no redirect, because it would send the person's token to the new address.
- **The platform may record the code and the `state`.** They are in the URL of `GET /arm/return`, which the platform may record in its request telemetry (not verified). Both work once, and the code is bound to the PKCE verifier (kept on the server) and to the managed identity's client assertion, so a recorded code cannot be redeemed by anybody else. The access token is never logged, stored or shown. The first real run checks it with a query over `requests` for the `/arm/return` URL ([run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md#security-analysis)).
- **No secret, anywhere.** The sign-in uses a federated credential, and so does the redeem of an approval's code; the mail uses an identity and Exchange's scoped role; the store uses an identity; a change uses the approver's own token, which exists for one request. The test that scans for keys (rule 23) covers the new code and templates, and the app registration is checked by a tool that refuses an app that holds a secret or a certificate.
- **The plan is not a model's.** A model's output is checked against the same schema as before; the plan is built from typed facts, and a test fails if a model's sentence reaches a plan or a letter.
- **A letter says what matters and nothing that is not needed.** The subject starts with `[Meternaut]`, says the point and holds no resource name and no amount of money. The sender's display name ends with "(AI)" and the message carries the header `x-ai-generated: true`. The mailer sends only to an address on the list of people, only as that person on the list, and keeps a record of the kind, subject, time and outcome of each letter, and never its text. Graph's answer 202 means that Graph took the letter, not that it arrived: delivery is proved by receipt.
- **Letters are limited.** A person's weekly budget, a pause that a person set and the choice of the weekly desk hold a letter; an approval letter is the one kind that no budget holds, because a plan that waits for an answer has to reach its approver. A letter that is held or refused is written down with the reason, and a letter that was held or failed is offered again by the timer after 15 minutes, at most 12 times, because Exchange can take up to two hours to apply a new right to send. A letter that was sent is never sent again.
- **Forms.** A form is accepted only as a POST from the same site, in the form content type, at most 16 KB, with a one-use token that belongs to the person and the plan. Every page carries a strict content security policy and no script; a page never shows a trace, and a fault says only that nothing was changed. The policy's `form-action` allows the function app and `https://login.microsoftonline.com`: the approve form is answered with a redirect to the sign-in, and Chromium-based browsers check that redirect against `form-action` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Headers/Content-Security-Policy/form-action) says that browsers differ).
- **The loop is optional.** If the store's name is not set, the app is the Watch alone, as it was before. If it is set, every setting that the loop needs has to be valid, or the loop does not start. The settings of the sign-in to Azure are set together or not at all: without them the loop works and nobody can approve a change, and the page says so.

## Going through the requests, quiet hours and two people

Built and tested against fakes; **not run in Azure**, so by [rule 32](../../AGENTS.md) none of the three is done until it has.

- **Going through the requests.** With two or more requests waiting, the Decisions page has one link, "Go through the N requests one by one", to the one with the most at stake. After each answer the result page offers "Next request" as its main button and keeps the way back as a link. Each request keeps its own page, its own code and its own form, and no route takes more than one plan, so no answer covers several. A request that the person has answered, asked about or put off is left out of the walk, and so is one that is not theirs. The walk stores nothing: `?walk=1` only adds a line at the top that says how many wait after this one.
- **One sign-in to Azure for each approval, kept.** The sign-in is bound to one plan: its `state` holds the plan, the plan's digest, a PKCE verifier and a nonce, and the change is made in the request that the return starts. One sign-in for several approvals would have to keep approvals that nobody has signed yet, or make several changes in one request that has a time limit, one token and, for each, a possible claims challenge. Either loosens the binding of one digest to one approval that [run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md) keeps. The request has no `prompt`, so the sign-in is silent while the person's session meets the tenant's policies: the cost of keeping it is one redirect for each approval, and a second factor where the tenant asks for one.
- **Quiet hours.** A person can set a time to be left alone from and until, in a time zone of their own (UTC when none is known); the default is none. Inside the hours no letter and no Teams card is sent, whatever its kind, and there is no override (rule 13). The mailer holds a letter with the words "quiet hours", and the rounds that offer a letter or a card again look at the hours first and write nothing, so waiting uses none of the 12 tries and nothing is sent twice. The weekly summary and the letter of a tripped switch are made again after the hours, from what is true then. A window may cross midnight, and a change of the setting while something waits applies at the next look. A person who chose Teams gets the letter 20 minutes after the hours end, so the card goes first. A person who saved Settings before this, with the old default of 18:00 to 08:00, now has those hours.
- **Two people for a larger change.** Off until the owner saves a rule on the page `/ops/two-people` (kept in the settings table; ops and the owner read it, the owner changes it): a saving of at least so many **US dollars** a month (the system has no euro figure and no exchange rate), and/or kinds of change. A plan that the rule covers when it is made gets the approval class R3, which is in the plan's digest: a rule saved later changes no open request, and a request cannot be made a one-person request afterwards. Then the first person gives a confirmation of the plan's code and digest on the page, with no sign-in to Azure and never from a card; a different person who is named as an approver approves it and signs in to Azure with their own rights, and the change is made with the second person's rights. The person who confirmed cannot approve: the check is made at the form (before the sign-in token is used), again after the action is claimed, and by the runner before any call to Azure. Either person can decline or say that it is not theirs, and that ends the request. A confirmation of another form of the plan does not count, and the same person can confirm again. Both answers are in the History. With fewer than two approvers listed nobody can approve it, the page says so, and the rule page refuses to switch on. Limits: no letter is sent when someone confirms (the letter at the start says that two people are needed; the Decisions page says who confirmed); two accounts of one human count as two people; a person who loses the role after confirming stays counted until the request ends.

A person who owns the place does each of these once, with their own sign-in. Each has a tool that reads before it writes, changes nothing without `--apply`, refuses to run in a pipeline and removes nothing. ## Known limits

Written down so that nobody has to find them.

- **The change is made in the same app as the Watch.** It uses its own HTTP client with its own list of calls, and it holds the person's token for one request, but it runs in the same process and the same deployment as the Watch and the function app. A flaw that gives an attacker code execution in the app during an approval could use that token for whatever the approver may do at Azure, until the token expires. A separate app for the function app and the change would shrink that exposure ([run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md#security-analysis)).
- **Nobody can cancel after the press.** There is no delay and no cancel button: the press of "Approve" is the confirmation. A second approver who is told about a plan cannot stop the first one's approval. For a larger change the owner can switch on [the rule that two people approve](#going-through-the-requests-quiet-hours-and-two-people); it is off until then.
- **When Azure refuses the change** (403, or a claims challenge that asks for another multifactor sign-in), the plan closes as failed for every approver and a new plan follows on a later run. There is no step-up flow with the `claims` parameter yet. If the store fails between the claim on the plan's action and the recorded decision, the function app releases the claim on a best-effort basis ([run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md#known-limits)).
- **Quiet hours, the walk through the requests and two people are tested against fakes only.** None of them has run in Azure ([above](#going-through-the-requests-quiet-hours-and-two-people)).
- **Approvals are not counted against the weekly budget of letters,** by design, so a plan always reaches its approver; a person who wants fewer of them can pause messages, and then the plan expires.
- **Teams is built and not deployed** ([Teams and Outlook](13-teams-and-outlook.md)). Until it is, the person is told by letter only. A card is posted into a chat with the person, which a guest of the tenant may not get; it is untested. A card cannot carry the sign-in to Azure either: it opens the plan page.
- **The mail is accepted, not proved to be delivered.** Graph's 202 is not receipt. The first delivery to a person's inbox in another organisation is the proof, and a mail-flow rule that rejects mail from the mailbox to anybody who is not on the list of people is not made by any tool here.
- **Exchange needs time.** A new role assignment or a changed scope applies to live sends after 30 minutes to two hours.
- **The saving of a new resource is unknown at first.** A resource that was made a moment ago has no cost lines, so the plan shows no saving and says so; the check two days later reads the cost lines.
- **Only two kinds of resource, and only what fits in a request.** The change code cannot delete anything else, and it cannot resize, stop or buy anything. A request waits for a disk or a public IP address, which Azure deletes in seconds. A change that takes minutes, a change on a schedule and a purchase each need a design of their own ([run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md#known-limits)).
- **The History workbook still depends on the viewer's network.** The function app shows the same runs and cases from the store.
- **The sign-in to Azure is proved once on the real estate** (2026-11-05: one approver who had the right, the consent given beforehand, no Conditional Access refusal). A refusal by Azure, a sign-in that needs consent, a Conditional Access refusal and a claims challenge for another multifactor check have not been seen there; the first of these is tested with fakes only.
- **A form sent after the sign-in has expired is untested.** The platform then answers the POST with a redirect to Microsoft Entra. The page's `form-action` now allows the sign-in address of Microsoft Entra, so a Chromium browser should follow that redirect. This has not been tried. Reloading the page starts a new sign-in, because a GET without a session is sent to Entra.

## Not built, and next

The proof that a Teams card reaches a person (and a test of whether a guest of the tenant can receive it), the agents' move into the Foundry Agent Service as hosted agents, a separate app for the function app and the change, forwarding a finding to its likely owner (it contacts a person who is not on the list, which needs the owner's word first: rule 15 (g)), and more families of change. A family has to fit [run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md): a plan that code writes, a person's answer, the person's own sign-in to Azure, a re-check right before the change, and a check afterwards. A change that takes longer than a request, such as a resize of a virtual machine, needs a design of its own. A change on a schedule is not possible. A purchase is advice that a person carries out with their own right to buy. A customer's own pipeline is the option that fits an estate that is managed as code.

An alert when a timer round fails is built and not yet made in Azure: the rule `timer-round-failed` of the [governance template](../../environment/governance/main.bicep) reads the error lines that the four timer functions log when a round fails, and the host's own failed invocations of them, every hour, and mails the owner through the action group of the AI security ([the proposal](10-ai-security-and-governance.md)). The action `governance` makes it, and until it has run the drift check says that the rule is not there.
