# Alternative prices: what a switch to another tier or size would cost, from Azure's public price list — a decision record

**Status: decided on 2026-11-17. The tool that shows the prices is built, tested against fakes, and read once against the real public price list (counts and numbers only, below). The form `alternative` of a change, which prices a switch from those prices, is built on it ([below](#the-form-alternative-a-switch-priced-by-code)), and a person is told on the page, in the card and in the letter that the alternative has not been checked for features. No model was called for either. Not deployed.**

## What was asked

Many of the best savings are a switch to another tier, size or SKU of the same service. The Investigator can see from its measurements that something is bigger than its use needs, but the bill holds only what is paid now, so it could not price the alternative, and a saving that nobody can price is not one that a person can decide on. An independent read of a real estate priced such switches from Azure's public price list. Meternaut has to do the same, for any service, with code doing the arithmetic and no model writing an amount ([rules 26 (d) and 30](../../AGENTS.md)).

## The tool: `price_alternatives`

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Input | `meter`: the handle of a meter of one resource, as `cost_meters` showed it (`m3`). `within`: `product` (the default) or `service`. `offset`, to read a long table page by page. The meter of a whole type has no region, so it is not offered |
| What leaves the app | One GET of the price list, with no token. The address holds four typed words: the service and the product of the meter, as the bill gives them (its category and its subcategory), the region of the resource, and the currency (US dollars). Each word passes the check that `cost_meters` applies to the words of a meter, and a word that fails makes no call. No name, tag, id, group or amount of the estate is in it |
| What comes back | A table of pay-as-you-go prices in dollars: a price handle (`p1`, `p2`, ...) that stands for the line in this run only, the SKU and the meter, the price of one unit, the region (or `global`), and `steps` when the price changes with the quantity; the product is given once when it is the same for every row. Only the lines in the unit of measure of the meter are listed (`other_units` counts the others), the lowest price first, and a price with steps after the others, because the price of its first step is not the price of a quantity (in the repo's snapshot of the list, 58 of 93 lines with steps have a free first step). A price with steps has no handle, so the code that prices a switch cannot take one. A price of nothing is no price: it is left out of the table, has no handle and is not offered, for it would save all that a meter costs (a review of the first version read one service in one region and found 126 of its 942 lines with one price at 0 dollars, among them free operations). A text of the list that is not a short word of the platform is shown as `other`, as a word of a meter is |
| The line of the meter itself | `current`: the line whose product, meter and unit are the words of the meter on the bill, with `retail` (its list price), `paid` (what the bill paid for one unit over the window: the cost divided by the quantity) and `rate_ratio` (paid divided by retail). `match` says `found`, `none` or `several`. The list price (`retail`) of that line is kept for each meter in the register of the run (`Toolbox.retail`) for the code that prices a switch (the form `alternative`, below): what remains after a switch is the list price of the alternative over it, so that the rate that the bill pays against list (a negotiated or a reserved one, or one above list) is carried over to any other line, and the figure does not pretend that the estate pays list prices. A list price does not depend on the window of the bill or on the ask, so a longer window for the meter does not make it stale, and a later ask that did not find the line leaves it (a cap may have cut that read before the line); it is taken away only by an ask that finds that the line has steps, or that several lines with the words of the meter disagree. A bill that paid nothing or gave money back has no `paid` and no ratio, and no switch is priced for it |
| What it does not say | Nothing about which line is better, and no list of what to look at. It shows prices. The Investigator decides, and says what the alternative must still do |

**The product of the meter, by default.** A page of the list holds 1,000 lines and about 600 KB, and one service has more than 7,000 pay-as-you-go lines in one region, so a read of a whole service is slow, and the list answered HTTP 429 to a burst of reads from one machine. The product of the meter is a few dozen lines at most, one page, and it is where the next size of the same series is. `within service` reads every product of the service, up to five pages (5,000 lines), and says `complete: false` when it did not read the whole list.

**The lines that hold everywhere, too.** The list names the region of a price that holds in every region `Global`, and writes some such prices with no region at all (the repo's own snapshot of the list, [`price-queries.json`](../design/cost/price-queries.json), has 14 pay-as-you-go meters with an empty region, in 4 services). The dearest meter of the top service of a real bill is a `Global` price, so a read of the region of the resource alone found a line for 4 of the 11 meters of that service, and not for the dearest. The address asks for the region, for `Global` and for the empty region, a line with no region is read as `Global`, and the line of the region wins when there are both. A resource in the region `global` is asked for `Global` and the empty region. The real list accepted the three-part filter.

**What counts as the line of the meter.** The product, the meter and the unit of the line are those of the bill: the category of a meter on the bill is the service of the list, its subcategory the product, its name the meter, and its unit the unit. The words must be equal. When several lines have them, the line of the region is the one; when the lines that are left disagree on the price the match is `several` and there is no line and no rate (the SKUs of one meter share its price, and that is not a disagreement). A line whose price has several steps is shown with `steps` and has no rate and no list price kept, because what the bill paid for one unit mixes the steps. A price that the list gives twice with two values is left out, and so is a price of nothing and a line that is not a pay-as-you-go price in dollars (a reservation, a dev/test price, a savings plan, another currency). Each line that the list gives is also checked here: its service must be the one that was asked for and its region the one that was asked for, `Global` or none, whatever the filter of the address says, so that a line of another service or region is never priced.

**Limits of a read.** At most five pages and 6,000 prices for one read, a page of at most 3 MB (a real page is about 600 KB: the transport is told to read that much and one more character, so that a page cannot fill the memory, and a longer one is refused as `unavailable` with the code word `TooLarge`), and 20,000 prices held by a run. A read takes at most 24 seconds and never more than the time that one call of the toolbox may take (45 seconds): a try of a page takes at most 8 seconds and never more than the time that is left, and a second try (for a list that is busy or fails, waiting at most 5 seconds) is made only when the time that is left holds it. The 8 seconds are the timeout of one socket operation, as the standard library gives it, not of a whole answer: a host that sends a character at a time can hold a page for longer, the clock of the read is checked between pages and not inside one, and what bounds a page is its length. `complete` is false when a cap or the time ended the read, and when lines were cut to the cap. A list is read once for a service, a region and a product in a run, whether it worked or not. A read that fails (the call, an answer that is not the list, a page that is too large, a next page somewhere else) answers `unavailable` with the platform's error code word and never its text, a note says so, and the rest of the investigation goes on. A host that gives no answer at all is tried once in a run: the failure is kept for the whole run and answers every later ask, whatever service, region or product it names, without another try (a first deployment whose subnet cannot reach the list then loses at most 8 seconds, once). A failure after a page that was read keeps the prices of the pages before it: the answer has `complete: false`, `ended: unavailable` and the platform's code word, and a note says so.

## The form `alternative`: a switch priced by code

A finding states a switch as `{"action":"switch","effects":[{"handle":"r1","meters":["m4"],"remains":{"form":"alternative","price":"p3"}}]}`: one meter of the resource, and a price handle that `price_alternatives` showed. Code, never the AI, works out what it saves ([proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md) says how every form is priced).

| Part | What code does |
|---|---|
| Which price | Only a handle that the tool showed in this run for that meter (`Toolbox.offers`: the rows of the pages that it showed and its current line), so a handle that was invented, or shown for another meter, is refused. The line must be of the same service as the meter, in the unit of the meter (else the plain reason that the units differ) and one price for the whole quantity (a price with steps has no handle in the register, and is refused here again). The region is the one that the tool read for that meter. |
| The arithmetic | The quantity of the meter at the same monthly rate as the cost that it replaces. A meter costs a month at three rates ([run-rate](24-priced-findings.md#priced-at-what-it-costs-now-and-a-change-stated-in-the-quantity-of-a-meter)): the last 7 days, the mean since it began, a typical day. A quantity at the same rate is that cost over what the bill paid for a unit (`paid`: the cost of the window over its quantity), so what remains after the switch is one share for every rate. Like for like, the alternative costs its list price over the list price of the current line (`retail`: the rate that the bill pays against list, whatever it is, is carried over); at list prices alone it costs its list price over `paid`. |
| A range | The two shares are the two ends of the range: the larger gives the low end of the saving, which is the one that counts, and a share of 1 or more at either end is no saving. A meter whose current line has no list price kept (no line, several, a price with steps) is not priced and the reason says that the list price of the current meter is not known: with one share (the list price over `paid`) a bill that pays above list would have its saving overstated, and an alternative at the price of the current line would show a saving. |
| No saving | An alternative that costs as much or more at either end (a share of 1 or more), a bill line with no cost per unit, a current meter whose list price is not known, and a price that is no price (a price of nothing too), make no figure, each with its own plain sentence. |
| The level | Scenario: the choice is the AI's, and carrying a rate over is an assumption. The figure is counted once like every other (a bill line once in a finding, the low end only, never above what the resource costs). |
| What is said | The basis ends "the alternative has not been checked for features", on the page, in the card and in the letter, because a list price shows neither features nor limits. The sentence is on every basis that rests on an alternative, whatever the form of a group: the figure of a group whose rules differ says how many of its bill lines rest on an alternative and ends with it, and resources, or meters of one resource, that switch to the same price keep the rule when their bills pay them at slightly different rates (the basis then says "the rate of each bill line"). The first AI is told to say what the alternative must still do and how it knows; the second AI is shown the words of the alternative (through the check that the tools make of a word of a meter and a tighter one of its own: letters, digits and single spaces, at most 40 characters and 6 words, else `other`, so that a long phrase in Title Case cannot reach it), its list price, the rate and the shares that remain, and a doubt keeps the figure and the finding needs a person. A review of the real Storage list found that about 23 percent of its product words fail the tighter check (names over 40 characters, and names with a hyphen such as `RA-GRS`) and are shown to the second AI as `other`: that is accepted, for it still sees the price, the rate and the shares, and the words that it does see are short. |

An invented example: 600 hours cost 60 dollars (0.10 an hour). The line of the meter is listed at 0.125, so the bill pays 0.8 of list. The alternative is listed at 0.0625. Like for like it costs 0.0625 / 0.125 = 0.5 of what the meter costs, so 30 are saved; at list prices alone it costs 0.0625 / 0.10 = 0.625 of it, so 22.50 are saved. The figure is 22.50 to 30.00 a month, and 22.50 counts. A bill that pays above list: the bill pays 0.10 and the line of the meter is listed at 0.08, so it pays 1.25 of list. An alternative at 0.06 costs 0.06 / 0.08 = 0.75 of the meter like for like (15 saved) and 0.06 / 0.10 = 0.6 of it at list prices alone (24 saved): 15 counts, where one share of 0.6 would have counted 24. An alternative at 0.08, the price of the current line, costs a share of 1 like for like and is refused as dearer, where one share of 0.8 would have counted 12. For a meter that rose in the last week (23 days of 1 dollar and 7 of 2, for 370 hours), the same two shares (0.5 and 0.625) apply to each rate: 30, 37 and 60 a month at a typical day, the mean and the last 7 days. The figure is 11.25 to 30.00, and not what a trailing quantity of 30 days would give (370 × 0.0625 × 0.8 = 18.50 off 37).

## Which client, which call

The Watch's own client reads it, with the Watch's list of calls, which gets one more exact entry: `GET https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices` (`http.PRICE_CALL`). It is the entry that the Study's list already held ([proposal 15](15-learning.md)), defined once and used by both lists. The list sees an address without its query, so it pins the method, the host and the path, and nothing else of the host: no write, no other path, no other port. The query is made by `cases/prices.py` from checked words only, and a test pins it. The call carries no token (a test checks the headers), so no role is needed: the call is on no line of [`environment/access-requirements.json`](../../environment/access-requirements.json), the test of that file names it as a call that needs none, and the drift check probes that the list lets the GET through and refuses a POST and another path. The list writes the address of the next page with the port 443; the app follows it only on the same host and path, asks again without the port, and refuses any other.

## What a real read showed (2026-11-17, read only, counts and numbers only)

The real tool ran, with its real client and the real list, over the words of 23 meters that the real bill showed (the dearest meters of four services of the top spend: 5, 2, 6 and 10 meters). The bill was read with a person's own read-only sign-in, and no name or id of it is kept.

- **Every meter found its line:** `match` was `found` for 23 of 23, in the region of the resource or, for some, as a `Global` line.
- **The rate ratio:** 1 for 17 meters, within 0.01 of 1 for 2, above 1 for 3 (1.087, 1.1025, 1.2485: why is not examined) and not worked out for 1, a price with steps. The estate mostly pays list prices.
- **Alternatives of the same unit exist:** the product of the meter listed from 1 to 52 lines in its unit (a median of 23), and for 22 of the 23 meters there was at least one other line; the whole top service had 24 lines in 6 products (17 distinct prices).
- **Paging:** the read of a product was one page, a service of 1,672 prices came in two pages (about 1 MB, 0.9 seconds), and one of more than 5,000 lines stopped at the cap of five pages (3 MB, 5.7 seconds), said `complete: false` and still found the line of the meter.
- **A fault that only the real list could show:** it writes the address of the next page with `:443`, which the first version refused; fakes now write it too.
- **The list throttles:** HTTP 429 came after a burst of reads from one machine, and none came when the reads were 5 to 6 seconds apart. The page does not say what the limit is.

Facts about the list are from [the Azure Retail Prices REST API overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/rest/api/cost-management/retail-prices/azure-retail-prices), read on 2026-11-17 (the page is dated 2026-01-06): no sign-in is needed; the filter fields include `serviceName`, `productName`, `armRegionName` and `priceType`, and their values are case sensitive; at most 1,000 lines come back for a call, with the link to the next page; the types of price are `Consumption`, `Reservation` and `DevTestConsumption`; the list holds prices in US dollars, and this version holds the Commercial cloud only. The version `2023-01-01-preview` that the app asks for is the one the page names.

## Access at each level (rule 12)

The list is public, so the tool needs no access of its own at any level. What it needs is a meter handle, which comes from the bill that the Watch reads with the Cost Management Reader role (R2): a Watch that cannot read the bill has no meter and the tool answers `unknown-handle`. A bill in another currency has no meter handle either, because `cost_meters` shows dollars only. An agreement with negotiated or reserved rates is what `rate_ratio` is for: it is worked out from the cost lines that the Watch can read, and a person who sees the figure sees that it rests on the rate that the bill shows.

## What is not proved

- **That the app's network reaches the host.** The Study reads the same host with its own client and the network requirements name it ([`tools/cases-check.py`](../../tools/cases-check.py)); whether the function app's subnet reaches it is for the platform team, and nothing here ran from the app. A failed read is `unavailable` and the investigation goes on. A host that gives no answer is tried once in a run (8 seconds at most), so a failure of the network at that moment ends the tool for the rest of that run, and the next run tries again.
- **That the words of the bill match the list for every service.** They did for 23 of 23 meters of four services. A meter whose words differ has no line and no rate. Marketplace products, other agreements' meter words and a meter renamed inside the window were not looked at.
- **Why three rates are above 1.** A resource in another region than its row, a price that changed inside the window, or usage that the list prices in steps could each do it. The ratio is one number for the whole window.
- **That an alternative does the same work.** The tool shows prices and nothing else, and code does not check features or limits for the form either: the figure says so in its words. They are judged by the Investigator from its measurements and from what the Study read, by the second AI and by a person.
- **That the rate of the bill carries over to another SKU.** A reservation, or a rate that was negotiated for one size, may not. That is why the low end of a switch is the larger of two shares, one like for like and one at list prices alone; the real rates of three meters were above 1, and for them the share like for like is the larger one.
- **That the list price of the current line is the one that the bill's meter belongs to.** It is the line of the same product, meter and unit in the region of the resource (the SKUs of one meter share a price). A line that the tool does not find, or finds with several prices or with steps, leaves the meter without a list price, and no switch is priced for it.
- **That a clock bounds a read.** The 8 seconds of a try are the timeout of one socket operation; the clock of the read is checked between pages. A host that sends a character at a time could hold one page for longer than the 24 seconds of the read; the length of a page is what bounds it.
- **That the share of a month is one share for every rate.** It holds when the meter bills one unit price all through its window; a price that changed inside the window, or units that are priced in steps, break it (the tool shows the ratio as one number for the window).
- **That the Investigator states a switch well.** No model was called for the form either: whether it names the right meter, a price that fits, and says what the alternative must still do, is for the first real run.
- **That the Investigator uses the tool well.** No model was called for this change.
- **The limit of the list.** It is not documented; the tool reads once for a list, and takes a refusal for `unavailable`.
- **What it leaves out.** Reservation, dev/test and savings plan prices (a commitment is a change of its own: [proposal 17](17-reservations-instructions-only.md)), other currencies, and the Government clouds.
- **What the tests pin.** Mutation-checked one at a time: the global lines and the lines with no region, each cap, the length of a page, `complete` when lines were cut, the price given twice, a price with no first step, the match by region and by price, the host of the next page, that no token is sent, the currency and the kind of price, the length of a text, the check of the words of the address, the region `global`, the unit filter, the rate for a price with steps, the check of the words shown (also the product and the region that the list gives), the read once, the region that is not a word, the scope of a read, the register of list prices (kept when a longer window replaces the meter and when a later ask was cut before the line, taken away only by an ask that finds steps or lines that disagree), the bound on what a run holds, the code word of a failure, the meter of a whole type, a bill that gave money back or cost nothing, the order and the handle of a price with steps, a price of nothing (not in the table, no handle, not offered, a price with a free first step too), the service and the region of each line that the list gives, the length that the transport is told to read and a page of exactly the cap and one more, the prices that stay when a later page fails, a host that gives no answer (once in a run, with a fake clock), the time of a try and of a read, and a second try only when the time holds it. For the form: the price handle that was never shown, shown for another meter, of another service, in another unit and with steps; a negative, no-number, zero and zero-quantity price or bill line; the share at one and above one; the list price of the current line (none, nothing, a negative or no number: the switch is not priced), a bill that pays above list and a switch to the price of the current line; the same share at each rate of a meter that rose; the digest, the sentence and what the Skeptic is shown, with the words of an alternative (the phrase in Title Case of 44 characters, one of 41, one of 40, seven words, marks); a group with one rule and a group with two; the same rule for resources and for meters of one resource whose bills pay slightly different rates (three rates), and the sentence on the basis of every group that rests on an alternative, whatever its form; and the registers of the real tool (`Toolbox.offers`, `retail`, `prices`) as what the arithmetic reads, also after a longer window replaced the meter.
