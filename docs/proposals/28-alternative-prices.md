# Alternative prices: what a switch to another tier or size would cost, from Azure's public price list — a decision record

**Status: decided on 2026-11-17. The tool that shows the prices is built, tested against fakes, and read once against the real public price list (counts and numbers only, below). The form `alternative` of a change, which prices a switch from those prices, is built on it ([below](#the-form-alternative-a-switch-priced-by-code)), and a person is told on the page, in the card and in the letter that the alternative has not been checked for features. No model was called for either. Not deployed.**

## What was asked

Many of the best savings are a switch to another tier, size or SKU of the same service. The Investigator can see from its measurements that something is bigger than its use needs, but the bill holds only what is paid now, so it could not price the alternative, and a saving that nobody can price is not one that a person can decide on. An independent read of a real estate priced such switches from Azure's public price list. Meternaut has to do the same, for any service, with code doing the arithmetic and no model writing an amount ([rules 26 (d) and 30](../../AGENTS.md)).

## The tool: `price_alternatives`

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Input | `meter`: the handle of a meter of one resource, as `cost_meters` showed it (`m3`). `within`: `product` (the default) or `service`. `offset`, to read a long table page by page. The meter of a whole type has no region, so it is not offered |
| What leaves the app | One GET of the price list, with no token. The address holds four typed words: the service and the product of the meter, as the bill gives them (its category and its subcategory), the region of the resource, and the currency (US dollars). Each word passes the check that `cost_meters` applies to the words of a meter, and a word that fails makes no call. No name, tag, id, group or amount of the estate is in it |
| What comes back | A table of pay-as-you-go prices in dollars: a price handle (`p1`, `p2`, ...) that stands for the line in this run only, the SKU and the meter, the price of one unit, the region (or `global`), and `steps` when the price changes with the quantity; the product is given once when it is the same for every row. Only the lines in the unit of measure of the meter are listed (`other_units` counts the others), the lowest price first, and a price with steps after the others, because the price of its first step is not the price of a quantity (in the repo's snapshot of the list, 58 of 93 lines with steps have a free first step). A price with steps has no handle, so the code that prices a switch cannot take one. A price of nothing is no price: it is left out of the table, has no handle and is not offered, for it would save all that a meter costs (a review of the first version read one service in one region and found 126 of its 942 lines with one price at 0 dollars, among them free operations). A text of the list that is not a short word of the platform is shown as `other`, as a word of a meter is |
| The line of the meter itself | `current`: the line whose product, meter and unit are the words of the meter on the bill, with `retail` (its list price), `paid` (what the bill paid for one unit over the window: the cost divided by the quantity) and `rate_ratio` (paid divided by retail). `match` says `found`, `none` or `several`. The list price (`retail`) of that line is kept for each meter in the register of the run (`Toolbox.retail`) for the code that prices a switch (the form `alternative`, below): what remains after a switch is the list price of the alternative over it, so that the rate that the bill pays against list (a negotiated or a reserved one, or one above list) is carried over to any other line, and the figure does not pretend that the estate pays list prices. A list price does not depend on the window of the bill or on the ask, so a longer window for the meter does not make it stale, and a later ask that did not find the line leaves it (a cap may have cut that read before the line); it is taken away only by an ask that finds that the line has steps, or that several lines with the words of the meter disagree. A bill that paid nothing or gave money back has no `paid` and no ratio, and no switch is priced for it |
| What it does not say | Nothing about which line is better, and no list of what to look at. It shows prices. The Investigator decides, and says what the alternative must still do |

**The product of the meter, by default.** A page of the list holds 1,000 lines and about 600 KB, and one service has more than 7,000 pay-as-you-go lines in one region, so a read of a whole service is slow, and the list answered HTTP 429 to a burst of reads from one machine. The product of the meter is a few dozen lines at most, one page, and it is where the next size of the same series is. `within service` reads every product of the service, up to five pages (5,000 lines), and says `complete: false` when it did not read the whole list.

**The lines that hold everywhere, too.** The list names the region of a price that holds in every region `Global`, and writes some such prices with no region at all (the repo's own snapshot of the list, [`price-queries.json`](../design/cost/price-queries.json), has 14 pay-as-you-go meters with an empty region, in 4 services). The dearest meter of the top service of a real bill is a `Global` price, so a read of the region of the resource alone found a line for 4 of the 11 meters of that service, and not for the dearest. The address asks for the region, for `Global` and for the empty region, a line with no region is read as `Global`, and the line of the region wins when there are both. A resource in the region `global` is asked for `Global` and the empty region. The real list accepted the three-part filter.

**What counts as the line of the meter.** The product, the meter and the unit of the line are those of the bill: the category of a meter on the bill is the service of the list, its subcategory the product, its name the meter, and its unit the unit. The words must be equal. When several lines have them, the line of the region is the one; when the lines that are left disagree on the price the match is `several` and there is no line and no rate (the SKUs of one meter share its price, and that is not a disagreement). A line whose price has several steps is shown with `steps` and has no rate and no list price kept, because what the bill paid for one unit mixes the steps. A price that the list gives twice with two values is left out, and so is a price of nothing and a line that is not a pay-as-you-go price in dollars (a reservation, a dev/test price, a savings plan, another currency). Each line that the list gives is also checked here: its service must be the one that was asked for and its region the one that was asked for, `Global` or none, whatever the filter of the address says, so that a line of another service or region is never priced.

**Limits of a read.** At most five pages and 6,000 prices for one read, a page of at most 3 MB (a real page is about 600 KB: the transport is told to read that much and one more character, so that a page cannot fill the memory, and a longer one is refused as `unavailable` with the code word `TooLarge`), and 20,000 prices held by a run. A read takes at most 24 seconds and never more than the time that one call of the toolbox may take (45 seconds): a try of a page takes at most 8 seconds and never more than the time that is left, and a second try (for a list that is busy or fails) is made only when the time that is left holds it; a list that asks to wait is waited for once, up to 20 seconds, and that wait is not a part of the 24 ([below](#the-price-book-the-wait-for-the-list-and-the-warm-up-2026-11-21)). The 8 seconds are the timeout of one socket operation, as the standard library gives it, not of a whole answer: a host that sends a character at a time can hold a page for longer, the clock of the read is checked between pages and not inside one, and what bounds a page is its length. `complete` is false when a cap or the time ended the read, and when lines were cut to the cap. A list is read once for a service, a region and a product in a run, whether it worked or not, except a list that asked to wait: that is asked again when the time has passed. A read that fails (the call, an answer that is not the list, a page that is too large, a next page somewhere else) answers `unavailable` with the platform's error code word and never its text, a note says so, and the rest of the investigation goes on. A host that gives no answer at all is tried once in a run: the failure is kept for the whole run and answers every later ask, whatever service, region or product it names, without another try (a first deployment whose subnet cannot reach the list then loses at most 8 seconds, once). A failure after a page that was read keeps the prices of the pages before it: the answer has `complete: false`, `ended: unavailable` and the platform's code word, and a note says so.

## The form `alternative`: a switch priced by code

A finding states a switch as `{"action":"switch","effects":[{"handle":"r1","meters":["m4"],"remains":{"form":"alternative","price":"p3"}}]}`: one meter of the resource, and a price handle that `price_alternatives` showed. Code, never the AI, works out what it saves ([proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md) says how every form is priced).

| Part | What code does |
|---|---|
| Which price | Only a handle that the tool showed in this run for that meter, or for a meter of the same resource with the same service, product and unit (`Toolbox.offers`: the rows of the pages that it showed and its current line, [below](#a-list-that-serves-every-meter-that-shares-it-and-a-charge-that-the-other-price-does-not-have-2026-11-21)), so a handle that was invented, or shown for a meter that does not share its list, is refused. The line must be of the same service as the meter, in the unit of the meter (else the plain reason that the units differ) and one price for the whole quantity (a price with steps has no handle in the register, and is refused here again). The region is the one that the tool read for that meter. |
| The arithmetic | The quantity of the meter at the same monthly rate as the cost that it replaces. A meter costs a month at three rates ([run-rate](24-priced-findings.md#priced-at-what-it-costs-now-and-a-change-stated-in-the-quantity-of-a-meter)): the last 7 days, the mean since it began, a typical day. A quantity at the same rate is that cost over what the bill paid for a unit (`paid`: the cost of the window over its quantity), so what remains after the switch is one share for every rate. Like for like, the alternative costs its list price over the list price of the current line (`retail`: the rate that the bill pays against list, whatever it is, is carried over); at list prices alone it costs its list price over `paid`. |
| A range | The two shares are the two ends of the range: the larger gives the low end of the saving, which is the one that counts, and a share of 1 or more at either end is no saving. A meter whose current line has no list price kept (no line, several, a price with steps) is not priced and the reason says that the list price of the current meter is not known: with one share (the list price over `paid`) a bill that pays above list would have its saving overstated, and an alternative at the price of the current line would show a saving. |
| No saving | An alternative that costs as much or more at either end (a share of 1 or more), a bill line with no cost per unit, a current meter whose list price is not known, and a price that is no price (a price of nothing too), make no figure, each with its own plain sentence. |
| The level | Scenario: the choice is the AI's, and carrying a rate over is an assumption. The figure is counted once like every other (a bill line once in a finding, the low end only, never above what the resource costs). |
| What is said | The basis is in plain sentences, on the page, in the card and in the letter: what the resource costs a month and at which rate (the last 7 days, say), for what (the name of the meter that the bill pays now), what the alternative would cost for the same use and what a switch would save, and that nobody checked that the resource can be switched to it, because a list price shows neither features nor limits ("At the rate of the last 7 days this resource costs about $864 a month for Standard Deployment. At list prices, a switch to Basic Deployment would cost about $252 a month for the same use, so it would save about $612 a month. Meternaut did not check whether this resource can be switched to it, or whether it has what this resource needs."). What would remain is worked out, not subtracted: each part keeps its monthly cost at its lowest rate times what remains at the dearer end (`afterUsd`, summed for a group only when every resource has it, and absent from a digest made before it). The sentence says what the switch would cost only when that and what is paid add up to the saving, and says the saving alone when they do not: a credit on the resource caps the saving at what the resource costs, and resources whose rates differ each have their own lowest rate. What it says is the difference of the two amounts that the sentence shows (864.60 paid and 612.30 saved show as $865 and $612, and $253 remains, not the $252 that 252.30 rounds to), so that the three add up as printed, and the saving is the figure that the page states above the sentence; when that difference is not a whole dollar from $100 up, or a number of cents below, the sentence says the saving alone. When the bill pays more or less than list, a sentence says so ("Your bill pays about 80 percent of the list price of Standard Deployment. Meternaut prices Basic Deployment both at its list price and at 80 percent of it.") and the range follows. When the list prices came from a copy of the price book that is at least a day old (a copy can be up to 44 days old when the list cannot be read), the sentence says how old the list is, once and in plain words, and the page, the card and the letter keep the one text ("Your bill pays about 80 percent of the list price of Standard Deployment as it was 10 days ago."; when the bill pays list prices: "At list prices as they were 10 days ago, a switch to Basic Deployment would cost ..."; for a group, the oldest of its lists: "as it was up to 10 days ago"). The toolbox keeps the age with the meter and with each price that the copy showed (`Toolbox.ages`), the figure says the older of the two lists that it rests on (the line of the meter and the alternative's price), and the digest keeps it as `listAge` (whole days, from 1: a read of the list and a copy of today say nothing, and neither does a digest made before it). The caution is on every basis that rests on an alternative, whatever the form of a group: the figure of a group whose rules differ says that the resources were not checked for the alternatives, and resources, or meters of one resource, that switch to the same price keep the rule when their bills pay them at slightly different rates (the basis then says that the bill pays another share of list for each). **The names of both ends** are kept in the digest (`now`: the meter name that the bill pays now; `to`: the meter name of the alternative's price line, or its SKU when the bill already has that meter name), each only when it is plain (letters, digits and single spaces, at most 40 characters and 6 words, and passes the check that the tools make of a word of a meter); the title says "from Standard Deployment to Basic Deployment" and the sentence of what the person is asked names them too. A switch whose alternative cannot be named in plain words is not priced (the reason `alternative`): a request that cannot name its target is no request. A digest made before the names says "another price of the same service", and a figure that is a mix of rules says nothing of the target, because no one price is it; a group keeps the name of the target only when it is the same for all, because one price has two names when the bills of its resources name their meters differently, and it is still one alternative. The first AI is told to say what the alternative must still do and how it knows; the second AI is shown the words of the alternative (through the check that the tools make of a word of a meter and a tighter one of its own: letters, digits and single spaces, at most 40 characters and 6 words, else `other`, so that a long phrase in Title Case cannot reach it), its list price, the rate and the shares that remain, and a doubt keeps the figure and the finding needs a person. A review of the real Storage list found that about 23 percent of its product words fail the tighter check (names over 40 characters, and names with a hyphen such as `RA-GRS`) and are shown to the second AI as `other`: that is accepted, for it still sees the price, the rate and the shares, and the words that it does see are short; the same words make no request, because a switch that cannot be named is not asked. |

An invented example: 600 hours cost 60 dollars (0.10 an hour). The line of the meter is listed at 0.125, so the bill pays 0.8 of list. The alternative is listed at 0.0625. Like for like it costs 0.0625 / 0.125 = 0.5 of what the meter costs, so 30 are saved; at list prices alone it costs 0.0625 / 0.10 = 0.625 of it, so 22.50 are saved. The figure is 22.50 to 30.00 a month, and 22.50 counts. A bill that pays above list: the bill pays 0.10 and the line of the meter is listed at 0.08, so it pays 1.25 of list. An alternative at 0.06 costs 0.06 / 0.08 = 0.75 of the meter like for like (15 saved) and 0.06 / 0.10 = 0.6 of it at list prices alone (24 saved): 15 counts, where one share of 0.6 would have counted 24. An alternative at 0.08, the price of the current line, costs a share of 1 like for like and is refused as dearer, where one share of 0.8 would have counted 12. For a meter that rose in the last week (23 days of 1 dollar and 7 of 2, for 370 hours), the same two shares (0.5 and 0.625) apply to each rate: 30, 37 and 60 a month at a typical day, the mean and the last 7 days. The figure is 11.25 to 30.00, and not what a trailing quantity of 30 days would give (370 × 0.0625 × 0.8 = 18.50 off 37).

## Which client, which call

The Watch's own client reads it, with the Watch's list of calls, which gets one more exact entry: `GET https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices` (`http.PRICE_CALL`). It is the entry that the Study's list already held ([proposal 15](15-learning.md)), defined once and used by both lists. The list sees an address without its query, so it pins the method, the host and the path, and nothing else of the host: no write, no other path, no other port. The query is made by `cases/prices.py` from checked words only, and a test pins it. The call carries no token (a test checks the headers), so no role is needed: the call is on no line of [`environment/access-requirements.json`](../../environment/access-requirements.json), the test of that file names it as a call that needs none, and the drift check probes that the list lets the GET through and refuses a POST and another path. The list writes the address of the next page with the port 443; the app follows it only on the same host and path, asks again without the port, and refuses any other.

## What a real read showed (2026-11-17, read only, counts and numbers only)

The real tool ran, with its real client and the real list, over the words of 23 meters that the real bill showed (the dearest meters of four services of the top spend: 5, 2, 6 and 10 meters). The bill was read with a person's own read-only sign-in, and no name or id of it is kept.

- **Every meter found its line:** `match` was `found` for 23 of 23, in the region of the resource or, for some, as a `Global` line.
- **The rate ratio:** 1 for 17 meters, within 0.01 of 1 for 2, above 1 for 3 (1.087, 1.1025, 1.2485: why is not examined) and not worked out for 1, a price with steps. The estate mostly pays list prices.
- **Alternatives of the same unit exist:** the product of the meter listed from 1 to 52 lines in its unit (a median of 23), and for 22 of the 23 meters there was at least one other line; the whole top service had 24 lines in 6 products (17 distinct prices).
- **Paging:** the read of a product was one page, a service of 1,672 prices came in two pages (about 1 MB, 0.9 seconds), and one of more than 5,000 lines stopped at the cap of five pages (3 MB, 5.7 seconds), said `complete: false` and still found the line of the meter.
- **A fault that only the real list could show:** it writes the address of the next page with `:443`, which the first version refused; fakes now write it too.
- **The list throttles:** HTTP 429 came after a burst of reads from one machine, and none came when the reads were 5 to 6 seconds apart. The page does not say what the limit is.

Facts about the list are from [the Azure Retail Prices REST API overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/rest/api/cost-management/retail-prices/azure-retail-prices), read on 2026-11-17 (the page is dated 2026-01-06): no sign-in is needed; the filter fields include `serviceName`, `productName`, `armRegionName` and `priceType`, and their values are case sensitive; at most 1,000 lines come back for a call, with the link to the next page; the types of price are `Consumption`, `Reservation` and `DevTestConsumption`; the list holds prices in US dollars, and this version holds the Commercial cloud only. The version `2023-01-01-preview` that the app asks for is the one the page names.

## The price book, the wait for the list and the warm-up (2026-11-21)

**What was measured.** In the first real run on the real estate (the Hub's `cases-run`, 2026-11-21) the tool read the list 9 times and 8 reads failed: `429 Too many requests at this point. Please retry after some time.` and a timeout. The failures came in pairs inside one second, because the seed of a beat asks for the product and, when that fails, at once for the whole service, and nothing was waited for: the client does not retry a 429 whose wait is longer than the 5 seconds that the read allowed. The same address with the same small filter answered 200 six times in a row from a developer machine, in 0.15 to 1.4 seconds, so the list is fine and the limit is on the path of the app (a shared address of the landing zone is the likely cause: not checked). Two more answers, read from a developer machine the same day, carry two rate-limit headers on a 200 (the first is read by nothing: only the second, which `http.retry_after` reads like any header that ends in `-retry-after`, and only for a 429): `x-ms-ratelimit-remaining-retailprices-requests` and `x-ms-ratelimit-retailprices-retry-after`; the second answer said 9 requests left and 60 seconds. So the list limits the requests of one address in a window of 60 seconds, and what that one reading showed is about ten requests in it. The limit is not documented, and nobody tried to reach it.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| The wait | A 429 that says how long to wait is waited for once, when that is at most 20 seconds (`prices.WAIT`: the 24 seconds of a read and one such wait are 44, which fit in the 45 of a call) and the call has the time: the wait is not a part of the 24 seconds, and a call that is given less than 45 has less to wait. A read waits once for its address in a run, never twice. A wait that is not taken (the 60 seconds that the list asked for are not) ends the read with the words of what the list asked ("the price list asked to wait 60 seconds (429)", with the code 429 when the body has no code word of its own) and never an address or a header, and holds the list for that time in the run (`prices.Hold`, kept by the toolbox: no state outside the run and no thread): no call of the run, for any address, reaches the list before the time has passed, and a call after it asks again. The tries of a read are the read's own and not the client's, so that each one can be a line of the History. |
| The book | One table, `mnpricebook`, with a row for each service, region and product (or the whole service). The row is the hash of the three words, which a key cannot hold, and the record holds the words, the day of the read, whether it was complete, the number of lines and the lines. Only a complete read with some lines is written. The lines are compact JSON, deflated and written in base64 (`prices.pack`): a real product of 24 lines was 2,575 characters of JSON, 371 bytes deflated and 496 characters in base64. A property of a table holds 64 KB (about 32,000 characters) and an entity 1 MB, and the store keeps a record in one property of 30,000 characters at most, so about a thousand lines fit and a larger list is not kept (a note says so). A copy is fresh for 10 days and usable as a stale copy for 45 (`FRESH_DAYS` and `STALE_DAYS`: the investigation runs every 7 days, `INVESTIGATE_EVERY_DAYS`, so a copy that was fresh for 3 days was always stale at the next run and every run would have paid for the warm-up; a list price changes rarely, and the limit of the list is for the reads that cannot be spared). A copy that says it was written more than a day after the clock of the run is no copy (it would count as fresh for ever); a day between two clocks is allowed. A stale price is a list price that may have moved, never what the bill paid, which code takes from the bill as before (`rate_ratio`). Reading the book never raises into the toolbox: a broken record (not base64, not deflate, not JSON, a row that is no line, a price that is none, another number of lines, other words than the ones asked) is no copy. Only the Table store has a book: the memory store of the tests has none, so that no test of the investigation runs the warm-up. |
| The order of the tool | The run's own memory, then a fresh copy of the book, then the list; when the list fails or asks for more than a call can wait, or a failure cuts a read short after some pages, an old copy of the whole list; then the failure as before. A read that a cap ended is shown as it was read, as it always was. A list that asked to wait is asked again when that time has passed, and is not kept as a failure for the run. The answer says `source` (`list` or `book`) and, for a copy, `age_days`; the description of the tool says that a copy older than 10 days is stale and is shown only because the list could not be read. A complete read is written to the book, whether the Investigator's call or a read that code made for a beat made it. A copy that is at least a day old leaves its age with the handles that it showed (`Toolbox.ages`), for the sentence of the figure (above). No text of a model is stored: the book holds prices of the platform. |
| The warm-up | Before the first beat, code reads the list for the products of the 12 dearest meters of the last 30 days that the book does not hold fresh (`Toolbox.warm_prices`). The cost lines that the run holds (the Watch read them, and the same client keeps them) hold a resource and a service for each line, and no meter, so the products come from one query of Cost Management for each subscription that has some of the 24 dearest resources (by resource, meter category and meter subcategory, the 30 closed days, a filter on their ids) and one read of Resource Graph for their regions, which the tools need anyway. A meter with no product would ask for a whole service, which is up to five requests, so it is left out. The reads are one at a time, and no two requests that reach the list are less than 6 seconds apart (the list showed ten requests in 60 seconds): not two reads, and not a second try of one read, whether the list asked for a wait or not. A wait of up to 60 seconds that the list asks for is taken (the warm-up has the time that a call has not), and all of it, the reads of Azure included, ends after the least of 180 seconds and an eighth of the time of the investigation (the share that a call of a tool gets for reading the subscriptions, too). **It ends at once when the list says no:** with no answer at all, or with a 429 that the wait did not change (the list was asked again after the wait that it asked for and said it again), or that asked for more than the warm-up takes, or that gave no time and said it again after the pause. Before, a list that always said 429 and asked for 60 seconds cost 121 seconds and four requests and gave nothing, and one that said 429 and gave no time took 24 requests in 91 seconds, which the list's own limit does not allow; the questions of the run read the list as they need, with the wait of a call. When the list answers at once it takes about 70 seconds; one wait of 60 seconds makes it about 130. It makes no model call and no call of a tool of the run, never fails the run, writes a note for each read that failed and one line in the notes (how many products were read from the list, how many the book held fresh, how many were not read) and does nothing when the store has no book. |
| The History | One line `prices.read` for each try that reaches the list: `service` and `region` as the platform names them, `product` (not for a whole service), `status` (0 when no answer came), `ms` and, for a 429 that said how long to wait, `retryAfter`. It holds no address, id or name of the estate. The pass rate of the list is the share of the tries with status 200: `traces \| where message startswith 'meternaut_event ' \| extend e = parse_json(substring(message, 16)) \| where tostring(e.type) == 'prices.read' \| summarize Tries = count(), Answered = countif(toint(e.status) == 200), Asked = countif(toint(e.status) == 429), NoAnswer = countif(toint(e.status) == 0) by Day = bin(timestamp, 1d)` |

**What is not proved.**

- **That the book fills in the real network.** Nothing was run against the real list from the app's network, and the new line `prices.read` is the way to see what happens there. The cause of the limit is not known: a shared address of the landing zone is a guess. The limit itself (about ten requests in 60 seconds) rests on the headers of one reading; it was not tried, and the pace of the warm-up (6 seconds) and its longest wait (60) come from it.
- **That a call of the Investigator gets prices when the list limits it.** A call waits at most 20 seconds and the list asked for 60, so a product that the warm-up did not read, and that the book does not hold, still ends in `unavailable` while the limit holds, and a stale copy answers only when the book has one. What the warm-up and the book give is the dearest products of the estate, not every product.
- **That the query of the warm-up gives what the real Cost Management gives.** Its shape (three groupings, no days, a filter of the ids as Resource Graph spells them) was held against a fake only, and no call was made to Azure. A meter with no subcategory is not warmed, and how many of the real meters have none is not measured.
- **That the dearest 12 meters are the products that the questions ask about.** A beat looks first at what the runs of the last days did not look at, so its dearest meter may be another one. The book still fills from every read that the tool makes.
- **That 10 and 45 days are the right ages.** They are starting values written down before the measurement (10 because the investigation runs every 7 days); how often the real list changes was not measured, so a list price that moved in the last 10 days can still be shown as fresh, and the sentence of the figure says how old the copy is from the first day.
- **That a list larger than about a thousand lines is kept.** It is not: the record holds one property. A whole service of a few thousand lines stays a read of the list every time, and a read of five pages is five requests of the ten.
- **That the Investigator reads `source` and `age_days` well.** No model was called for this change. Rows of the table older than 45 days are not deleted: a read of the same words writes over them.
- **What the warm-up costs a run.** Up to 180 seconds of the time of an investigation (or an eighth of it, when that is less), before the first beat, and a few requests of Cost Management and Resource Graph; a list that says no ends it at the first product. How much of it the first real run takes is not known.

No new resource, role or call that needs an identity: the table is made by the first write, with the right that the app has on its storage account for its other tables, and a read of a product is a few hundred characters.

## A list that serves every meter that shares it, and a charge that the other price does not have (2026-11-21)

**What was measured.** The replay of the candidate instructions `others-research` on the invented row `bastion-four-units-full-story` made the Investigator state a real other way for the invented host. The host is billed for a base charge (720 hours at 0.29, 208.80 a month) and for additional units (1,440 hours at 0.14, 201.60 a month): switch the base charge to the cheaper price of 0.19 an hour, and the additional units, for which that price has no line, go away. As the answer stated it, with invented data: `{"action": "switch", "effects": [{"handle": "r1", "meters": ["m1"], "remains": {"form": "alternative", "price": "p2"}}, {"handle": "r1", "meters": ["m2"], "remains": {"form": "none"}}]}`. Code could not price it, and the replay said that Meternaut did not find the alternative that the AI named. Two causes, both reproduced with the app's own code on the invented row. The tool had registered the price handles and the list price of the line only for the meter that was asked; the Investigator asked for the additional units and used a price of that table for the base charge, so the base charge had no price that "was shown for it". And `none` was refused in a switch, so the charge that the new price does not have was left out and the figure would have been 72.00, the base charge alone, and not the 273.60 that both charges make: an under-statement that reads like the saving of the way.

| Part | What code does |
|---|---|
| A list counts for the meters that share it | The table that the tool shows is the table of one product in one unit, so it is the table of every meter of the same resource that has the same service, product and unit (the first, second and sixth word of the bill's meter). After the tool registers `Toolbox.offers`, `retail` and `ages` for the meter that was asked, it registers them for each of those other meters too: the handles that the answer showed (its page and its current line), the line that the words of that meter name (its price handle is made when no page showed it) with its list price, and the age of the copy that showed it. A line that has steps, or several lines that disagree on the price, take the list price of that meter away, as they do for the meter that was asked; a line that this read did not find leaves what the meter had. A meter of another resource, unit, product or service is never touched. The answer of the tool, its schema and its description are the same, and so is `pricing`: the form `alternative` still takes a price only from the handles that were shown for that meter, and a meter has them now when it shares the list. Only the two hashes of the lock that cover the code of the tool moved |
| `none` in a switch | In a change whose action is `switch`, an effect that states the form `none` says that the new price has no counterpart for those charges. Nothing in the bill checks that, so code reads it as an assumed share with nothing remaining: the form `assumed`, a share of 0 to 0 that remains, a Scenario and never Expected, with the words of an assumed share ("The AI assumes that 100 percent of that would go away. That is the AI's own assumption, and Meternaut did not check it."), and a way that carries it is an estimate. Under `reduce`, `schedule` and `commit` it is still no form, and `remove` is as it was. A switch that puts one charge on a price of the list and says `none` for another has the form `mixed` (72.00 and 201.60: 273.60 a month of the invented host's 410.40), and a switch that says `none` for every charge is an assumed share of 100 percent (410.40). That is also how a switch to a tier that costs nothing, for which the list has no line, can be priced: as what the AI says and nothing else |
| The words of one resource | The figure of one resource that rests on a switch and on other charges (the form `mixed` with a switched line) says what each part rests on, and no longer "the resources" and "the alternatives" for what is one resource. For the invented host: "At the rate of the last 7 days this resource costs about $410 a month for what the change would shrink. One of its 2 charges would be switched to another price. Meternaut took that price from the price list and compared it with the bill like for like. For the other charge, the AI says that it would go away in full, and Meternaut did not check that. **What is not proved.**

- **That the Investigator states `none` for a charge that the new price does not have, in a real run.** One replay on an invented estate showed it; no replay was run with this code.
- **That the charge goes away.** `none` is the AI's word. Nothing in the bill or in the list says that the other price has no counterpart for the charge, and the sentence says so. The second AI and a person judge it.
- **That the words of the bill are the words of the list for every meter that shares a list.** The words decide, as they do for the meter that was asked. A meter whose line the list does not find gets no list price and no switch; a real bill with a meter renamed inside the window was not looked at.
- **That a meter that no tool had shown when the ask was made is registered.** It is not: the toolbox registers the meters that it holds a handle for at that moment, and `cost_meters` shows every meter of a resource that fits in 40 rows. The replay reads its registers at the end of the trace and may register one more.
- **The numbering of the handles in the replay.** It is the real tool's when the first ask is for a meter in the unit of the first meter, as before.
- **That a person reads the new words as intended.** They were not seen on a page, a card or a letter in Teams or in Outlook.

## Access at each level (rule 12)

The list is public, so the tool needs no access of its own at any level. What it needs is a meter handle, which comes from the bill that the Watch reads with the Cost Management Reader role (R2): a Watch that cannot read the bill has no meter and the tool answers `unknown-handle`. A bill in another currency has no meter handle either, because `cost_meters` shows dollars only. An agreement with negotiated or reserved rates is what `rate_ratio` is for: it is worked out from the cost lines that the Watch can read, and a person who sees the figure sees that it rests on the rate that the bill shows.

## What is not proved

- **That the app's network reaches the host.** The Study reads the same host with its own client and the network requirements name it ([`tools/cases-check.py`](../../tools/cases-check.py)); whether the function app's subnet reaches it is for the platform team, and nothing here ran from the app. A failed read is `unavailable` and the investigation goes on. A host that gives no answer is tried once in a run (8 seconds at most), so a failure of the network at that moment ends the tool for the rest of that run, and the next run tries again.
- **That the words of the bill match the list for every service.** They did for 23 of 23 meters of four services. A meter whose words differ has no line and no rate. Marketplace products, other agreements' meter words and a meter renamed inside the window were not looked at.
- **Why three rates are above 1.** A resource in another region than its row, a price that changed inside the window, or usage that the list prices in steps could each do it. The ratio is one number for the whole window.
- **That an alternative does the same work.** The tool shows prices and nothing else, and code does not check features or limits for the form either: the figure says so in its words ("Meternaut did not check whether this resource can be switched to it, or whether it has what this resource needs."). They are judged by the Investigator from its measurements and from what the Study read, by the second AI and by a person.
- **That the names of the two ends read well for every service** (added 2026-11-19). The title and the sentence of what is asked use the meter name that the bill pays now and the meter name, or else the SKU, of the alternative's line. They read well where those words tell the two apart (Standard Deployment and Basic Deployment of a firewall do). A service whose meter names are the same at both ends and whose SKUs differ is named by the SKU; one whose names are long or have a hyphen or an underscore is not named, and its switch is not priced. How often that is so in a real estate is not measured, and the real rendering in Teams and in Outlook was not seen.
- **That the rate of the bill carries over to another SKU.** A reservation, or a rate that was negotiated for one size, may not. That is why the low end of a switch is the larger of two shares, one like for like and one at list prices alone; the real rates of three meters were above 1, and for them the share like for like is the larger one.
- **That the list price of the current line is the one that the bill's meter belongs to.** It is the line of the same product, meter and unit in the region of the resource (the SKUs of one meter share a price). A line that the tool does not find, or finds with several prices or with steps, leaves the meter without a list price, and no switch is priced for it.
- **That a clock bounds a read.** The 8 seconds of a try are the timeout of one socket operation; the clock of the read is checked between pages. A host that sends a character at a time could hold one page for longer than the 24 seconds of the read; the length of a page is what bounds it.
- **That the share of a month is one share for every rate.** It holds when the meter bills one unit price all through its window; a price that changed inside the window, or units that are priced in steps, break it (the tool shows the ratio as one number for the window).
- **That the Investigator states a switch well.** No model was called for the form either: whether it names the right meter, a price that fits, and says what the alternative must still do, is for the first real run.
- **That the Investigator uses the tool well.** No model was called for this change.
- **The limit of the list.** It is not documented. Its headers showed ten requests in a window of 60 seconds ([below](#the-price-book-the-wait-for-the-list-and-the-warm-up-2026-11-21)); a read waits once for a wait of up to 20 seconds, and a wait that it cannot take ends in `unavailable`, or in an old copy of the book.
- **What it leaves out.** Reservation, dev/test and savings plan prices (a commitment is a change of its own: [proposal 17](17-reservations-instructions-only.md)), other currencies, and the Government clouds.
- **What the tests pin.** Mutation-checked one at a time: the global lines and the lines with no region, each cap, the length of a page, `complete` when lines were cut, the price given twice, a price with no first step, the match by region and by price, the host of the next page, that no token is sent, the currency and the kind of price, the length of a text, the check of the words of the address, the region `global`, the unit filter, the rate for a price with steps, the check of the words shown (also the product and the region that the list gives), the read once, the region that is not a word, the scope of a read, the register of list prices (kept when a longer window replaces the meter and when a later ask was cut before the line, taken away only by an ask that finds steps or lines that disagree), the bound on what a run holds, the code word of a failure, the meter of a whole type, a bill that gave money back or cost nothing, the order and the handle of a price with steps, a price of nothing (not in the table, no handle, not offered, a price with a free first step too), the service and the region of each line that the list gives, the length that the transport is told to read and a page of exactly the cap and one more, the prices that stay when a later page fails, a host that gives no answer (once in a run, with a fake clock), the time of a try and of a read, and a second try only when the time holds it. For the form: the price handle that was never shown, shown for another meter, of another service, in another unit and with steps; a negative, no-number, zero and zero-quantity price or bill line; the share at one and above one; the list price of the current line (none, nothing, a negative or no number: the switch is not priced), a bill that pays above list and a switch to the price of the current line; the same share at each rate of a meter that rose; the digest, the sentence and what the Skeptic is shown, with the words of an alternative (the phrase in Title Case of 44 characters, one of 41, one of 40, seven words, marks); a group with one rule and a group with two; the same rule for resources and for meters of one resource whose bills pay slightly different rates (three rates), and the sentence on the basis of every group that rests on an alternative, whatever its form; and the registers of the real tool (`Toolbox.offers`, `retail`, `prices`, and `ages` for a copy of at least a day) as what the arithmetic reads, also after a longer window replaced the meter. The pins of the wait, the book, the warm-up and the age of the list (2026-11-21), 87 tests of which each rule was removed in memory and caught, are in [the changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md).
