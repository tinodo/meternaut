## The owner's words

"Teams bot? What … teams bot? I didn't ask for a bot. I asked for team cards … I do NOT want any app registrations that need specific permissions." And: "use the same mechanism as tinodo/secure-request-classifier", which is the owner's own repository ([secure-request-classifier](https://github.com/tinodo/secure-request-classifier), MIT licence): a Power Platform flow that reaches private Azure resources over virtual network support, with no public endpoint and no stored credential.

## What this is

A person who chose Teams gets the card in a Teams chat. The cases app writes the card as one message on a private Storage queue. A Power Automate flow, which runs in a subnet of the project's own virtual network, reads the message and posts the card with Microsoft's own Teams connector.

What is not needed any more: an Azure Bot resource, a public endpoint, an app registration, a Graph permission, a Teams app package, a secret and a key. [Proposal 13](13-teams-and-outlook.md) first described a bot. Since 2026-11-11 it says what the card shows, what the message is and what the app knows; this page is the Azure and Power Platform side, and the last section says what became of the bot.

How the flow reaches a queue that has no public endpoint: Power Platform has *virtual network support*. A Power Platform environment is linked to an *enterprise policy* of kind NetworkInjection, and the policy names a subnet that is delegated to `Microsoft.PowerPlatform/enterprisePolicies`. From then on the connectors that support it (the Azure Queues connector is one, generally available) run their calls from containers in that subnet, so the storage account is reached over its private endpoint, as the function apps reach it ([overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/vnet-support-overview), [set up](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/vnet-support-setup-configure)). Nothing of the landing zone is touched: the template makes a subnet and a policy in the project's own group and network, and no private DNS zone, link, peering or route ([AGENTS.md rule 19](../../AGENTS.md)).

## The message

One JSON text on the queue, not base64:

```json
{"v":1,"id":"<delivery id>","to":"<mail address>","title":"<short title>","card":{ "type": "AdaptiveCard", "...": "..." },"request":"<plan id>"}
```

`v` is the version, always 1; `id` is the delivery id, the id of the record that the app keeps of the try, so that a run of the flow and the record can be put side by side; `to` is the person's mail address, taken from the list of people and never from a text of the estate; `title` is a short plain title (the fixed words of a notification), used in the run history only; `card` is the Adaptive Card as JSON, schema 1.4; `request` is on the card of a request that waits, and is the id of its plan. The cases app takes the queue's name from its setting `TEAMS_CARDS_QUEUE`, which [the cases template](../../environment/cases/main.bicep) sets to `teams-cards` (the queue is made there, next to `cases-control`). The whole message is at most 30,000 bytes, and the card in it at most 20,000: a card that does not fit is replaced by a minimal one with the headline and the link, and the JSON is never cut ([proposal 13](13-teams-and-outlook.md)).

Two more messages are part of the closing of a card (see "The card closes itself"). An *update* is a message of the same queue with `"op":"update"` and `"message":"<id of the Teams message>"`, and a `card` that has no button; the flow replaces the message that the id names with it. The message that the flow writes after a post goes to the queue `teams-posted`: `{"v":1,"request":"<plan id>","to":"<mail address>","message":"<id of the Teams message>","conversation":"<id of the conversation>"}`, written as base64 text, because the Functions host that reads the queue decodes the text of every message as base64 unless `messageEncoding` in host.json says otherwise ([QueueTriggerAttribute](https://learn.microsoft.com/dotnet/api/microsoft.azure.webjobs.queuetriggerattribute), read on 2026-11-12), and the Azure Queues connector writes the text as it is given. `message` and `conversation` are the `id` and the `conversationId` that the post action returns ([Teams connector](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/teams/), `PostToConversationResponse`).

Three rules follow from what Microsoft says, and the card has to keep them:

- **Buttons open links and do nothing else.** A card that is posted by this action cannot collect an answer: any button other than `Action.OpenUrl` shows an error in Teams, and `Action.Submit` too, unless the card is posted by one of the "wait for a response" actions, which this flow does not use ([Power Automate: adaptive cards for Teams, known issues](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-automate/overview-adaptive-cards#known-issues)). So an answer is a link to a page of the app, which sends it (see "The card carries the decision"), and nothing is answered in Teams. The one other action is `Action.ToggleVisibility`, which the client carries out and which sends nothing.
- **No templating.** Power Automate does not support Adaptive Cards that use the templating feature ([Copilot Studio: send a proactive adaptive card](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-copilot-studio/advanced-proactive-message#send-a-proactive-adaptive-card)), so the app sends a finished card.
- **The card has to stay under about 28 KB.** An action that posts a message is limited to about 28 KB, all markup counted; a larger one fails with "Request Entity too large" ([Teams connector, known issues and limitations](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/teams/)). The queue allows 64 KB; the app keeps a card under 20,000 bytes and the whole message under 30,000, so Teams' limit is not expected to be reached. If Teams answers 413 all the same, the flow deletes the message (see the next section).

The app gives each message a time to live of 24 hours (`messagettl=86400`), because a card that nobody has read in a day is of no use. A queue keeps a message for seven days unless the writer says otherwise ([Put Message](https://learn.microsoft.com/rest/api/storageservices/put-message)).

## The card carries the decision

The first cards of 2026-11-11 had one link, "Review and decide", and took no answer: a person had to leave Teams to answer. The owner's word on 2026-11-12: the card must carry all the information and the decision. So the card shows what a person needs to decide, in the words and with the figures of the page (the headline with names, what changes, the cost figures, the risk, how sure, how to undo it, and who does it, said once), and then the buttons. Nothing here gives Meternaut a right: every change in Azure is made by a natural person, with that person's own rights, after that person approved that exact change ([AGENTS.md rule 29](../../AGENTS.md)). No bot, no app registration and no role of its own were added.

**An answer is a link, and a link does nothing on its own.** Each answer button is an `Action.OpenUrl` to an address of the function app: `/ok/<request>/approve`, `/confirm` or `/decline`, with the plan code and a link token, a one-time token that the app made for that person when it made the card and that holds until the request expires (`cases/cardlinks.py`). What follows:

1. Opening the address (a GET, which a link preview or a prefetch may also do) changes nothing and writes nothing: the page only looks at the token.
2. The sign-in of the function app (Easy Auth) signs the person in with Microsoft Entra ID first, silently when a session exists. The query of the address survives that redirect.
3. The page is small. It sends the form of the answer by itself with one fixed script, `var f=document.getElementById("send");if(f){f.submit();}`. The Content-Security-Policy of that page allows exactly that text by its hash (`script-src 'sha256-...'`) and is otherwise the policy of every page, so no other script, inline or from a file, runs there. The same page shows a button with the same word, for a browser where scripts are off.
4. The form is the form of the request page, so everything after it is unchanged: the token is used up, and the plan code, the plan digest, the switches, the right of the person, the second person for a large change and the look at the resource that the page makes before it offers Approve are all checked. The approval record names the person. Azure's own sign-in for the change is silent when a session exists, and the change runs right after with that person's own token. There is no second page that asks the person to decide again.

"Decline" is the same link and page and records the answer at once. A request that only asks for advice (nothing changes in Azure) has "Worth acting on" and "Not worth it". A link works once: a link that was used, or that ran out of time, or whose request changed, shows a page that says so and leads to the page of the request.

**A change that cannot be undone asks twice, on the card.** "Approve" is an `Action.ToggleVisibility` that shows a hidden part, "This cannot be undone: <what is lost, where>", with "No, go back" first and "Yes, delete it", which is the link. For a person who comes from a card this replaces the page that asks again; that page stays for a person who comes from a letter or from the list. `Action.ToggleVisibility` is from schema 1.2 ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/adaptive-cards/schema-explorer/action-toggle-visibility)) and Learn lists it among the actions that Teams supports ([Teams cards reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/microsoftteams/platform/task-modules-and-cards/cards/cards-reference)). Learn says too that Teams does not draw the styles `positive` and `destructive` of an action, so the order and the words, not a colour, tell the safe answer from the other.

**Who sees which button.** For a change that needs two people the first person's button reads "Confirm" and records a confirmation, and the second reads "Approve". A request that a switch holds, that the look at the resource refuses, or that the person may only read, has a card with the link to the page only ("Review and decide" or "See the request"), and so has a card for which no token could be made. The page, the letter and the card keep the same words.

**A tap sends nothing back:** the app learns an answer from the page that the link opened. What the card does when the request is decided is in the next section.

## The card closes itself

A card that keeps live-looking buttons in a chat after its request is decided is a trap, and it leaves the person asking whether the answer counted. So the card closes, and the app still holds no right in Azure or Teams: it writes messages to queues, as before.

1. After a post, the flow writes the request, the Teams message and the conversation to the queue `teams-posted` (step 4 of the data path). The app reads that queue with the trigger of its function `cards-posted`, through the host storage connection, as it reads `cases-control`. It keeps the pair for the person that the message names (a record of the kind `card-posted` in the store of its messages, which no count of letters sees). It refuses a message that is not exactly what the flow writes, or that names no approver of a request that exists; a store that cannot be written makes the host try again, five times, and then move the message to `teams-posted-poison`.
2. Every minute, with the other cards, the app looks at the requests that it has a posted card for. When one has ended (answered, by this person or by another one, on the page, from a card or from the letter; declined; expired; withdrawn; or an approved change that was made or was not made) it puts an `update` message on `teams-cards` for each posted message that no closing has replaced yet, with a small card: the headline of the request, one line, and no button. The line is worded from what became of the request, as the page words it (the same brief, with the action of the request): "Decided: <answer> by <name>, <time>. Nothing more to do." for an answer, a decline, a withdrawal or a change that was made ("Approved and done"); "Approved by <name>, not made: <why>" for a change that Azure refused or that failed; "Expired. Nothing more to do." A request has not ended while it is open, while its approval waits for the extra sign-in check, or while its change is being made or is not confirmed yet: its card stays until it has. The try is written as a record of the kind `card-closed`; a try that failed is made again after 15 minutes, 12 times at most, and a card that was closed is not closed again. A card that was closed does not count as sent for a request that is open again: that request gets a new card, which closes in its turn. A closing tells nobody anything new: no switch, pause or quiet hour holds it back, and no weekly budget counts it.
3. The flow replaces the card with the Teams action *Update an adaptive card in a chat or channel* (`UpdateCardInConversation`). On Learn its inputs are *Post as*, *Post in*, an optional parent message id (for a reply in a channel) and a dynamic body ([Teams connector](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/teams/), read on 2026-11-12). The flow gives the body the fields of the post action (`recipient`, `messageBody`) and the id of the message in `messageId`. The names inside the body are not documented, so the first run proves them; `python tools/powerplatform.py test-close` does that without a request. An update replaces the whole card. The flow deletes an update message that Teams answers 400, 404 or 413 for, as for a post, and keeps the others.

What it does not do. A card whose request still waits for a second person keeps its buttons (the first person's "Decline" still works). A card also stays while an approval waits for the extra sign-in check (ten minutes at most), and while Azure is making the change or has not shown it yet: its buttons then lead to a page that says that nothing more can be recorded. A card that was posted before this version is never closed, because the app does not know which message it is, and neither is one whose posted message was not written (the run of the flow is marked failed with the code `PostedMessageNotWritten`). Whether Teams replaced a card, the app cannot tell: its record of a closing says that the queue took the message (see "The limits").

## What the flow does with a message

The definition is [one JSON file](../../powerplatform/solution/src/Workflows/MeternautCards-E6272F40-4E93-4EF9-B20B-2FCD222057AF.json) in an unpacked solution. The trigger looks once a minute, which is the least that Power Automate allows, takes up to 32 messages at a time and hides each of them for 15 minutes. One run handles one message, one run at a time.

| What happens | What the flow does with the message |
|---|---|
| A card is posted | Writes the message of step 4 to `teams-posted`, then deletes the message. If the write fails, the message is deleted all the same, because the card must not be posted twice, and the run ends as failed (`PostedMessageNotWritten`). |
| A card is replaced (`op` is `update`) | Updates the card, then deletes the message. |
| The message is not a message of version 1, or its `op` is not one that the flow knows | Deletes it and ends the run as failed. The text stays in the run history. |
| Teams answers 400 (the card is wrong), 404 (the person or the message is not found) or 413 (the card is too large), for a post or for an update | Deletes it and ends the run as failed: these do not get better with time. |
| Anything else: a timeout, 401 or 403 (the Workflows app is not allowed, or a connection needs a new sign-in), 429 (too many cards), 5xx | Leaves it. It is visible again after the 15 minutes and is tried again, until it expires. The run is marked failed. |

Delivery is at least once. If a post succeeded and the delete did not arrive, the message comes back after 15 minutes and the card is posted a second time, and the app gets a second posted message and closes both cards. That is rare and is accepted.

**What the app learns of this.** For the card of a request, it learns which Teams message the card is, from the message of step 4, and nothing else: not that the person saw it, and not a failed run. The queue `teams-cards` is one way. There is no dead-letter queue for it: Storage queues have none (the Functions host makes a `-poison` queue for the queues that it reads itself, and the flow reads this one). A card that was refused for good, and a card that kept failing until its message expired, show up in one place only, the failed runs of the flow, which Power Automate keeps for 28 days by default ([run history](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-automate/dataverse/cloud-flow-run-metadata)). A card that has no posted message is a card that the app cannot close, and the letter still follows when no card was sent. Whatever the app does when no card arrives cannot depend on the card having been posted.

## The room in the network

The project's /24 holds five subnets: the private endpoints (a /27), the cases app (a /27), the Operator (a /27), the web page (a /27) and the AI Gateway (a /26). One /26 is free. [The parameter builder](../../tools/environment-params.py) (`--powerplatform`) takes the last free /27 of the network for `snet-powerplatform`, so one /27 stays free. If the network has no free /27, the builder makes no file and says why in its output (`powerplatform_reason`); it never invents a range.

| Size | Fits |
|---|---|
| /28 (11 usable addresses) | The least. A Sandbox or Developer environment needs 6 to 10 addresses, and Azure keeps five in every subnet. |
| /27 (27 usable) | The default here: a Developer or Sandbox environment with room, and one /27 left in the network. |
| /26 (59 usable) | A Production environment, which needs 25 to 30 addresses ([subnet size](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/vnet-support-overview#estimating-subnet-size-for-power-platform-environments)). It takes the whole free block. |

**This is the owner's decision, and it has to be made before the first deploy.** The address range of a delegated subnet and the DNS servers of its network cannot be changed while an environment is linked: the environment has to be unlinked first, the change made, and the link made again; after a change of DNS servers, wait 30 minutes before linking ([FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/vnet-support-overview)). The size is the constant `POWERPLATFORM_SUBNET_LENGTH` in the parameter builder. Power Platform uses the network's own DNS servers, which here are the landing zone's, so the name of the storage account has to resolve to its private address from the subnet, as it does for the function apps.

## What a person does by hand, in order

The owner starts the Hub actions. The roles in Teams and in Power Platform may be held by the owner or by others, and each step says which role it needs. `python tools/powerplatform.py steps` prints the same steps in the same order, with the names filled in from its arguments, and sends nothing. The Hub action `powerplatform` ends by printing them with placeholders only, because the log of this repository is public and no identifier of the estate may be printed in it.

**Before anything runs**

1. Decide the size of the subnet (above). It cannot change once an environment is linked.

**In the Hub** (the Actions tab, the workflow Hub, which runs in the environment `hub`)

2. Start the action `cases` once more. It makes the queues `teams-cards` and `teams-posted` next to `cases-control` and gives the app its settings `TEAMS_CARDS_QUEUE` and `TEAMS_POSTED_QUEUE`; the app's new code, which writes the cards, keeps the answer of the flow and replaces a card when its request ends, goes out in the same run. Cards that are written before the flow is on wait in the queue for as long as their time to live lasts.
3. Start the action `powerplatform`. It stops before it changes anything, and says why, unless the resource provider `Microsoft.PowerPlatform` is registered, the virtual network is there (`model-side`) and the parameter builder found a free /27 for the subnet (`powerplatform_files`). The deploy identity cannot register a provider: a person does it once with `az provider register --namespace Microsoft.PowerPlatform` (it was registered in the pilot subscription when this was read on 2026-11-11). It then reads whether the subnet and the policy are in Azure, previews what the template would make, makes the subnet and the enterprise policy (each only when it is not there), waits for the landing zone's route table to appear on the subnet (20 minutes at most), as the AI Gateway's step does, signs in again and reads both back. A policy that is linked cannot be written again by Power Platform, so the template makes it only when it does not exist.

**In Azure, by a person.** The deploy identity gives roles to service principals only, never to a person.

4. Give the account that owns the flow's connections (a person, or a cloud-only service account if the owner chooses one) a data role on each of the two queues, and nothing wider. A role takes up to ten minutes to apply:

   ```
   az role assignment create --assignee-object-id <object id of the person> --assignee-principal-type User --role "Storage Queue Data Message Processor" --scope /subscriptions/<subscription id>/resourceGroups/<resource group>/providers/Microsoft.Storage/storageAccounts/<storage account>/queueServices/default/queues/teams-cards
   az role assignment create --assignee-object-id <object id of the person> --assignee-principal-type User --role "Storage Queue Data Message Sender" --scope /subscriptions/<subscription id>/resourceGroups/<resource group>/providers/Microsoft.Storage/storageAccounts/<storage account>/queueServices/default/queues/teams-posted
   ```

   The person's own object id is `az ad signed-in-user show --query id --output tsv`, run by that person; the account's name is `az storage account list --resource-group <resource group> --query "[].name" --output tsv`. The first role reads, peeks and deletes the messages of the cards; the second lets the flow write the message of step 4 of the data path, and the app reads that queue with the role that it holds on the whole account ([built-in roles](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/role-based-access-control/built-in-roles/storage)). Both are the right of a natural person on one queue each: no identity of the app gets a right to change anything. The connector wants a Microsoft Entra ID connection, and its page says that guest accounts are not supported; a B2B guest's connection worked on 2026-11-11 all the same.
5. If the Power Platform administrator who links the environment cannot read the policy, give that person Reader on it:

   ```
   az role assignment create --assignee-object-id <object id of the administrator> --assignee-principal-type User --role Reader --scope /subscriptions/<subscription id>/resourceGroups/<resource group>/providers/Microsoft.PowerPlatform/enterprisePolicies/ep-meternaut-netinjection
   ```

**In Teams, by a Teams administrator**

6. Check that the app *Workflows* (formerly Power Automate) is allowed in the [Teams admin center](https://admin.teams.microsoft.com/policies/manage-apps). The action that posts a card needs it ([Teams connector, known issues](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/teams/)).

**In Power Platform, by a Power Platform administrator with their own `az login`, and the tool.** Each command is a dry run until `--apply` is added. The tool never runs in a pipeline: its output names environments of the estate, and the deploy identity holds no right in Power Platform.

7. `python tools/powerplatform.py create-environment --name <environment name> --sku Developer --apply` (region `sweden`, Dataverse, euro, English; it waits until the environment is Ready; an environment of that name is left alone, so this step is skipped when the environment exists). From 15 November 2026 Learn allows only a managed developer environment ([Developer Plan](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/developer/plan)). The tool makes the environment first and turns Managed Environments on second, and whether the first call is still accepted after that date is not known: if it is refused, make the environment in the Power Platform admin center with Managed Environments on, and go on with step 9.
8. `python tools/powerplatform.py managed --environment <environment name> --apply`.
9. `python tools/powerplatform.py link --environment <environment name> --subscription <subscription id> --resource-group <resource group> --apply`. It checks first that the environment is in Sweden, is a type that supports virtual network support, is a Managed Environment and is not linked to another policy, and that the policy and its subnet look right. Then wait: connections can be unstable for up to 30 minutes after a link or an unlink.
10. `python tools/powerplatform.py status --environment <environment name> --subscription <subscription id> --resource-group <resource group>` must say that the project's policy is linked.

**When the maker portal refuses you** ("You are not permitted to make flows in this environment"). A person who is a guest (B2B) of the tenant that holds the subscription gets this in a new Dataverse environment, because every new environment refuses guests ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/security/guest-access); the setting `restrictGuestUserAccess` is changed in the Power Platform admin center, which does not let a guest sign in at all: "You attempted to login as a guest user, which is not supported"). The owner of this project is such a guest, even as Global Administrator. The platform's own API takes the same token, so use steps 15 to 19 instead of 11 to 14: the connections and the flow are made without the portal, and nothing in Dataverse is needed.

**In the maker portal, by the person of step 4**, who made the environment or has been made an administrator of it.

11. `python tools/powerplatform-pack.py` makes `powerplatform/out/meternaut-cards.zip`, the same bytes every time. In the environment: Solutions, Import solution, that file.
12. The import asks for the two connections (one for each connection reference of the solution) and for the three environment variables. Make each connection there, or from the actions of the flow in its designer, and not first under Data, Connections to pick afterwards: the owner's reference repository found that this can leave a second connection reference for the same connector. Azure Queues: sign in with *Microsoft Entra ID Integrated*, not an access key. Microsoft Teams: sign in. The variables: *Storage queue endpoint* is the whole address, `https://<storage account>.queue.core.windows.net/`, *Queue name* is `teams-cards` and *Posted queue name* is `teams-posted`. No address of the estate is in the repository.
13. Open the flow *Meternaut cards*, save it, and turn it on. It arrives switched off, and saving does not turn it on.
14. Send the first card before the app does: a second flow in the same environment with a manual trigger and one *Put a message on a queue (V2)* action with the same connection, endpoint and queue, and a message in the format above. It needs the role *Storage Queue Data Message Sender* on the queue for the time of the test. The card should arrive in the person's chat within a few minutes, and the queue should be empty. If the first runs after the link are slow and fail once with a 404 from the connector's host, wait out the 30 minutes and run again before looking for another cause (the reference repository's experience, not proven here).

**After a new version of the solution.** An import replaces the flow, and the replacement arrives switched off with no connection chosen: pick the two connections again (they are kept, no new sign-in), save, and turn it on, as in steps 12 and 13 ([the reference repository](https://github.com/tinodo/secure-request-classifier/blob/main/docs/deployment.md#what-a-redeployment-resets)). With the tool, `flow --apply` updates the flow that is there and keeps it started.

**Without the maker portal, by the person of step 4** (the way that was run on 2026-11-11; each command is a dry run until `--apply`):

15. `python tools/powerplatform.py connections --environment <environment name> --apply` makes the two connections that are missing and prints the address at which the person signs in for each. Open each address in a browser, choose your own account, tick *I have verified this request and trust the source* and click *Allow access*. The addresses are good for about 15 minutes. If your sign-in name has `#EXT#` in it (you are a guest) and the Azure Queues sign-in page has `/common/` in its web address, put the tenant id in its place before you choose the account: otherwise the connection is made with the token of your home tenant, and the queue, which is in this tenant, refuses it. Run `connections` again: both must say *connected*.
16. `python tools/powerplatform.py flow --environment <environment name> --storage-account <storage account> --apply` makes the flow *Meternaut cards* from the definition of the unpacked solution (the same one that the packer packs), with the three values and the two connections, and starts it. It is not a solution: it lives in the person's own flows.
17. `python tools/powerplatform.py test-card --environment <environment name> --storage-account <storage account> --to <your mail address> --apply` makes a flow with a button, runs it once, deletes it, and says where the card is. It needs the role *Storage Queue Data Message Sender* on the queue for the time of the test (take it away after); without it the queue answers `AuthorizationPermissionMismatch` and the tool says which role is missing. `python tools/powerplatform.py test-close --environment <environment name> --to <your mail address> --apply` does the same for the closing: its flow posts a card with the post step of the flow *Meternaut cards*, waits ten seconds and replaces the card with the update step of the same flow. It needs no queue and no role, and it shows whether the names of the body fields of the update step are right.
18. `python tools/powerplatform.py flow-runs --environment <environment name>` lists the last runs of the flow and, for a failed one, the actions that failed; the code of a run says which failure it was (`CardNotPosted`, `PostedMessageNotWritten`, `CardNotUpdated`, `MessageCannotBeRead`). This is how a card that did not arrive is followed: the app learns only which message a posted card is.
19. The card is in the chat of the person with the app *Workflows*. A guest finds it in the organisation of the tenant, not in the home organisation.

The import by an API call (`ImportSolutionAsync`) is not written: its connection references and environment variable values go in as a collection of entities that the Web API reference gives no example of.

**The way back.** Turn the flow off, so that cards wait in the queue; `python tools/powerplatform.py unlink ...` (only the project's own policy is unlinked, and the tool deletes nothing); wait 30 minutes; then remove the subnet and the policy by hand, the policy only after the unlink. No Hub action deletes them.

## The limits

| Limit | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Looking at the queue | At most once a minute, 32 messages at a time, so a card arrives within a minute or two. | [Limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-automate/limits-and-config), the connector's definition |
| Cards posted as the Flow bot | 25 requests per connection in 300 seconds. A burst above that is delayed by the retry of 15 minutes. | [Teams connector, throttling](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/teams/) |
| A flow that fails | A cloud flow whose trigger or actions fail without a break for 14 days is turned off. One that nothing triggers for 90 days may be turned off, unless its owner has a premium licence. | [Limits of cloud flows](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-automate/limits-and-config) |
| A developer environment | One that is inactive for 30 days is disabled. Whether a flow that only looks at a queue counts as use is not stated. | [Developer Plan](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/developer/plan) |
| A person behind the flow | The connections belong to a person. If that account is disabled, or a sign-in rule asks for a new sign-in, the flow stops and the cards wait in the queue until their messages expire. A dedicated service user is the usual cure; it needs its own licences and is not decided. This was seen on 2026-11-12: the trigger failed with Unauthorized because the sign-in token of the Azure Queues connection, and of the Teams connection, had expired and was not renewed; the owner is a guest of the tenant, and its rules probably stop a server from renewing a token (not proved). The app's messages do not depend on who owns the connections: whichever account does needs the two roles of step 4, and a card that an earlier account posted may not be replaceable by a later one (not tried). | [Azure Queues connector](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/azurequeues/) |
| A private channel | Posting to a private channel is not supported; the flow posts to a person's chat only. | [Teams connector](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/teams/) |
| Closing a card | **Unproven until `python tools/powerplatform.py test-close` has run live.** A public report (Microsoft Q&A, 10 September 2026) has the update step answer 404, "Invalid ThreadId", for a card that the Flow bot posted in its chat with a person; the moderator's answer says that this chat may give no thread that the update step can use. The flow ends its run as failed and deletes the message of a 404 (it would be tried for ever otherwise), and the app has already recorded the try as `sent`, because the queue took it. A closing that Teams refuses therefore shows in the runs of the flow (`flow-runs`) and not in the app: the card stays as it was. | [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/answers/questions/5999238/how-do-i-update-an-adaptive-card-post-posted-to-te), [Teams connector](https://learn.microsoft.com/connectors/teams/) |
| Region | The Power Platform region `sweden` maps to `swedencentral` only, so there is one network and no second region to fail over to. | [Supported regions](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/vnet-support-overview#supported-regions) |

## What is proven, and what is not

**Proven**, on 2026-11-11, with the owner's own sign-in (he is a guest of the tenant that holds the subscription) and the Hub on GitHub:

- The Hub action `powerplatform` ran on GitHub (OpenID Connect, no stored credential; the public log held none of the estate's identifiers): it made the subnet (a /27, delegated, with its own network security group) and the enterprise policy, the landing zone put its route table on the subnet within three minutes, and the read-back of both passed.
- `tools/powerplatform.py link` linked the environment (it was *Linked* about three minutes later). The address of the operation that the platform names is on its regional host (`unitedstates.api.bap.microsoft.com`), which the tool's guard refused the first time; it is fixed and tested.
- The maker portal refused the owner, and the platform's own API did not: the two connections (the Azure Queues one with *Microsoft Entra ID Integrated*, and the Teams one) were made and signed in with the commands `connections`, and the flow was made and started with `flow`. A first flow was refused with `ActionDescriptionTooLong` (an action description may have 256 characters; the solution's had 295), which is fixed in the solution too.
- The flow reads the private queue through the delegated subnet. The storage account has no public access, and a request that arrived over any other path would have been refused by the network; the queue answered `AuthorizationPermissionMismatch` before the role was given, which is its own answer to a request that reached it, and the same request succeeded once the role had applied.
- The flow posted the card as the Flow bot in the owner's chat with the app *Workflows*, in the organisation of that tenant, and then deleted the message. The Teams connector answered 201. The card was seen in Teams: first a test card, then three real requests that were waiting in the function app and that the app put on the queue within two minutes of the owner choosing Teams (a finding about a subscription, a private endpoint at 7.20 euro a month, and an older planted disk), each with the button *Review and decide*.
- The Azure Queues connection with Microsoft Entra ID worked for a B2B guest, although the connector's page says that guest accounts are not supported.
- The tool's own commands ran against the live environment: `connections`, `flow` (the second time as an update), `flow-runs` and `test-card`.
- The templates compile and pass the Bicep linter; the tests run the tool, the packer and the template checks against fakes.

**Not proven.**

- The solution has never been imported. The flow that runs was made by the API from the same definition, and it is not a solution: nothing here proves that the import in the maker portal works, and the designer may mark an action or write the definition again.
- That `Action.ToggleVisibility` shows and hides the part of a card that the Flow bot posted, on every client. If it does not, "Approve" has to be the link itself, and the page asks the second time, as it does for a letter. The answer links have been tested with fakes and not yet pressed in Teams.
- That the Teams action *Update an adaptive card in a chat or channel* takes the fields that the flow gives it (`recipient`, `messageId`, `messageBody`) for the chat with the Flow bot, and replaces the card. Learn names the action and its inputs, and not the fields of its dynamic body; the flow has not run with it, and a public report says that this step answers 404 "Invalid ThreadId" in that chat (see the limits). `test-close` is made for that. If the step needs the conversation id, which the app keeps, the app and the flow change together; if it refuses the chat for good, closing cannot work in it and the way of delivery has to change, which is the owner's decision.
- That the post action returns `id` and `conversationId` as Learn says, that the Functions host decodes the base64 that the flow writes, and that the person's connection may write to `teams-posted` once it has the role of step 4. A first real card shows all three: the app keeps a record of the kind `card-posted`, and a run of the flow that cannot write fails with `PostedMessageNotWritten`.
- That a card reaches a person who is a member of the tenant. Only the owner, a guest, has been written to.
- That the flow keeps running: the connections belong to a person, and a token that expires, an account that is disabled or a new sign-in rule stops it (see the limits). The first run of the flow after a long time is the thing to look at (`flow-runs`).
- That the licences of the owner cover a Managed Environment and a premium connector beyond a developer environment. The flow ran in the developer environment of the owner, with no licence step.
- That a flow which only looks at a queue keeps a developer environment alive.
- That the maker portal's import asks for the two connections and the two variables as step 12 says, and that its designer works with a storage account that has no public access. That a new developer environment can still be made and then turned managed after 15 November 2026.
- The custom prefix of the solution's parts is `mtnaut`, not `meternaut`: a publisher prefix has 2 to 8 characters ([pac solution init](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/developer/cli/reference/solution#pac-solution-init)).
