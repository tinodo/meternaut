# Beats: the investigation looks at the estate in parts, by what it costs — a decision record

**Status: decided on 2026-11-17, built and tested against fakes and scripted Foundry runs. Nothing here has run against a real estate or a real model: the arithmetic below is a design, and the first real run is what scores it.** Until the function app that carries it is published, nothing changes. Nothing was changed in Azure for this record.

## What was asked, and the evidence

One Investigator session looks at the estate as a single conversation, and it starves. The real runs of 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-17 on ten subscriptions (7,920 US dollars in 30 days) made 22 to 29 tool calls in 414 to 600 seconds and used 340,000 to 360,000 tokens, and the agent's own notes for the dearest kinds read "not examined before tool budget ended", "measurement hit budget limit" and "stopped because tool budget was exhausted". The first improved run found one quiet service and said of a large network appliance only that it had data processed and no waste was found: it looked for idle, and never asked whether the thing is larger than its use needs ([proposal 12](12-investigation-toolbox.md)). Which kinds got looked at depended on what the agent happened to open first.

The cost of a session is the second fact. A model reads the whole conversation again at every step, so the tokens of a session grow with the square of its steps: 22 calls were 345,323 tokens through the gateway, about 1.4 to 4 US dollars for each million tokens (the price through the gateway lies between the two), so about 0.5 to 1.4 dollars for one session. The owner's standing limit for each caller is 5 US dollars a day and 25 a month ([rule 15 (g)](../../AGENTS.md)), 50,000 tokens a minute and 60 requests a minute. Several full sessions in a Watch do not fit; a few short ones, started from what code has read already, do.

## The decision

1. **Code chooses the parts, by dollars, and names no kind.** From the cost lines that the Watch has already read (the same data as `estate_overview`), [`cases/beats.py`](../../src/case-functions/cases/beats.py) ranks the kinds of resource, and the lines of the bill that no resource owns as one more entry, by their cost of the last 30 days, dearest first. It keeps the dearest entries that together make 95 percent of the cost (`FLOOR_PERCENT`). A beat opens with the dearest entry that no beat has and takes the dearer-first entries of the same service after it, three kinds at most (`BEAT_KINDS`; the service is the part of the type before the slash). The last beat holds what is left: the kinds below the floor and those that do not fit in the number of beats, and it names the dearest eight (`CATCH_ALL_KINDS`) and counts the others. The plan has at most `INVESTIGATE_BEATS` beats (6), is the same for the same bill whatever the order of its lines, and is never made of a word that the code wrote: a kind goes in as the word that the cost lines gave and comes out as the same word. A test fails when a kind of resource is written in the module.
2. **One session of the Investigator for each beat, one after the other, on one toolbox.** The Foundry client is not safe for a second thread ([proposal 12](12-investigation-toolbox.md)). A beat has its own budget of tool calls, seconds and tokens, and every beat shares the run's toolbox: its handles, its evidence, its caches, its register of meters and its pass over the references, so that nothing is read twice. The toolbox allows 40 calls and what a run adds; each beat raises that when it starts to the calls made so far plus its own allowance, and never lowers it, so that a beat that starts late is not starved by the ones before it.
3. **Code reads the pictures first.** Before a beat starts, code calls the tools that the agent has, through the same toolbox, with no model: the listing of each of the beat's first five kinds, dearest first, and one `profile` of the dearest resources (the dearest of each kind first, then the next dearest of each, five in all). The beat's input holds the answer, so that the first step already has the facts, the meters, the metrics with data, the links and the age of the resources that cost most. The alternative was to leave it to the agent's own `profile` calls: that spends the first two or three steps of the beat on listing and reading, about a quarter of its tokens by the arithmetic below, to arrive where this one starts. What a tool answers is typed values and handles, as it always was, so nothing of the estate's text reaches a model, and the cited numbers are checked against the same evidence.
4. **A beat is told what the beats before it concluded, as typed lines.** For each earlier beat: its kinds and, when it gave an answer, the basis, the proposal and the handles of its findings and its own `examined` phrases (cleaned like every text of an AI; at most 3,000 characters in all, the oldest phrases cut first); when it gave none, only that it did not conclude, so that the next one knows its kinds were not judged. The instructions say in one general sentence what a beat is, that it may leave its part only when the links of a resource lead elsewhere, and that the lines of the bill that no resource owns can be a beat of their own. No kind, measurement or rule of waste is in the sentence, and the test that scans the instructions for such words holds.5. **One pool of findings.** Findings of all beats are numbered in turn and pooled. A resource is in one finding only: a later beat's finding about a resource that an earlier finding has loses that resource, which is counted as `repeated` on its line of the History, and the finding is dropped with that reason when none is left. Every finding goes through `_verify`, the guards, the pricing of [proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md) and the rules of the cases exactly as a finding of one session does; the cap of cases for a run and the limit of open requests are what they were.
6. **The Skeptic judges all the survivors, in as many calls as they take.** A finding joins the group before it while every resource of the group keeps the rows of the second richest level of detail within its input of 30,000 characters, so that it is not given poorer rows for being given more findings. At most four calls (`SKEPTIC_CALLS`), each with its own record in the harness; a finding that gets none has no verdict and goes to a person, as a finding without a verdict always did. The input is built by code in one place, so that the next change, which gives the Skeptic tools, can take the seam.
7. **A beat that fails is a note; a brake stops all of them.** A refusal of the model service, a time overrun or an answer that cannot be read fails that beat only: a note, a line of the History, and the other beats still count. When no beat gave an answer, the investigation failed, as one session did. A refusal of the harness (a pulled switch, a stored definition that differs, a run that used twice its tokens, three refusals in one run) in any beat holds the whole investigation: no case is opened from findings that nobody judged. The harness gives each beat a record of its own, so the three refusals count in one beat and its trace line holds that beat's tokens.

`INVESTIGATE_BEATS=1`, and an estate whose cost could not be read, give one open beat: the whole estate as one session, with `INVESTIGATE_MAX_CALLS` calls and the seconds of the investigation. That is the way back, and what every older test exercises.

## Budgets

| Setting | Default | Ceiling | What it bounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `INVESTIGATE_BEATS` | 6 | 8 | beats in a Watch; 1 is one open session |
| `INVESTIGATE_BEAT_CALLS` | 8 | 16 | tool calls of one beat (soft: the next are refused and the agent writes its answer) |
| `INVESTIGATE_BEAT_SECONDS` | 160 | 300 | one beat, including the reads that code makes for it; its tool calls are refused after 70 percent of what is left for the run |
| `INVESTIGATE_BEAT_TOKENS` | 100,000 | 200,000 | tokens of one beat before its tool calls are refused; the harness stops it at twice that |
| `INVESTIGATE_TOTAL_TOKENS` | 800,000 | 1,200,000 | tokens of all the beats; no beat starts with fewer than 30,000 left, and the last one gets what is left |
| `INVESTIGATE_MAX_SECONDS` | 1,080 (was 600) | 1,500 (was 600) | the whole investigation: six beats of 160 seconds, and 120 for the checks and the Skeptic. A beat does not start with fewer than 45 seconds left before that reserve |

Every setting is optional and a setting can lower the ceiling and never raise it. Only the seconds of the investigation are set by the template (`investigateMaxSeconds`, whose default and ceiling follow the code), so the Hub's `cases` is what changes the deployed value.

## The arithmetic

Measured on the fakes, with the real toolbox and an invented estate: the reads that code makes for a beat of three kinds are four calls (three listings of 224 to 282 characters and one picture of five resources of 4,973 characters), and the beat's first input is 5,238 characters. A picture may be as large as `profile` allows, 16,000 characters. Computed from the code: every step of every session also carries the instructions (9,508 characters) and the definitions of the 24 tools (26,493 characters), about 36,000 characters, which is about 9,000 tokens at four characters a token (an estimate; the real count is not measured here). So a beat of six steps costs about six times (9,000 plus 1,300 to 4,000 for the pictures) plus what its tool answers add to every later step, about 85,000 to 100,000 tokens: the target is six steps and 100,000 tokens, which is what the soft cap holds. After the cap the agent has the answers of its refused calls and writes its answer in one or two rounds, so a beat ends between 100,000 and 140,000 tokens.

| For a Watch | Tokens | At 1.4 US dollars a million | At 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical: six beats of 85,000 and a Skeptic of 30,000 | about 540,000 | 0.8 | 2.2 |
| Worst that the settings allow: the beats stop starting at 800,000, the last may end 40,000 over, the Skeptic takes up to 44,000 | about 880,000 | 1.2 | 3.5 |
| One session of 22 calls, for comparison | 345,000 | 0.5 | 1.4 |

- **A day** (5 US dollars): one Watch and the Study on the same Monday, at most 3.5 plus 0.5, fit. Two Watches on one day do not at the dearer price (7.0); at the cheaper price they do (2.4).
- **A month** (25): the weekly Watch and the weekly Study, 4.35 times, are 17.4 at the dearer price and 7.4 at the cheaper one at the worst size (3.5 plus 0.5, and 1.2 plus 0.5) and 11.7 and 4.4 at the typical size (2.2 plus 0.5, and 0.8 plus 0.2), so at least two runs that a person starts fit in the rest (7.6 left at the dearer price and the worst size). A daily Watch would be 25 to 107 and does not fit at the dearer price: the timer keeps waiting `INVESTIGATE_EVERY_DAYS` (7).
- **A minute** (50,000 tokens): 880,000 tokens need 17.6 minutes of the allowance and the investigation has 18, so the tokens a minute and the seconds are about equally binding. A beat of 100,000 tokens needs 2 minutes of the 160 seconds. When the gateway answers 429 the client waits (at most 90 seconds a call); a beat that is slowed down has its tool calls refused after 70 percent of its time and still writes its answer, and a beat that is cut off by its hard time is a failed beat and loses its findings. The real run will show which of the two limits ends a beat.
- **Time.** Reading ten subscriptions took 345 seconds. A Watch of that size is 345 reading, up to 1,080 investigating, a few minutes for the cases and the series, and the telemetry arrives late: about 29 minutes against the 30 that the action `cases-run` waits (`--minutes 30`, inside the container's 2,400 seconds). That is little room. A Watch that reads slowly (the reading may take up to 9 minutes) ends inside the function's time, which `host.json` now sets to 45 minutes (the Flex Consumption default is 30, the maximum is unbounded, an execution that passes it is cut off and its worker restarts, and a platform update gives a running execution 10 minutes of grace: [Learn, Function app timeout duration](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-functions/functions-scale#function-app-timeout-duration), read on 2026-11-17), and is recorded, but the check would say that no end of the run arrived in 30 minutes. If the first real run shows that, the wait of the action goes to 36 minutes (the container's 2,400 seconds still hold): a change of the workflow that this record does not make.

## A Watch is never run again by a redelivery

The control queue hands a message over again when the invocation that had it was cut off (a recycled worker, a platform update with its ten minutes of grace, the function's time) and `maxDequeueCount` is 5. Until now only a message older than 15 minutes was refused, so a Watch cut off in its first minutes and handed over again within 15 minutes of the message would have begun a second paid run; a Watch of 30 minutes makes such a cut more likely. `control.handle_message` now refuses, and records as `run.failed`, a message for a Watch or a Study that the queue says it has handed over before (`dequeue_count` above 1), whatever its age; the card test, which makes no model call, is not held. The owner starts a new run by hand. What a run that is cut off leaves behind (a case opened, a letter sent) is what it always was: a case is keyed by its resource and a run that is repeated by hand finds it judged lately.

## What is recorded and shown

- **History.** `investigation.beat`, one line for each beat: `beat`, `of`, `kinds` (how many), `billing`, `seeded` (the pictures that code read), `looked` (how many of its kinds its tools read), `toolCalls`, `tokens`, `seconds`, `end` (`answered`, `time`, `calls`, `tokens`, `refused` or `failed`), `ok` and `findings`. `investigation.started` and `investigation.finished` say how many beats there are (`beats`) and carry the totals; `investigation.finding` says which beat wrote it. The tool that reads a run for the Hub (`tools/cases-check.py`) takes the first start and the first end, and counts an end with `ok` false as a failed investigation, which a failed beat must not be: that is why a beat has a line of its own and no pair of its own.
- **The record of the investigation** ([proposal 23](23-what-the-ai-looked-at.md)) gains one line for each beat (its kinds, calls, seconds and tokens, how it ended, how many of its kinds its tools read and the first three phrases of what it said that it examined) and `more`, the findings that the record had no room for (it keeps at most 16 when it has beats, and fewer when a row of the store would not take them). No amount of money and no share of the bill is in it, nor on the page: the coverage is a count of the kinds that the tools read against the kinds that the estate has, ordered by cost that code computed ([rule 25](../../AGENTS.md)).
- **The page "What the AI looked at"** shows a block "The parts that the AI looked at" with a row for each beat, a column that names the beat in the table of findings and the new end word, "Its tokens ran out".

## What this does not do

- It does not prove that beats find more than one session did. The first real run is that test, and it can be scored by the lines above: each beat's calls, tokens and seconds, how it ended, how many of its kinds its tools read, and what each concluded.
- It does not prove the numbers of tokens, seconds and dollars: they are one measured session, the sizes of the code's own texts and the fakes, with four characters a token and two prices for the gateway. The reads that code makes for a beat were timed against a fake estate only; on a real one `profile` has up to 45 seconds for its reads, which comes out of the beat's 160.
- It does not widen what Meternaut may change: nothing here touches the runner, the approval, the delegated sign-in or a family of change, and the guards, the caps and the limit of open requests are not widened.
- A kind that the bill names and no resource has (cost lines of a deleted resource) is a kind with a count of none, and the beat says so. A resource that sits behind an attachment is found by the links of the dearest resource, if the agent leaves its part for it; nothing makes it.
- What the Hub must run: `cases` (the template carries the new ceiling of the seconds and the package carries `host.json` and the code), then `cases-run` once. No new right, no new resource and no new setting of the template.
