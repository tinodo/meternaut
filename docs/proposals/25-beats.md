# Beats: the investigation looks at the estate in parts, by what it costs — a decision record

**Status: decided on 2026-11-17, built and tested against fakes and scripted Foundry runs. Nothing here has run against a real estate or a real model: the arithmetic below is a design, and the first real run is what scores it.** Until the function app that carries it is published, nothing changes. Nothing was changed in Azure for this record.

## What was asked, and the evidence

One Investigator session looks at the estate as a single conversation, and it starves. The real runs of 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-17 on ten subscriptions (7,920 US dollars in 30 days) made 22 to 29 tool calls in 414 to 600 seconds and used 340,000 to 360,000 tokens, and the agent's own notes for the dearest kinds read "not examined before tool budget ended", "measurement hit budget limit" and "stopped because tool budget was exhausted". The first improved run found one quiet service and said of a large network appliance only that it had data processed and no waste was found: it looked for idle, and never asked whether the thing is larger than its use needs ([proposal 12](12-investigation-toolbox.md)). Which kinds got looked at depended on what the agent happened to open first.

The cost of a session is the second fact. A model reads the whole conversation again at every step, so the tokens of a session grow with the square of its steps: 22 calls were 345,323 tokens through the gateway, about 1.4 to 4 US dollars for each million tokens (the price through the gateway lies between the two), so about 0.5 to 1.4 dollars for one session. The owner's standing limit for each caller is 5 US dollars a day and 25 a month ([rule 15 (g)](../../AGENTS.md)), 50,000 tokens a minute and 60 requests a minute. Several full sessions in a Watch do not fit; a few short ones, started from what code has read already, do.

## The decision

1. **Code chooses the parts, by dollars, and names no kind.** From the cost lines that the Watch has already read (the same data as `estate_overview`), [`cases/beats.py`](../../src/case-functions/cases/beats.py) ranks the kinds of resource, and the lines of the bill that no resource owns as one more entry, by their cost of the last 30 days, dearest first. It keeps the dearest entries that together make 95 percent of the cost (`FLOOR_PERCENT`). A beat opens with the dearest entry that no beat has and takes the dearer-first entries of the same service after it, three kinds at most (`BEAT_KINDS`; the service is the part of the type before the slash). The last beat holds what is left: the kinds below the floor and those that do not fit in the number of beats, and it names the dearest eight (`CATCH_ALL_KINDS`) and counts the others. The plan has at most `INVESTIGATE_BEATS` beats (6), is the same for the same bill whatever the order of its lines, and is never made of a word that the code wrote: a kind goes in as the word that the cost lines gave and comes out as the same word. A test fails when a kind of resource is written in the module.
2. **One session of the Investigator for each beat, one after the other, on one toolbox.** The Foundry client is not safe for a second thread ([proposal 12](12-investigation-toolbox.md)). A beat has its own budget of tool calls, seconds and tokens, and every beat shares the run's toolbox: its handles, its evidence, its caches, its register of meters and its pass over the references, so that nothing is read twice. The toolbox allows 40 calls and what a run adds; each beat raises that when it starts to the calls made so far plus its own allowance, and never lowers it, so that a beat that starts late is not starved by the ones before it.
3. **Code reads the pictures first.** Before a beat starts, code calls the tools that the agent has, through the same toolbox, with no model: the listing of each of the beat's first five kinds, dearest first, and then two calls of `profile` for the five dearest resources (the dearest of each kind first, then the next dearest of each): **the dearest three at the grain of the hour and the other two at the day**. The beat's input holds the two answers, so that the first step already has the facts, the meters, the metrics with data, the links and the age of the resources that cost most. The alternative was to leave it to the agent's own `profile` calls: that spends the first two or three steps of the beat on listing and reading, about a quarter of its tokens by the arithmetic below, to arrive where this one starts. What a tool answers is typed values and handles, as it always was, so nothing of the estate's text reaches a model, and the cited numbers are checked against the same evidence. **Why the hour for three.** A row of a metric at the grain of the day has the total, the maximum and the average of daily buckets and no peak; what a resource must carry, which is what decides whether it is larger than it needs to be, shows in the peak and the 95th percentile of the finer buckets, and the picture gives those only at the hour. The orchestrator measured on the real estate (2026-11-17, read only, no model, ten subscriptions, `main` at `9a4720b`) the picture of the dearest resource of each of the five dearest kinds: **24 seconds at the day and 39.5 at the hour**, both complete (`partial` false, nothing unread), and each filling the 16,000 characters that the answer may have (15,995 and 15,988). Three at the hour is one for each kind of a beat that holds three, and the three dearest of a beat of one kind; the other two cost less at the day. The pair was estimated at about 34 seconds from those two figures (8 seconds a resource at the hour, 5 at the day); the whole seeding was then timed on the real estate, beat by beat, and is in the next section ('What the seeds took'). **Each of the two pictures that code reads has a time of its own, `SEED_PICTURE_SECONDS` (25 seconds), inside the 45 that the reads of a call may take:** what is not read by then is `unread` in the answer, as it is for any call whose time runs out, and the Investigator's own `profile` calls keep the seconds of the toolbox. It is a bound by time and not by the number of metrics, because what made a seed long was a picture of resources that offer very many metrics, and any rule that names which metrics to read at the finer grain has to rank them (the greatest daily maximum first would need the day read before the hour) and so to change the picture's task; this record does not make that change (see 'What this does not do'). The Investigator can still ask for an hourly picture of any other resource.
4. **A beat is told what the beats before it concluded, as typed lines.** For each earlier beat: its kinds and, when it gave an answer, the basis, the proposal and the handles of its findings and its own `examined` phrases (cleaned like every text of an AI; at most 3,000 characters in all, the oldest phrases cut first); when it gave none, only that it did not conclude, so that the next one knows its kinds were not judged. The instructions say in one general sentence what a beat is, that it may leave its part only when the links of a resource lead elsewhere, and that the lines of the bill that no resource owns can be a beat of their own. No kind, measurement or rule of waste is in the sentence, and the test that scans the instructions for such words holds.
5. **One pool of findings.** Findings of all beats are numbered in turn and pooled. A resource is in one finding only, and **the surer finding keeps it**: a later beat's finding about a resource that an earlier finding has takes it only when its confidence is higher (the earlier finding gives it up, which is counted as `surpassed` on its line of the History, and it is dropped with that reason when none is left); with the same confidence the earlier keeps it, and the later finding loses it, which is counted as `repeated`, and is dropped with that reason when none is left. A finding that the check of the cited measurements drops (a handle that no tool showed, a claim of no usage that no measurement proves) never takes a resource, so a resource does not end with no finding because a finding that could not stand had it first. What the guards, the check of 'judged lately' and the cap of cases do afterwards is about the resource (the guards) or the facts that a finding cites ('judged lately'), and is done after the beats, on the findings that are left: a resource that is held back there is held back for the finding that has it, and it is not handed to the finding that lost it, which would meet the same guards, but which might cite other facts than the first for 'judged lately'; that is a rare case that this record does not cover. Every finding goes through `_verify`, the guards, the pricing of [proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md) and the rules of the cases exactly as a finding of one session does; the cap of cases for a run and the limit of open requests are what they were.
6. **The Skeptic judges all the survivors, in as many calls as they take.** A finding joins the group before it while every resource of the group keeps the rows of the second richest level of detail within its input of 30,000 characters, so that it is not given poorer rows for being given more findings. At most four calls (`SKEPTIC_CALLS`), each with its own record in the harness; a finding that gets none has no verdict and goes to a person, as a finding without a verdict always did. The input is built by code in one place, so that the next change, which gives the Skeptic tools, can take the seam.
7. **A beat that fails is a note; a brake stops all of them.** A refusal of the model service, a time overrun, an answer that cannot be read or **a beat that uses more tokens than its hard limit** (twice its cap, or its cap and 60,000, whichever is more: `harness.beat_limit`) fails that beat only: a note ("over its limit of …", and the others go on), `ok` false on its line `investigation.beat`, `failed` with the reason `tokens` on its line `harness.run`, and the other beats still count and keep their findings; **it pulls no switch and sends no letter**, because a beat that asks once more after its cap is a model that was slow to stop and no loop. When no beat gave an answer, the investigation failed, as one session did. A refusal of the harness (a pulled switch, a stored definition that differs, three refusals in one run, a repeated call) in any beat holds the whole investigation: no case is opened from findings that nobody judged, and a run with no cap (the open beat) keeps its brake on tokens, which pulls the switch. The harness gives each beat a record of its own, so the three refusals count in one beat and its trace line holds that beat's tokens.

`INVESTIGATE_BEATS=1`, and an estate whose cost could not be read, give one open beat: the whole estate as one session, with `INVESTIGATE_MAX_CALLS` calls and **480 seconds for tool calls (`OPEN_SECONDS`), as one session had them when the investigation had 600 seconds, whatever the seconds of the investigation are**. That is the way back, and what every older test exercises.

## Budgets

| Setting | Default | Ceiling | What it bounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `INVESTIGATE_BEATS` | 6 | 8 | beats in a Watch; 1 is one open session |
| `INVESTIGATE_BEAT_CALLS` | 8 | 16 | tool calls of one beat (soft: the next are refused and the agent writes its answer) |
| `INVESTIGATE_BEAT_SECONDS` | 160 | 300 | the time of the model and its tool calls in one beat, on top of the reads that code makes for it, **and never more than `MODEL_SECONDS` (100)**: the setting can lower it. Its tool calls are refused after 70 percent of it |
| `INVESTIGATE_BEAT_TOKENS` | 100,000 | 200,000 | tokens of one beat before its tool calls are refused; a beat that uses more than twice that, or more than that and 60,000, is a failed beat |
| `INVESTIGATE_TOTAL_TOKENS` | 800,000 | 1,200,000 | tokens of all the beats; **no beat starts with fewer than 60,000 left** (a step of the model costs 13 to 20 thousand, and one that asks once more after its cap must still end inside its hard limit), and the last one gets what is left |
| `INVESTIGATE_MAX_SECONDS` | 1,080 (was 600) | 1,500 (was 600) | the whole investigation: the reads that code makes for the beats, 100 seconds of model and tool calls for each beat, and 120 for the checks and the Skeptic. **A beat is not started when what is left before that reserve is less than its own reads plus its allowance** (the mean of the reads of the beats before it, 60 seconds when none has been read, plus the seconds that its model will have, 100 or what `INVESTIGATE_BEAT_SECONDS` sets when that is less, and never less than `MIN_SECONDS`, 90); the kinds of the beats that were not started are in the record as not looked at |

Every setting is optional and a setting can lower the ceiling and never raise it. Only the seconds of the investigation are set by the template (`investigateMaxSeconds`, whose default and ceiling follow the code), so the Hub's `cases` is what changes the deployed value.

## The arithmetic

Measured on the fakes, with the real toolbox and an invented estate of five resources of one kind with seven metrics each: the reads that code makes for a beat are one listing for each of its kinds (224 to 282 characters each on the fakes) and the two pictures, and **the beat's first input is 7,057 characters**: the pictures of 4,244 (three at the hour) and 2,666 (two at the day) and what else the input holds. On the fakes one picture of five at the day is 6,582 characters and at the hour 7,038. A picture of the real estate fills its cap, and the two caps are 9,600 for three handles and 6,400 for two, 16,000 in all, the same as the cap of one picture of five: the first input is then at most about 16,300 characters, and about 19,300 with the 3,000 that the earlier beats may add, about 4,800 tokens at four characters a token. Computed from the code: every step of every session also carries the instructions (9,508 characters) and the definitions of the 24 tools (26,493 characters), about 36,000 characters, which is about 9,000 tokens at four characters a token (an estimate; the real count is not measured here). So a beat of six steps costs about six times (9,000 plus 1,300 to 4,000 for the pictures) plus what its tool answers add to every later step, about 85,000 to 100,000 tokens: the target is six steps and 100,000 tokens, which is what the soft cap holds. After the cap the agent has the answers of its refused calls and writes its answer in one or two rounds, so a beat ends between 100,000 and 140,000 tokens.

**What the Investigator's own `profile` of the same resources reads again.** The two calls of the seed are calls of the tool `profile` with the arguments that the Investigator could give itself (`handles`, `days` null, `grain` hour or day), and the toolbox keeps what they read. A test that counts the questions that the fake Azure got shows that a second call for the same handles, at the same or at the other grain, asks Resource Graph, Cost Management, Advisor and the Activity Log nothing: the references, the ages, the Advisor items, the meters, the Activity Log and the diagnostic settings are kept. **The metrics are not kept**: the second call reads them again (33 reads for three handles at the hour on the fake, against 53 for the whole seed). So a repeat is not a second read of 25 to 40 seconds and is not free; what the metrics take of the 24 and 39.5 seconds on the real estate is not known, as those figures are for all the parts. A cache of the metric reads is a small change in the picture's task that this record does not make; the lines of the tool calls will show whether the Investigator repeats a picture.

**What the seeds took on the real estate.** The orchestrator rehearsed the plan and the seeds of tip `8cd40b2` on the real estate (read only, no model, ten subscriptions, 2026-11-17): the plan of the real costs, then the seed of each beat through `Toolbox.preload`. The plan has 22 kinds with a cost: the first beat has three kinds that are about a third of the dollars, the next four beats one kind each, the sixth is the catch-all that names eight kinds, and about seven smaller kinds are in no beat and only counted, which is right for a first run. **The seeds took 56.0, 31.9, 104.1, 20.9, 7.6 and 29.2 seconds: 250 seconds in all, 24 tool calls, none failed.** A picture is 7 to 10.5 thousand characters at the hour and 4 to 7 at the day, so the input of a beat is 12 to 17 thousand characters (on the fakes it is 7,057). The third seed took 104 seconds because the three resources of its kind, read at the hour, offer about 99 metrics each; while a beat's 160 seconds included its reads, the model had about 56 seconds there and about 100 in the first beat. **So a beat now has a time for the model that does not depend on its reads**: `MODEL_SECONDS` (100) for the model and its tool calls, on top of the reads, inside the time of the investigation; a beat whose model time is left under `STEP_SECONDS` (30) is not asked at all and fails at no cost in tokens; and the two pictures of a seed have 25 seconds each, so the third seed cannot take much more than its listings (7 to 10 seconds) and two pictures: about 60 seconds. That bound is by construction (a test sees the 25 seconds on the code's calls and the toolbox's own on the agent's), the rehearsal was made before it, and only a real run shows the seconds.

**The arithmetic of the seconds.** Six seeds of 250 seconds, measured, and six allowances of 100 seconds for the model: **250 + 600 = 850 seconds**, against the 960 that the beats have (the 1,080 of the investigation less the 120 that the checks and the Skeptic keep): 110 seconds of room. The beats do not all need their 100 seconds, and a beat is not started when what is left is less than the mean of the reads so far plus 100 (60 plus 100 before any has been read), so a slow seed ends the beats early and says which kinds were not looked at, instead of starting a beat that could not afford its model.

|---|---|---|---|
| Typical: six beats of 85,000 and a Skeptic of 30,000 | about 540,000 | 0.8 | 2.2 |
| Worst that the settings allow: the beats stop starting at 800,000, the last may end 40,000 over, the Skeptic takes up to 44,000 | about 880,000 | 1.2 | 3.5 |

- **A day** (5 US dollars): one Watch and the Study on the same Monday, at most 3.5 plus 0.5, fit. Two Watches on one day do not at the dearer price (7.0); at the cheaper price they do (2.4).
- **A month** (25): the weekly Watch and the weekly Study, 4.35 times, are 17.4 at the dearer price and 7.4 at the cheaper one at the worst size (3.5 plus 0.5, and 1.2 plus 0.5) and 11.7 and 4.4 at the typical size (2.2 plus 0.5, and 0.8 plus 0.2), so at least two runs that a person starts fit in the rest (7.6 left at the dearer price and the worst size). A daily Watch would be 25 to 107 and does not fit at the dearer price: the timer keeps waiting `INVESTIGATE_EVERY_DAYS` (7).
- **A minute** (50,000 tokens): 880,000 tokens need 17.6 minutes of the allowance and the investigation has 18, so the tokens a minute and the seconds are about equally binding. A beat of 100,000 tokens needs 2 minutes at that rate and a beat has 100 seconds for the model and its tool calls, so a beat that spends its whole cap is held back by the gateway or ends by its time before it ends by its tokens. When the gateway answers 429 the client waits (at most 90 seconds a call); a beat that is slowed down has its tool calls refused after 70 percent of its time and still writes its answer, and a beat that is cut off by its hard time is a failed beat and loses its findings. The real run will show which of the two limits ends a beat.
- **Time.** Reading ten subscriptions took 345 seconds. A Watch of that size is 345 reading, 850 investigating at the measured seeds (up to 1,080 at the most), a few minutes for the cases and the series, and the telemetry arrives late: about 25 minutes at the typical size and about 29 at the slowest settings (the figure in the workflow of the Hub), against **the 38 minutes that the action `cases-run` waits (`--minutes 38`) and the 45 minutes (2,700 seconds) that its container group is given**, both already on `main` (pull request 51). A Watch that reads slowly (the reading may take up to 9 minutes) ends inside the function's time, which `host.json` sets to 45 minutes (the Flex Consumption default is 30, the maximum is unbounded, an execution that passes it is cut off and its worker restarts, and a platform update gives a running execution 10 minutes of grace: [Learn, Function app timeout duration](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-functions/functions-scale#function-app-timeout-duration), read on 2026-11-17), and is recorded: 540 reading, 1,080 investigating, a few minutes for the cases and the telemetry make about 33 minutes, inside the 38 with little room.

## A Watch is never run again by a redelivery

The control queue hands a message over again when the invocation that had it was cut off (a recycled worker, a platform update with its ten minutes of grace, the function's time) and `maxDequeueCount` is 5. Until now only a message older than 15 minutes was refused, so a Watch cut off in its first minutes and handed over again within 15 minutes of the message would have begun a second paid run; a Watch of 30 minutes makes such a cut more likely. `control.handle_message` now refuses, and records as `run.failed`, a message for a Watch or a Study that the queue says it has handed over before (`dequeue_count` above 1), whatever its age; the card test, which makes no model call, is not held. The owner starts a new run by hand. What a run that is cut off leaves behind (a case opened, a letter sent) is what it always was: a case is keyed by its resource and a run that is repeated by hand finds it judged lately.

**The wait of 7 days starts from a Watch that spent tokens, whatever came of it.** `investigation_wait` counts from the runs that investigated, and that was true only when a beat gave an answer: a Watch in which every beat failed (a hard time, answers that could not be read) spent up to about 880,000 tokens and started no wait, so the daily timer would have run it again the next day, against the 5 US dollars of a day and 25 of a month. A run now counts as having investigated when a beat answered **or any token was spent**; a run that could not begin (nothing read, no model call) still starts no wait. The catch-up of the timer after a worker restart (a timer that is past due and fires again 10 or 50 minutes later) is tested with the fake clock: it starts no second Watch inside the wait. **What this does not cover:** a Watch that is cut off inside its investigation writes no record of the run, so there is nothing to count from, and a timer that is past due after the restart could start one more Watch; the guard above holds the redelivery of the queue and not the timer. It is rare (the function's time is 45 minutes and a Watch ends at about 33 at the slowest), it is bounded by the limits of tokens and dollars of a day and a month, and starting the wait earlier would undo the rule that a run that could not investigate starts none.

## What is recorded and shown

- **History.** `investigation.beat`, one line for each beat: `beat`, `of`, `kinds` (how many), `billing`, `seeded` (the pictures that code read), `looked` (how many of its kinds its tools read), `toolCalls`, `tokens`, `seconds`, `end` (`answered`, `time`, `calls`, `tokens`, `refused` or `failed`), `ok` and `findings`. `investigation.call`, one line for each tool call ([below](#one-line-for-each-tool-call)). `investigation.started` and `investigation.finished` say how many beats there are (`beats`) and carry the totals; `investigation.finding` says which beat wrote it. The tool that reads a run for the Hub (`tools/cases-check.py`) takes the first start and the first end, and counts an end with `ok` false as a failed investigation, which a failed beat must not be: that is why a beat has a line of its own and no pair of its own.
- **The record of the investigation** ([proposal 23](23-what-the-ai-looked-at.md)) gains one line for each beat (its kinds, calls, seconds and tokens, how it ended, how many of its kinds its tools read and the first three phrases of what it said that it examined) and `more`, the findings that the record had no room for (it keeps at most 16 when it has beats, and fewer when a row of the store would not take them). No amount of money and no share of the bill is in it, nor on the page: the coverage is a count of the kinds that the tools read against the kinds that the estate has, ordered by cost that code computed ([rule 25](../../AGENTS.md)).
- **The page "What the AI looked at"** shows a block "The parts that the AI looked at" with a row for each beat, a column that names the beat in the table of findings and the new end word, "Its tokens ran out".

## One line for each tool call

**Why.** The first real run of one open session (2026-11-17, read by the orchestrator in Application Insights) made 22 calls in 450 seconds and 378,575 tokens, and found one thing that it had found before. The model had been called 24 times, about 3.5 seconds each, so about 85 seconds of the 450 were the model's: the rest, about 360 seconds, were tools, about 15 seconds for a call. Nothing in the History said which tool, for what, or how long: the private record of the investigation says what it looked at and not how long each read took, and the people who read a real run cannot read it. So each tool call that runs writes one line, `investigation.call`, and the run can be read from the History alone.

**What a line holds**, all words and numbers that code checks: `beat` and `n` (the call in its beat, the reads that code makes first), `by` (`code` for the reads that code makes for a beat, `agent` for the calls of the Investigator), `tool` (a tool of the toolbox, `preload`, or `unknown` for any other name), `resourceType` (only when the arguments hold a resource type in the shape that Azure gives, as `type_word` of the toolbox checks it, and never any other text of the arguments: it is not called `type`, which is the kind of the line), `handles` and `metrics` (how many it asked about, not which), `ok`, `error` (when it did not work: the word of the toolbox's closed list, or `budget-used-up`; any other word is `other`), `chars` (of the answer) and `seconds` (by the toolbox's own clock, to a tenth). No handle, name, id, tag, text of an agent, text of an answer or amount is in it, and a test feeds it hostile text to show that. The first eight calls that the Investigator asked for after a beat's budget was used up, and that were refused, have a line too (`ok` false, `error` `budget-used-up`, no seconds): what it still wanted is what a person reading the History looks for; the others are counted by the line of the beat.

**How many.** For each beat at most its calls (8 by default, 16 at the ceiling), the 7 reads that code makes (five listings and the two pictures) and the read ahead, and 8 refused: 144 lines for six beats by default and 256 for eight beats at the ceilings; one open session has at most 40 and 8 refused. A line is about 290 characters (about a kilobyte in Application Insights with its own fields, an estimate), so a run adds about 140 KB by default and 260 KB at the ceilings: a few hundred kilobytes a run is small against what the rest of the app writes, though it was not priced here, and no field is cut. `host.json` has the sampling of Application Insights off, so no line is dropped on the way. The check of the Hub counts them like the others (`investigationLines` of the end of the run) and waits for them, which is more lines to wait for and nothing else.

**What the Hub prints.** `tools/cases-check.py` prints, in the log of a public repository too, a line for each beat (kinds, what code read first, kinds looked at, calls, tokens, seconds, how it ended, findings) and, from the call lines, how many tool calls ran and how long, how many gave no answer and why, the reads of code against the calls of the Investigator, and the calls and the seconds of each tool, the longest first. Counts, seconds and tool names: a name that is not a plain lower-case word is `other`, and the resource type is never printed.

**What it cannot tell.** Which metric, how many days, which grain, the page of a listing or what an answer said are not in a line: the line says which tool took the time, for how many resources, and whether it worked. The model's own time is a beat's seconds less its calls' seconds.

## What runs at the same time, and what does not

**Inside a call, as before:** the subscriptions of one read (4 at a time, the limit of Cost Management), the parts of a picture (`profile`: up to 6 reads, in 60 seconds) and the metrics (8). **New, for the reads that code makes for a beat:** `Toolbox.preload` reads the resources of the beat's kinds at the same time, one question of Resource Graph for each, in a pool of at most five (the quota of Resource Graph is the identity's, so the pool is no wider than the five kinds of a seed). The workers only read; what they read is registered by the thread that called, in the order of the kinds, by the code that registers a listing's own read, so no state of the toolbox is touched by two threads; and the method holds the toolbox's lock as a call does, so nothing runs beside it. The listings that follow find the resources in the caches and ask nothing. A type that is read already, a word that has not the shape of a type and a single kind are left alone, and a read that fails is left to the listing, which asks again and says why in its own words. A test shows that the reads overlap (a fake whose questions wait until all are in flight, so that reads one after the other would not pass), that the answers and the handles are what the listings give alone, and that the lock holds. The History has one line for it (`by` `code`, `tool` `preload`).

**What it is worth is an estimate.** After the first, a listing of a kind is one question of Resource Graph, about a second or two; five in a row are 5 to 10 seconds and together about 2: under 10 seconds a beat, about 50 of the 1,080 seconds of a Watch. The reads that take the time are the pictures (24 seconds at the day and 39.5 at the hour for five resources on the real estate, the measure above) and the calls of the agent, and a picture is parallel inside its call already and needs the handles that the listings give, so it cannot start before them. The time of the pair was estimated and not timed there; the seeds of the six beats were then timed on the real estate (250 seconds, above), and in a real run the lines above will show the time tool by tool.

**Not built: the pictures of the next beat prepared while the model of this beat works.** It is not safe on this toolbox, and what it could win is small:

1. The Foundry client and the code's own reads share one HTTP client (`run.py` builds `Foundry(..., client, ...)` and `Arm(client, ...)`). During a model call the Foundry client replaces the client's transport by a wrapper that keeps the last answers (for the words of its own errors) and counts the answers that say "not now", and it sets the client's deadline to the end of the beat. A read from a second thread would go through that wrapper, so that its answers would be taken for the model's and its limits counted as the service's, and under the deadline of the beat. [Proposal 12](12-investigation-toolbox.md) reached the same answer for a read ahead.
2. `Toolbox.call` holds one lock for a whole call, by design: the time of a call, the handles, the evidence and the caches are one state. A read ahead through the toolbox would be serialized with the agent's own calls: it could overlap only the model's thinking (about 3.5 seconds a step by the measure above, so under half a minute for a beat) and the agent's next call would wait for it.
3. A beat counts its calls, its log, its allowance (`extra_calls`) and the kinds that it looked at by position in one log. A read ahead in the middle of another beat would put its calls in the wrong beat or need locks round all of it, and the order of the log, and so the numbers of the handles and the lines of the History, would depend on timing.

What would make it safe is a second `Arm` with an HTTP client of its own for the read ahead and a toolbox whose caches take their own lock: a change of the toolbox that is larger than this record and is worth making only if the lines show that the seeds are what ends the beats. The gain is at most the model's thinking time of a beat, an estimate.

## What this does not do

- It does not prove that beats find more than one session did. The first real run is that test, and it can be scored by the lines above: each beat's calls, tokens and seconds, how it ended, how many of its kinds its tools read, and what each concluded.
- It does not prove the numbers of tokens, seconds and dollars: they are one measured session, the sizes of the code's own texts and the fakes, with four characters a token and two prices for the gateway. The seeds were timed on the real estate by the orchestrator, before the picture time of 25 seconds and the allowance of 100 seconds for the model existed: what those two change is by construction and tested on the fakes (the seconds that a picture of code is given, and a model time that does not follow the reads), and only a real run shows the seconds. The number of metrics that a picture reads at the hour is not bounded by a count (a cap that takes the metrics with the greatest daily maximum first needs the day read before the hour, a change of the picture's task and of the locked texts that this record does not make); the time of a picture is.
- It does not widen what Meternaut may change: nothing here touches the runner, the approval, the delegated sign-in or a family of change, and the guards, the caps and the limit of open requests are not widened.
- A kind that the bill names and no resource has (cost lines of a deleted resource) is a kind with a count of none, and the beat says so. A resource that sits behind an attachment is found by the links of the dearest resource, if the agent leaves its part for it; nothing makes it.
- It does not prove the time that the read ahead saves, nor that the lines of the tool calls are enough to read a real run by: both are what the first real run shows. The lines are tested against fakes whose calls are scripted, and the number of lines in the telemetry and the time that the check of the Hub waits for them were not measured.
- What the Hub must run: `cases` (the template carries the new ceiling of the seconds and the package carries `host.json` and the code), then `cases-run` once, which waits 38 minutes and whose container group is given 45 (both are on `main` already, so no change of the workflow comes with this record). No new right, no new resource and no new setting of the template.
