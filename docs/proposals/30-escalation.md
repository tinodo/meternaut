# Escalation: a request to a person never times out and never becomes invisible — a decision record

**Status: decided on 2026-11-17 (the owner's rule, [AGENTS.md rule 38](../../AGENTS.md)); built and tested on this machine against fakes and a fake clock; not deployed and not run in the Hub. What is not proved is at the end.**

## Why

Before this, a request to a person (a plan of any family: advice, or a change) lived 72 hours (`PLAN_TTL_HOURS`). When nobody answered, it was closed as `expired`, its case went to `expired`, the Savings page filed it under "Declined or expired", and nothing reminded anybody. A finding that nobody answered faded. The owner's words, 2026-11-17: "Requests should not time out, rather they need to be escalated and should NEVER timeout and become invisible. ... To escalate to the manager: check Entra ID if you can find a manager or something. NEVER SILENTLY IGNORE!!!!!" What Advisor and Cost Management show today is ignored, and the money is wasted: Meternaut exists to be answered.

## What was decided

1. **No clock closes a request.** The time to live is gone (`ApprovalSettings.ttl_hours`, `Approvals.expire`, the setting `PLAN_TTL_HOURS` and the template parameter `planTtlHours`). A new plan has no end time. The stored word `expired` now means only "taken back by Meternaut, with a reason" (`brief.TAKEN_BACK`), and every page, letter and card says "Taken back" and the reason. No page says "open until". A request ends by a person's answer, by a change that was made or refused, or by Meternaut taking it back in words. A change plan of any age is still read again at the click: the runner re-reads the resource and its locks after the sign-in and refuses a change that no longer holds, which opens the request again with the reason.
2. **The requests that the old clock closed come back.** At each Watch, for each resource, the newest request that the clock closed (`expired`, no reason, and an end that is the start plus a whole number of hours from 1 to 336) and that nobody answered is opened again, and its case goes back to what it was before. The History says `plan.revived`. A resource that has an open request, a request that a person answered, a case that moved on, or a request that Meternaut took back with a reason is left alone, and a second run changes nothing. The plan's digest still holds, because revival changes only its state.
3. **The ladder** ([`cases/escalation.py`](../../src/case-functions/cases/escalation.py)), counted from the start of the wait (`waiting_since`, else the time the request was made; item 13), one step at a time and at least a day apart, so that a request that has waited long is caught up one step a day, never in a burst. Every read is for one request (`escalations_of(plan_id)`): there is no list of the newest rows, because the table store answers the first rows in key order, and a request past the first thousand would show no steps and never move:

   | Step | Age | Who |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | 3 days | A reminder to the people that it was made for |
   | 2 | 7 days | The next person (below) |
   | 3 | 14 days | The next level: the manager of that manager when there is one, else the escalation contacts again |
   | 4 | 21 days | The escalation contacts |
   | 5 and on | every 7 days | The contacts and the people of the chain together, for as long as it waits |

   Each step is one record (table `mnescalations`, claimed once, so a second run or a second instance makes none again): who, by which source, what could not be looked up on the way, and what became of the message. A person who puts the request off ("later") delays the next step by 7 days, the first three times only: a put-off never silences. "Not mine" on a request of advice keeps it open and makes the next step due at once.
4. **Who is next** ([`cases/directory.py`](../../src/case-functions/cases/directory.py)), in this order, each reported by a closed word: the manager that Microsoft Entra names (when `ESCALATE_TO` is `managers`; then the manager's manager); an address in the tag `escalation` on the resource, its group or its subscription; the people with the role `escalation` in `CONSOLE_PEOPLE`; and, when none of these is found, all the owners, with the plain note "No escalation contact is set": a request is never dropped and never sent nowhere. A lookup that fails is not an error but a sentence in the message ("I could not look up the manager (no permission yet)", "No manager is set for the person in the directory", and so on), and the next source is tried. A person from the directory is a target only when they are a Member of the tenant, their account is enabled, they have a mail address, and the address is not on `ESCALATION_DENY`; their name is cleaned, cut and shown escaped, and nothing else of the profile is kept.
5. **Delivery is by letter only.** Teams cards are not used for escalation. A person gets at most one escalation letter a day, a list when several requests have come to them, and a person who never heard from Meternaut is told who writes and why. A switch that is pulled, a pause, quiet hours and the one-letter-a-day rule hold a step, and a held step is kept, shown ("held: the reason") and sent when the hold ends; a failed send is tried again every 15 minutes, 12 times, and then once a day. Held means delayed and visible, never lost. The weekly budget of advice messages does not count escalation letters.
6. **The weekly letter never stops.** For as long as a request has waited more than a week, the escalation contacts get one letter every week of everything that still waits (oldest first, ten lines at most, then "and N more"), and when something stops the ladder (a Graph permission that is missing, a message that cannot be delivered) the owners get it too, with that problem named once.
7. **Waiting is shown with its cost.** A request's page has a block "Waiting" (since when, with whom it is now, who was told and when, how often it was put off, and what waiting has cost: the figure of a month over 30 days for the days waited, "about" for a scenario, nothing without a measured saving). The start page has "Waiting the longest". The Savings page has the rows "Declined" and "Taken back" instead of "Declined or expired", and says how long the longest request has waited.
8. **The settings.** `ESCALATE_TO`: `contacts` (the default) or `managers`; `off` is refused with a plain message, because a request that nobody answers is always passed on. The Hub variable `ESCALATE_TO` carries it (`tools/environment-params.py` refuses any other word and writes `escalateTo`; the template sets the setting), so the grant step "set `escalateTo`" is real. The others are read with their defaults: `ESCALATION_DAYS` (four increasing whole numbers, `3,7,14,21`), `ESCALATION_REPEAT_DAYS` (7), `ESCALATION_TAG` (`escalation`), `ESCALATION_DENY` (addresses or `@domains` that are never written to), `ESCALATION_SNOOZES` (3) and `ESCALATION_SNOOZE_DAYS` (7). The ladder runs only when `MAIL_SENDER` is set, because it sends letters. Without it the start page, the Savings page and the request page say "Escalation is not running: no mail sender is set up, so a request that nobody answers is not passed on", and every Watch writes one History line (`escalation.off`): it is never silent.
9. **Who may answer.** A person with the role `escalation` may answer a request of advice that the ladder has come to with them (a step names them), and no other; any other request of advice, and every change, needs an approver who is named on the request.
10. **A held finding does not wait.** A finding that a guard holds back is the state `held` ([proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)): nobody is asked about it, so the ladder makes no step for it, its page has no "Waiting" block, the start page and the Savings page do not count it among the requests that wait, and no clock closes it. The pages keep three words apart: *open* (a request that waits, and is passed on), *held back* (nobody is asked) and *taken back* (Meternaut closed the request and says why; a held finding that is released or left alone is closed the same way). When a held finding is asked about as any other, the request starts its wait then.
11. **Places for requests are freed by answers only.** The ten places for the findings that the AIs did not settle (`ADVICE_OPEN_MAX`, [proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)) used to be freed by the 72-hour clock. Now only an answer, or Meternaut taking a request back and saying why (item 12 is the one that works without a person), frees one: a request that is passed on keeps its place, and the findings beyond the ten wait as held ("waiting for a free place") until one is free.
12. **A request about a resource that is gone is taken back.** With no clock, nothing else ends a request whose finding stopped being true. At each Watch (after the revival) the open requests are checked against Resource Graph (`escalation.existence`: fixed queries that name only ids of the shape of an id). A request is taken back with the word `gone` (the pages say "Taken back: the resource is gone") only when it is certain: all the reads worked, the Watch read the subscription in full (no source failed, the recent creations and the managed groups were read), the subscription answered, and either the resource group is not there, or the resource is not there and Resource Graph lists its type (the types of the detectors, or one that the subscription lists now: a type that it does not list would look gone for ever, and is reported as not checked). A partial, failed or throttled read judges nothing: the request keeps waiting, and the History says how many were not checked and why. **Two limits are accepted.** Only the Watch takes a request back: the timer never does, because it has no full read of the subscription, so a request about a resource that is gone waits for the next Watch, and the ladder goes on meanwhile. And a resource that moved (to another resource group or another subscription) is not at its old id, so its request counts as gone and is taken back; the Watch makes a new request for it at its new id, and that request starts its wait anew.
13. **A request made again keeps its wait.** When a request of the same finding replaces another (the request of a group that replaces the requests of its resources, or a fresher request for the same resource and the same people), the one that stays carries the oldest start (`waiting_since`: the ladder, the cost of waiting and the pages use it), the steps already made for the old request count as made (their records are copied), and at most one catch-up step is made per request per Watch. What each replaced request cost for the days that it waited, at its own figure, is carried too (`waited_usd`), so the cost of waiting of a group is the sum of what each of its members cost for its own days, and not the whole figure of the group for the days of the oldest member. When the question itself changes (the rules changed, a different lead, Advisor delisting the recommendation), the new request starts again, and the closed word of the old one says which.
14. **"Told" means delivered.** The request page and the letter to the next person list as told only the steps that were delivered; a step that is held or failed is shown under "Not told yet" as "held: the reason" or "could not be delivered".
15. **An open request keeps the people that the list names now** (added on 2026-11-18, after the second review). The old 72-hour re-issue gave a request the approvers of that day, and nothing does now, so a person who was added to the list later could not answer the requests that were made before, and a change request whose approvers had left the list could never be answered. At each Watch (`Approvals.keep_approvers`, called by `upkeep.keep_approvers` in the step that keeps open requests up to date) each request in the state `open` gets the people that the rule of its creation names (`Extras.approvers`: the role `approver`): the people who are added can answer it, the people who are gone cannot, the owners are the floor when nobody has the role, and a request is never left with nobody (when the list names nobody at all it keeps its people). The approvers are part of the digest, so a request that changes is sealed again, with a new code (a button on an older card or letter then says that the request changed, and the page has the new one); a request that was never sealed stays unsealed and so cannot be approved by this step; a confirmation of the first of two people on a change does not count after the change, as for any other change of the request. Only a request that is open is touched, read again before it is written: not a held one, not one that a person answered or that is being changed. A list that names the same people writes nothing, and a second Watch changes nothing. The History says it in one line of counts and only when something changed (`plan.approvers`: requests, people added, people dropped; no names). The people who are added are kept on the request (a record of the ladder of the kind `added`), and the next step tells them too, whatever its source, as one of the people the request was made for and with one plain line: "You were added to the people who answer this request after it was made, so this is the first time that I write to you about it." The pages list them as "added to the people of the request", not as the manager. This changes who may open the page and answer, nothing about what an answer may do: a change is still made with the approver's own Azure rights at the click ([rule 29](../../AGENTS.md)). A step that goes to several people marks each of them from the newest version of its record (before, the second person's mark could erase the first's, and the first would have been written to again a day later).

## The one exception to the closed list of letters

A letter goes only to a person on the list ([rule 26 (c)](../../AGENTS.md)). The next person on a ladder is often not on it, so there is one exception, and only one: `Mailer.send_escalation` for the two kinds `escalation` and `escalation-weekly`. It takes a target that the directory module made (a person of the list, or a vetted profile), checks the kind, the shape of the address and of the object id, and the deny list again, records "Step N, source: …" with the letter, and refuses everything else. The Graph reads that find the person are the two calls of `http.directory_calls()` and nothing else: `GET /v1.0/users/{id or address}` and `GET /v1.0/users/{id}/manager`, host `graph.microsoft.com`, no list, no group, no write; the drift check probes that list like the others (eleven probes).

## What Entra showed (a read-only probe, 2026-11-17, counts only)

The owner signed in as a guest of the estate tenant. His own manager was not found (404). The first page of 100 users held only Members, and more pages exist. Of 20 Members, none had a manager set (404 each). In this tenant the manager is usually not filled in, so the contacts are the path that works, and the manager is a bonus when a tenant fills it in: which is why `ESCALATE_TO` starts at `contacts`. No name, address or id was printed or stored.

## The Graph permission, and how a person gives it

Reading a profile or a manager from the app needs the Microsoft Graph application permission **`User.Read.All`** (Read all users' full profiles; admin consent required; app role id `df021288-bdef-4463-88db-98f22de89214`, read on 2026-11-17 in the [permissions reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/graph/permissions-reference)). The app has none today, and nothing here grants it (rule 29: a change in Azure or Entra is made by a person with their own rights). It is requirement T4 in [`environment/access-requirements.json`](../../environment/access-requirements.json), and the table in [access and onboarding](03-access-and-onboarding.md) shows it. The steps, for a person who holds the Microsoft Entra role Privileged Role Administrator or Global Administrator:

1. Take the principal id that the template prints as `casesSystemPrincipalId` (the system-assigned identity of the cases app).
2. Find the service principal of Microsoft Graph (application id `00000003-0000-0000-c000-000000000000`) and its app role `User.Read.All`.
3. Make one app role assignment from the Graph service principal to that principal, for that app role. [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/entra/identity/managed-identities-azure-resources/assign-app-role-managed-identity-powershell) has the PowerShell steps (and [the same for the Azure CLI](https://learn.microsoft.com/entra/identity/managed-identities-azure-resources/assign-app-role-managed-identity-azure-cli)); it needs the consent of an administrator.
4. Set `escalateTo` to `managers` in the deployment, or leave `contacts`. The first lookup that works shows in the next weekly letter, which no longer lists a missing permission.

Only two things need the permission: `ESCALATE_TO=managers`, and an address in an escalation tag of a person who is not on the list of people. Without it each says "no permission yet" in plain words and the request goes on to the escalation contacts.

## What is not proved

- **No manager was found in the estate tenant** (0 of 20 Members), so nothing proves that a real manager is found, vetted and written to. The code is tested with a fake directory.
- **The Graph permission is not granted**, and Learn is not consistent about it: the page "List manager" lists `User.Read.All` as the least privileged delegated permission and shows no application permission, while the page for the same route for agent users lists the application permission `User.Read.All`, and the permission is widely used that way. Only the first read after the grant settles it. If it is refused, the lookup says "no permission yet" and the contacts are asked.
- **No real escalation was sent**, to anybody. Every test used invented people, a fake clock, and a fake directory and mail sender.
- **A person who is not on the list of people cannot open the page**: the web app knows only the people of the list, and its denied page says nothing on purpose. A manager from the directory is in that position until the owner adds them to the list with the role `escalation`. Their letter says so, has no link, and asks them to tell one of the contacts named in its footer what they decide: so they are told and the request is visible, but they cannot answer in the app.
- **Teams cards do not carry escalation**, and a request that Meternaut makes again because the question changed (the rules, the lead) starts its wait again; one made again for the same finding keeps it (item 13).
- **The check for a gone resource was tested against a fake Resource Graph.** Which types the real service lists is not proved: a type that it does not list is never judged gone (it is reported as not checked), and a resource of a type that the subscription no longer lists at all, outside the types of the detectors, keeps waiting until its resource group is gone or a person answers.
- **A change of the list of people was tested against fakes only** (item 15). Nobody was added to a real list, and the sealing again of every open request at once, when the list changes, was not tried on the real table: a card or letter that was sent before carries the old code, and its button says that the request changed until the next message brings the new one. An approver whose account left the directory is dropped only when the owner takes them off the list: Meternaut reads no directory for this.
- Nothing was run in Azure; the real size of the stored records, the run time of a round with many waiting requests, and the delivery of a letter to an address outside the list are untried.
