# Escalation: a request to a person never times out and never becomes invisible — a decision record

**Status: decided on 2026-11-17 (the owner's rule, [AGENTS.md rule 38](../../AGENTS.md)); built and tested on this machine against fakes and a fake clock; not deployed and not run in the Hub. What is not proved is at the end.**

## Why

Before this, a request to a person (a plan of any family: advice, or a change) lived 72 hours (`PLAN_TTL_HOURS`). When nobody answered, it was closed as `expired`, its case went to `expired`, the Savings page filed it under "Declined or expired", and nothing reminded anybody. A finding that nobody answered faded. The owner's words, 2026-11-17: "Requests should not time out, rather they need to be escalated and should NEVER timeout and become invisible. ... To escalate to the manager: check Entra ID if you can find a manager or something. NEVER SILENTLY IGNORE!!!!!" What Advisor and Cost Management show today is ignored, and the money is wasted: Meternaut exists to be answered.

## What was decided

1. **No clock closes a request.** The time to live is gone (`ApprovalSettings.ttl_hours`, `Approvals.expire`, the setting `PLAN_TTL_HOURS` and the template parameter `planTtlHours`). A new plan has no end time. The stored word `expired` now means only "taken back by Meternaut, with a reason" (`brief.TAKEN_BACK`), and every page, letter and card says "Taken back" and the reason. No page says "open until". A request ends by a person's answer, by a change that was made or refused, or by Meternaut taking it back in words. A change plan of any age is still read again at the click: the runner re-reads the resource and its locks after the sign-in and refuses a change that no longer holds, which opens the request again with the reason.
2. **The requests that the old clock closed come back.** At each Watch, for each resource, the newest request that the clock closed (`expired`, no reason, and an end that is the start plus a whole number of hours from 1 to 336) and that nobody answered is opened again, and its case goes back to what it was before. The History says `plan.revived`. A resource that has an open request, a request that a person answered, a case that moved on, or a request that Meternaut took back with a reason is left alone, and a second run changes nothing. The plan's digest still holds, because revival changes only its state.
3. **The ladder** ([`cases/escalation.py`](../../src/case-functions/cases/escalation.py)), counted from the start of the wait (`waiting_since`, else the time the request was made; item 13), one step at a time and at least a day apart, so that a request that has waited long is caught up one step a day, never in a burst. Every read is for one request (`escalations_of(plan_id)`): there is no list of the newest rows, because the table store answers the first rows in key order, and a request past the first thousand would show no steps and never move:

   | Step | Age | Who |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | 3 days | A reminder to the people that it was made for |
   | 2 | 7 days | The next person (below) |
   | 3 | 14 days | The next level: the manager of that manager when there is one, else the escalation contacts again |
   | 4 | 21 days | The escalation contacts |
   | 5 and on | every 7 days | The contacts and the people of the chain together, for as long as it waits |

   Each step is one record (table `mnescalations`, claimed once, so a second run or a second instance makes none again): who, by which source, what could not be looked up on the way, and what became of the message. A person who puts the request off ("later") delays the next step by 7 days, the first three times only: a put-off never silences. "Not mine" on a request of advice keeps it open and makes the next step due at once.
4. **Who is next** ([`cases/directory.py`](../../src/case-functions/cases/directory.py)), in this order, each reported by a closed word: the manager that Microsoft Entra names (when `ESCALATE_TO` is `managers`; then the manager's manager); an address in the tag `escalation` on the resource, its group or its subscription; the people with the role `escalation` in `CONSOLE_PEOPLE`; and, when none of these is found, all the owners, with the plain note "No escalation contact is set": a request is never dropped and never sent nowhere. A lookup that fails is not an error but a sentence in the message ("I could not look up the manager (no permission yet)", "No manager is set for the person in the directory", and so on), and the next source is tried. A person from the directory is a target only when they are a Member of the tenant, their account is enabled, they have a mail address, and the address is not on `ESCALATION_DENY`; their name is cleaned, cut and shown escaped, and nothing else of the profile is kept.
5. **Delivery is by letter only.** Teams cards are not used for escalation. A person gets at most one escalation letter a day, a list when several requests have come to them, and a person who never heard from Meternaut is told who writes and why. A switch that is pulled, a pause, quiet hours and the one-letter-a-day rule hold a step, and a held step is kept, shown ("held: the reason") and sent when the hold ends; a failed send is tried again every 15 minutes, 12 times, and then once a day. Held means delayed and visible, never lost. The weekly budget of advice messages does not count escalation letters.
6. **The weekly letter never stops.** For as long as a request has waited more than a week, the escalation contacts get one letter every week of everything that still waits (oldest first, ten lines at most, then "and N more"), and when something stops the ladder (a Graph permission that is missing, a message that cannot be delivered) the owners get it too, with that problem named once.
7. **Waiting is shown with its cost.** A request's page has a block "Waiting" (since when, with whom it is now, who was told and when, how often it was put off, and what waiting has cost: the figure of a month over 30 days for the days waited, "about" for a scenario, nothing without a measured saving). The start page has "Waiting the longest". The Savings page has the rows "Declined" and "Taken back" instead of "Declined or expired", and says how long the longest request has waited.
8. **The settings.** `ESCALATE_TO`: `contacts` (the default) or `managers`; `off` is refused with a plain message, because a request that nobody answers is always passed on. The Hub variable `ESCALATE_TO` carries it (`tools/environment-params.py` refuses any other word and writes `escalateTo`; the template sets the setting), so the grant step "set `escalateTo`" is real. The others are read with their defaults: `ESCALATION_DAYS` (four increasing whole numbers, `3,7,14,21`), `ESCALATION_REPEAT_DAYS` (7), `ESCALATION_TAG` (`escalation`), `ESCALATION_DENY` (addresses or `@domains` that are never written to), `ESCALATION_SNOOZES` (3) and `ESCALATION_SNOOZE_DAYS` (7). The ladder runs only when `MAIL_SENDER` is set, because it sends letters. Without it the start page, the Savings page and the request page say "Escalation is not running: no mail sender is set up, so a request that nobody answers is not passed on", and every Watch writes one History line (`escalation.off`): it is never silent.
9. **Who may answer.** A person to whom a step of the ladder of a request of advice was delivered may answer that advice, whatever their role on the list (the contacts, the owners, an approver who is not named on it): one rule for the decider and the pages (`models.may_answer`), and a step that was only meant for them, but not delivered, does not count. They have every answer of advice (worth acting on, not worth it, not mine, a question, decide later) and none of a change: a change keeps its named approvers, and the approval and the sign-in to Azure stay with them ([rule 29](../../AGENTS.md)).
10. **A held finding does not wait.** A finding that a guard holds back is the state `held` ([proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)): nobody is asked about it, so the ladder makes no step for it, its page has no "Waiting" block, the start page and the Savings page do not count it among the requests that wait, and no clock closes it. The pages keep three words apart: *open* (a request that waits, and is passed on), *held back* (nobody is asked) and *taken back* (Meternaut closed the request and says why; a held finding that is released or left alone is closed the same way). When a held finding is asked about as any other, the request starts its wait then. (Since 2026-11-18 an automation that manages a resource is no guard but a warning on an ordinary request, and the held requests of that old reason become ordinary open requests in place, keeping their start as `waiting_since`: the ladder counts from there, one step a day.)
11. **Places for requests are freed by answers only, and there are 30.** The places for the findings that the AIs did not settle (`ADVICE_OPEN_MAX`, [proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)) used to be ten and were freed by the 72-hour clock. Now only an answer, or Meternaut taking a request back and saying why (item 12 is the one that works without a person), frees one: a request that is passed on keeps its place, and the findings that no place is free for wait as held ("waiting for a free place") until one is free. There are 30 places since 2026-11-18, because the 12 requests that the old clock closed in the last 14 days (of 92 made) are opened again, and ten places could be filled by them and hold the fresh, dearest findings; one person is asked at most one letter a day and three cards a week, and every list ranks by the figure, so 30 do not flood. A finding that is worth more than the smallest of the open requests is not held behind them, up to a ceiling of 45 open requests (`ADVICE_OPEN_HARD`): beyond it nothing is asked about, whatever it is worth, nothing that is open is displaced, and the dearest of what waits takes a place when an answer frees one. A request that was held and is made an open request keeps its plan code, so the pages and cards that were made for it still work, and the days that it waited while nobody was asked count from its first making (`waiting_since`); the ladder gives it one step a round at most, a day apart, so an old request does not get several steps at once.
12. **A request about a resource that is gone is taken back.** With no clock, nothing else ends a request whose finding stopped being true. At each Watch (after the revival) the open requests are checked against Resource Graph (`escalation.existence`: fixed queries that name only ids of the shape of an id). A request is taken back with the word `gone` (the pages say "Taken back: the resource is gone") only when it is certain: all the reads worked, the Watch read the subscription in full (no source failed, the recent creations and the managed groups were read), the subscription answered, and either the resource group is not there, or the resource is not there and Resource Graph lists its type (the types of the detectors, or one that the subscription lists now: a type that it does not list would look gone for ever, and is reported as not checked). A partial, failed or throttled read judges nothing: the request keeps waiting, and the History says how many were not checked and why. **Two limits are accepted.** Only the Watch takes a request back: the timer never does, because it has no full read of the subscription, so a request about a resource that is gone waits for the next Watch, and the ladder goes on meanwhile. And a resource that moved (to another resource group or another subscription) is not at its old id, so its request counts as gone and is taken back; the Watch makes a new request for it at its new id, and that request starts its wait anew.
13. **A request made again keeps its wait.** When a request of the same finding replaces another (the request of a group that replaces the requests of its resources, or a fresher request for the same resource and the same people), the one that stays carries the oldest start (`waiting_since`: the ladder, the cost of waiting and the pages use it), the steps already made for the old request count as made (their records are copied), and at most one catch-up step is made per request per Watch. What each replaced request cost for the days that it waited, at its own figure, is carried too (`waited_usd`), so the cost of waiting of a group is the sum of what each of its members cost for its own days, and not the whole figure of the group for the days of the oldest member. When the question itself changes (the rules changed, a different lead, Advisor delisting the recommendation), the new request starts again, and the closed word of the old one says which.
14. **"Told" means delivered.** The request page and the letter to the next person list as told only the steps that were delivered; a step that is held or failed is shown under "Not told yet" as "held: the reason" or "could not be delivered".
15. **An open request keeps the people that the list names now** (added on 2026-11-18, after the second review). The old 72-hour re-issue gave a request the approvers of that day, and nothing does now, so a person who was added to the list later could not answer the requests that were made before, and a change request whose approvers had left the list could never be answered. At each Watch (`Approvals.keep_approvers`, called by `upkeep.keep_approvers` in the step that keeps open requests up to date) each request in the state `open` gets the people that the rule of its creation names (`Extras.approvers`: the role `approver`): the people who are added can answer it, the people who are gone cannot, the owners are the floor when nobody has the role, and a request is never left with nobody (when the list names nobody at all it keeps its people). The approvers are part of the digest, so a request that changes is sealed again, with a new code (a change is bound to its digest, so its code is the new one: a button on an older card of a change then says that the request changed and leads to its page, as for any stale button; **advice keeps its code**, because it changes nothing in Azure and nothing checks its digest, so a card or a letter that was sent before keeps working); a request that was never sealed stays unsealed and so cannot be approved by this step; a confirmation of the first of two people on a change does not count after the change, as for any other change of the request. Only a request that is open is touched, read again before it is written: not a held one, not one that a person answered or that is being changed. A list that names the same people writes nothing, and a second Watch changes nothing. The History says it in one line of counts and only when something changed (`plan.approvers`: requests, people added, people dropped; no names). The people who are added are kept on the request (a record of the ladder of the kind `added`), and the next step tells them too, whatever its source, as one of the people the request was made for and with one plain line: "You were added to the people who answer this request after it was made." (it does not say that this is the first message: an approver who chose Teams may have had a card already). The pages list them as "added to the people of the request", not as the manager. This changes who may open the page and answer, nothing about what an answer may do: a change is still made with the approver's own Azure rights at the click ([rule 29](../../AGENTS.md)). A step that goes to several people marks each of them from the newest version of its record (before, the second person's mark could erase the first's, and the first would have been written to again a day later).
16. **A round goes on from a cursor** (the final review). The 25 requests of a round were picked by the minute of the clock modulo the number of windows, so rounds spaced by a multiple of that number never reached some windows, and the timer skips a round when another runs long. Now a round takes the next 25 open requests after a cursor, oldest wait first, and goes round again to the oldest: the cursor is the key of the request that the last round ended with, kept in the store (one row of the table of the ladder), written before the work, so that every request is looked at within ceil(N / 25) rounds whatever the spacing and whatever a restart does. A request that has gone since changes nothing; an empty list moves nothing. The store has the say: a round reads the cursor from the store. A store that cannot read or write it does not send every round back to the oldest 25 (with 60 requests and ten rounds only 25 were looked at, and nobody knew): the round goes on from the cursor that the process kept, when the read fails or when the last write failed (the cursor that the store did not take is newer than the one that it has), and writes one History line of counts (`escalation.cursor`: how many reads and writes failed). The process keeps it in a dict of the module for each storage account (`escalation._MEMORY`, as `loop._teams_off_said` is kept), because the timer builds a new hook, and so a new ladder, every minute (`loop.tick` calls `wiring.build_extras`): the first version of this fallback kept it in the hook, so it kept nothing from one tick to the next, which a test that reused one hook did not see; a test now goes through the real `loop.tick` three times with a cursor that cannot be read, cannot be written, or neither. While 25 requests or fewer wait no cursor is read, so none can fail and none is said. A restart of the process loses the memory, and the next round then starts at the oldest request once while the store still cannot be read. **That the Functions worker keeps a module between two calls of the timer is not proved**: if it does not, the memory stands in for nothing and only the cursor of the store works, which is why the store stays the primary.
17. **The lists of requests are read whole, up to a cap** (the final review). `plans_open` read the first 1,000 rows in key order; no clock bounds the set now, so past 1,000 open requests the ladder, the lists and the letters saw an arbitrary 1,000. The plan lists that correctness depends on (open, held, recent, expired) follow the continuation of the table service up to 10,000 rows. When the service still has more at the cap, the store says which list it cut (`truncated`), the Watch writes one line for it (`plans.truncated`, counts only) and the start page says in one sentence that some open requests are not listed. The test fake of the table store pages like the real one (1,000 rows a page, continuation headers, key order, and a page that is empty and carries a continuation). Inside a Watch (`TableStore.reading`, opened by `run_watch`) each list is read once and kept right by the writes of that store, because the Watch asks the open and the recent lists for each case (`Approvals.advise` and `create`) and 2,500 rows cost three reads of a thousand each time: ten cases cost four reads, not forty. What another process writes during a Watch (a person who answers a request) is not seen by that Watch; the steps that write a plan read it again first.
18. **The digest counts requests.** Two steps of one request that wait for one person (held by quiet hours, say) are one decision in one letter, about the newest step, and both are marked told.
19. **The pages list what the ladder came to from an index, and the gate asks the ladder** (the final delta review). Item 9 lets a person who is not named on a request answer the advice that a step was delivered to them for; to know which requests to list for them, the pages read the ladder of every request, so a person with 40 open requests cost 40 reads on the start page and 40 on the History, and no clock closes a request any more. Now the round of the ladder keeps an index of who a step was delivered to (`Store.told_get`, `told_put`, `told_delete`: a row for each person and request, in the partition `told-` and the person's object id of the table of the ladder, with when it was last delivered), from the records of the requests that it looks at: a row is written when it is missing (a step that was just delivered, within a minute, or at the request's turn when more than 25 wait; a request that carries the steps of the one that it stands for; a write that failed) or older than the last step that was delivered to the person for that request (so it says when they were last told, not when they were first), and only for a person of the list (a manager of the directory cannot sign in to use a row). A row of a request that is not open and was last delivered more than 45 days ago is removed when the round meets the person: the people that the steps of its requests were delivered to, and one more person of the list in each round, chosen by the minute of the clock (so no memory is needed), so that the rows of a person who is told nothing any more go too, within as many minutes as the list has people. Nothing is removed while the list of open requests is cut at its cap, because it cannot then say which requests have ended. A page reads the index once (`Ctx.told`), whatever the number of requests, and lists with it; **when it cannot read it, the page says so** (`Ctx.told_failed`): the start page with the sentence that it has for any list that it cannot read ("I cannot read the open requests now. Nothing has changed.", and the same for the recent decisions), the History with its sentence for the requests ("I cannot read the requests now. Nothing has changed.") and its tiles marked as not readable, and not with "Nothing waits for you". The start page then shows no list at all, also for the requests that the person is named on (half a list under such a sentence would mislead), and the History shows the lines that it read with its tiles marked; the page of each request, the letters and the ladder are not affected. A person who is named on every request does not read the index at all, so the page cannot tell them so. **The index only says what to list.** `models.may_answer` is still the one rule, and goes by `delivered`, per request, for advice only; the decider and the page of one request (`web.common.may_open`, the notebook page too) ask the ladder of that one request, so a wrong row (a stale one, one for a step that was held, one for another request) shows a request in a list and cannot open it, and a person who is not on the list of people has no principal and is refused before any of this. An ops-only person told by a step opens the advice and answers it, and never a change: the change page, approve, decline and confirm answer 403 and no sign-in to Azure starts.
20. **A request ends when the bill shows what it suggested** (added on 2026-11-18). With no clock, a person who did what a request of advice suggests and never answered it would be reminded and passed on for ever. At each Watch, after the daily cost of the open requests is read (`read_cost_series`: nothing is read twice, no model takes part and no new kind of call is made), [`cases/verify.py`](../../src/case-functions/cases/verify.py) holds each open request of advice against it, and takes back the ones whose cost has gone down and stayed down. It judges only what it can hold the bill against. **The finding** has a saving of its own: a figure that code worked out from the bill (the typed change that the finding states, priced from the bill lines that it names, or the cost of a resource that it asks to delete). A finding that is Azure Advisor's own estimate, which no cost line backs (`brief.advisor_estimate`), a recommendation of Advisor, a finding with no figure and a finding about a whole subscription or resource group are never verified and nothing is said of them; a request of a change is never verified either. A request of a group is judged by the sum of its resources' daily cost, read when the case names every one of them, and only while the group names the same resources as when its cost was kept (`COST_OF`, a short digest of their ids): a finding that is found again with other resources has another cost to be held against. Otherwise it is never verified. **The resource** is still in Azure by the check of item 12 in the same Watch: one that is gone ends as `gone`, and one that was not checked is left alone. **The bill** was read whole up to yesterday, in US dollars: a read that failed or was partial leaves the series that ended before, which is never used, and the History says how many requests it could not judge and why (once for each reason in a Watch: `the daily cost was not read in full up to yesterday`, `the cost lines are not in US dollars`). **A commitment** (a reservation, a savings plan) that covers a resource makes its actual cost fall to nothing while the finding is as true as it was ([Learn, Understand Cost Management data](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/understand-cost-mgt-data)), so a request whose actual cost shows a fall is held against the **amortized** cost of the same days as well, which spreads a purchase over its term. It is read for those requests only (`run._amortized`, in the same queries by subscription and 50 resources as the actual cost, with the same handling of "too many requests"): the fall has to be there in both, and the request keeps the dearer of the two, which is the smaller fall. A request whose amortized cost was not read (refused, throttled, not in dollars) verifies nothing in that Watch, is tried again in the next one, and the History says so (`the amortized cost could not be read`). A request that carries the warning that something that is no person changed the resource lately (`automated`, [proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)) is never verified either: the bill cannot tell what that did from what a person did, and its case is not even read.
    The 30 closed days are cut into four weeks that end two days before the newest day, because the bill is one to two days late, and each week is scaled to a month (30/7 of its seven days). **A week with a day of nothing among days that cost something is not whole**: a day that is late or missing in the bill cannot be told from a fall, so that week says nothing (a week of nothing at all is a week that cost nothing, as for what was stopped). **The first Watch that sees the request open with a whole daily cost keeps, once, what a month cost by the cheapest of the four weeks, and how far apart the weeks were** (`COST_AT_ASK` and `COST_SPREAD`, the dearest less the cheapest, in the preconditions of the request; it keeps nothing until all four weeks are whole and cost something, because fewer measure badly how far a cost moves by itself; a sealed request is sealed again and, being advice, keeps its code, as in item 15). It is not the cost of the 30 days: a spike of a week in the month before the request would raise that one, and a cost that fell before the request would hide in it. The request is judged from the next Watch on. **It is verified when each of the two newest whole weeks costs at most that, less what the request states that it would save, and less the spread**; `verify.fall` is the one place that says, for a resource and for a group alike. The saving is the larger of the request's and the case's, never more than the cost (a saving above it asks for the whole cost); a request about a resource needs half of it, and a request about a group all of it, for it asks about each of its resources. The spread is there because a cost that varies with use moves by itself: a fall that is no bigger than the weeks before it moved would close a request now and then by chance, for as long as the request lives. A fall in one week only, or for a few days, is no fall, so a request that was acted on is closed about two and a half weeks after the action at the earliest (two weeks of bill, two late days), and the ladder goes on until then.
    `Approvals.take_back_verified` ends the request, as `expired` with `takenBack = verified` and the dearer of the two weeks of the two series (`COST_SEEN`), after it reads the request again: **a request that a person answered in any way (a question, "later", "not mine") is theirs and stays**, even if the cost has gone down. The case goes to `expired` like any request that Meternaut took back, and not to `verified`: that status is for a change that was made and that Cost Management confirmed ("Done and verified", added up in "Confirmed by Cost Management"). The History says `case.verified` (`closed: the cost has gone down as the request suggested`, with no amount). The ladder looks at open requests only, so it makes no step, tries no failed step again and lists nothing in its weekly letter; `escalation.revive` opens again only what the clock closed (a request with a reason is not that, whatever its hours), so a closed request is not opened again; no card or letter is made for it, and a card that was posted is replaced by one that says the words below and has no figure.
    What a person reads, on the page of the request, in the card and in the History: "Closed because the cost went down. The cost of this has gone down since the request was made, as it suggested. Meternaut closed the request. Nothing was changed by Meternaut." (not "in Azure": somebody changed something there). The figures are on the page of the request only: what its cheapest week cost, what the dearer of the last two weeks cost, and the saving that the request stated. The start page lists it under "Recently decided" with the word "Cost went down", the History with the word "Closed" (each with a link, not folded into the line "requests to look at a finding" that counts the other requests that Meternaut took back). Both lists take the requests that the bill closed from the store by the time of their closing (`web.common.closed_by_the_bill`), however long ago they were made: a request is closed two weeks after it was made at the earliest and none times out, so a list of the requests that were made lately (the 45 days of the History, the newest twelve of the start page) would miss many of them, and nobody gets a letter for it. The History keeps only those that were closed inside its window of 30 days, as its lines are, so that old closings do not take the places of the answers of the last days in the 40 requests that it reads. When they cannot be read the pages say so, as for any list that they cannot read. Savings found has the row "Cost went down (seen on the bill)": a count and no figure, never added up (the figures are on the page of each finding), and an Advisor recommendation about the same resource is not counted beside it. **The lesson** is written once by `learning.sync` (a lesson of advice is complete when it is written) with the result `verified`, no answer (`expired`) and no measured figure. The counts that the Investigator reads (`recall_lessons`) put it with the requests that nobody answered, and the one line of a brief ("Of 4 past cases like this, 3 were approved.") counts only what people answered, so it adds to neither: the lesson says that the request was acted on, and nothing reads that yet. A finding that is found again after it was closed is a new request, with a baseline of its own.
21. **A request for a finding that nothing backs is taken back, and the ladder never chases it.** A finding of the investigation is asked about only when code priced a saving for it, something was measured of the resource's use and, for a switch to another price of the list, both AIs were sure ([proposal 9](09-the-decision-brief.md)). The open requests that were made for one that lacks them are taken back by the Watch with the reason `rules` (`Approvals.hold_back_empty`: at the Watch, and not at the minute tick), after the revival of item 2, so that a request that comes back and that nothing backs is taken back in the same Watch. These are a case with no saving and a plan that states none (a request that was asked with a saving stays open when its finding is judged again and no price is found, and the case keeps that figure and its price), and a case that nothing measured or whose switch the AIs were not both sure of, when the plan has no `preconditions["askedUnder"]`: a request made or opened under today's rules keeps it and is never taken back for them, whatever a later judgment of its finding says. The ladder reads the open requests only, so it makes no step for a request that was taken back. A request that a person answered in any way is never taken back. The page says "Taken back" and why, and the History has one line for each request (`held back: Meternaut's rules changed`) and one count for the Watch. Not proved: the take-back of the real requests that the owner holds today; it was tested against fakes only.
## The one exception to the closed list of letters

A letter goes only to a person on the list ([rule 26 (c)](../../AGENTS.md)). The next person on a ladder is often not on it, so there is one exception, and only one: `Mailer.send_escalation` for the two kinds `escalation` and `escalation-weekly`. It takes a target that the directory module made (a person of the list, or a vetted profile), checks the kind, the shape of the address and of the object id, and the deny list again, records "Step N, source: …" with the letter, and refuses everything else. The Graph reads that find the person are the two calls of `http.directory_calls()` and nothing else: `GET /v1.0/users/{id or address}` and `GET /v1.0/users/{id}/manager`, host `graph.microsoft.com`, no list, no group, no write; the drift check probes that list like the others (eleven probes).

## What Entra showed (a read-only probe, 2026-11-17, counts only)

The owner signed in as a guest of the estate tenant. His own manager was not found (404). The first page of 100 users held only Members, and more pages exist. Of 20 Members, none had a manager set (404 each). In this tenant the manager is usually not filled in, so the contacts are the path that works, and the manager is a bonus when a tenant fills it in: which is why `ESCALATE_TO` starts at `contacts`. No name, address or id was printed or stored.

## The Graph permission, and how a person gives it

Reading a profile or a manager from the app needs the Microsoft Graph application permission **`User.Read.All`** (Read all users' full profiles; admin consent required; app role id `df021288-bdef-4463-88db-98f22de89214`, read on 2026-11-17 in the [permissions reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/graph/permissions-reference)). The app has none today, and nothing here grants it (rule 29: a change in Azure or Entra is made by a person with their own rights). It is requirement T4 in [`environment/access-requirements.json`](../../environment/access-requirements.json), and the table in [access and onboarding](03-access-and-onboarding.md) shows it. The steps, for a person who holds the Microsoft Entra role Privileged Role Administrator or Global Administrator:

1. Take the principal id that the template prints as `casesSystemPrincipalId` (the system-assigned identity of the cases app).
2. Find the service principal of Microsoft Graph (application id `00000003-0000-0000-c000-000000000000`) and its app role `User.Read.All`.
3. Make one app role assignment from the Graph service principal to that principal, for that app role. [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/entra/identity/managed-identities-azure-resources/assign-app-role-managed-identity-powershell) has the PowerShell steps (and [the same for the Azure CLI](https://learn.microsoft.com/entra/identity/managed-identities-azure-resources/assign-app-role-managed-identity-azure-cli)); it needs the consent of an administrator.
4. Set `escalateTo` to `managers` in the deployment, or leave `contacts`. The first lookup that works shows in the next weekly letter, which no longer lists a missing permission.

Only two things need the permission: `ESCALATE_TO=managers`, and an address in an escalation tag of a person who is not on the list of people. Without it each says "no permission yet" in plain words and the request goes on to the escalation contacts.

## What is not proved

- **No manager was found in the estate tenant** (0 of 20 Members), so nothing proves that a real manager is found, vetted and written to. The code is tested with a fake directory.
- **The Graph permission is not granted**, and Learn is not consistent about it: the page "List manager" lists `User.Read.All` as the least privileged delegated permission and shows no application permission, while the page for the same route for agent users lists the application permission `User.Read.All`, and the permission is widely used that way. Only the first read after the grant settles it. If it is refused, the lookup says "no permission yet" and the contacts are asked.
- **No real escalation was sent**, to anybody. Every test used invented people, a fake clock, and a fake directory and mail sender.
- **A person who is not on the list of people cannot open the page**: the web app knows only the people of the list, and its denied page says nothing on purpose. A manager from the directory is in that position until the owner adds them to the list with the role `escalation`. Their letter says so, has no link, and asks them to tell one of the contacts named in its footer what they decide: so they are told and the request is visible, but they cannot answer in the app.
- **Teams cards do not carry escalation**, and a request that Meternaut makes again because the question changed (the rules, the lead) starts its wait again; one made again for the same finding keeps it (item 13).
- **The check for a gone resource was tested against a fake Resource Graph.** Which types the real service lists is not proved: a type that it does not list is never judged gone (it is reported as not checked), and a resource of a type that the subscription no longer lists at all, outside the types of the detectors, keeps waiting until its resource group is gone or a person answers.
- **A card of a change that was posted before its people changed is not offered again** (item 15). Advice keeps its code, so its cards go on working; a change gets a new code, and the buttons of its old card say that the request changed and lead to its page, while the ladder's letters bring the new code. Offering a new card for such a change, and replacing the old one, is not built: the closing of a card in `offers.py` is for a request that has ended, and needs the code that the card was posted with, which the records of the cards do not keep.
- **The index of who was told** (item 19) was tested against fakes only. It follows the ladder by a minute, or by the turn of the request when more than 25 wait, so a request that was just delivered is missing from the list of a person who is not named on it for that long (the letter's own link opens it at once, because the page asks the ladder). A person whom a step was delivered to, and whose row a failed write left out, is listed again by the next round. A row of a request that is open is never removed; a row of a request that has ended goes 45 days after the last delivery, when the round meets the person (the rotation reaches each person of the list within as many minutes as the list has people, if the timer runs every minute), and the request is then no longer in that person's list of what was decided. The rows of a person who was taken off the list stay: they are small (one row for each request that the person was told about) and nothing reads them. How a partition of thousands of rows behaves on the real table, and the cost of the one read of the index for each person that a round meets and for the person of the rotation, were not measured.
- **The memory that stands in for the cursor** (item 16) is a dict of the module of the process. Nobody proved that the Functions worker keeps module state between two invocations of the timer: if it does not, a cursor row that the store cannot read or write still leaves the requests after the first 25 unlooked-at, and only the History line (`escalation.cursor`) says so. The cursor of the store is the primary and was tested against a fake table service; the memory was tested with the real `loop.tick` and a fake table service, never on a Function App, and it is lost at a restart (the round then starts at the oldest once).
- **The lists of plans that the Watch keeps for the run** (item 17) do not see what another process writes meanwhile; the writes of the Watch read the plan again first, and the step that gives open requests their people (item 15) does too, but a second Watch at the same moment could make a second request for one finding, which the held-findings step closes at its next run.
- **A change of the list of people was tested against fakes only** (item 15). Nobody was added to a real list, and the sealing again of every open request at once, when the list changes, was not tried on the real table: a card or letter that was sent before carries the old code, and its button says that the request changed until the next message brings the new one. An approver whose account left the directory is dropped only when the owner takes them off the list: Meternaut reads no directory for this.
- **A cost can fall for another reason, and the request is then closed as if somebody had acted** (item 20). Less use or a seasonal drop, a price or an agreement that changed, a part of a group that somebody else deleted, a project that ended, a commitment whose discount alone is more than the saving asks for (the amortized cost falls by the discount, and it is still a fall): the bill shows that the cost went down, never why, and the page, the lesson and the row "Cost went down (seen on the bill)" say "since the request was made" and never "because of it". The cheapest week keeps a spike or an earlier fall out of the baseline, the spread keeps a cost that moves by itself from passing for a fall, the weeks have to be whole and both have to be down, and the amortized cost has to agree; that guards against noise and against a purchase, and proves no cause. How often it still closes a request by chance was not measured against a real bill. A finding that is still true is found again at its next investigation, and asked about as a new request with a baseline of its own.
- **The bill is late** (item 20). The newest two days are left out, because a bill is a day or two behind; nothing here measured how late the bill of any service is. A bill that is later than that makes the newest of the two weeks look cheaper than it is. The older week has to be down as well, so a late bill alone does not close a request.
- **What the baseline holds** (item 20): the cheapest of the four weeks before the Watch first looked, and how far apart the weeks were. It takes all four, whole and costing something (by the simulation of a reviewer, a flat cost with a daily noise of 20 percent and a small saving passed for a fall twice as often from two weeks as from four; no real bill was used): a request has none, and is not judged, until a Watch finds them. A cost that went down in the last week or so before then is only partly in it, and can still be counted as a fall since the request; something fixed earlier shows in it and is never verified, so that request keeps waiting. A resource that is younger than four whole weeks, or whose cost has a day of nothing in some weeks (one that runs on some days only), has no baseline and keeps waiting; one that cost nothing in all four weeks is never verified. **One week of spike among the four makes the spread large for good**: a cost of 300, 900, 300 and 300 keeps a spread of 600 once, so that a real halving of it never verifies and its request keeps waiting. That is conservative and never a false claim. A request of a group is verified only when its case names every resource of the group and the same ones as when the cost was kept: a group that loses a resource because somebody fixed it is a request about what is left, and keeps waiting.
- **A request that a person answered, put off or passed on is never closed by the bill** (item 20), even when the cost has gone down: the person closes it, and until then it goes on up the ladder. Nor is a person who acts at once spared the first steps: two weeks of bill are needed, so the reminder of the third day, the next person on the seventh and the next level on the fourteenth can reach them before the request is closed.
- **The lesson of a request that the bill closed** (item 20) says that it was acted on and nothing reads that: the counts of past requests put it with those that nobody answered. Whether it should count for the confidence of a finding is not decided.
- **The pages read one more list** (item 20): the start page and the History read the list of the closed requests of the store (`plans_expired`) at every load, to find the requests that the bill closed by the day of their closing. It is at most ten calls of the table (the cap of the lists, item 17), is not kept from one load to the next, and was not measured on the real table.
- **No real run.** Item 20 was tested with a fake bill, a fake clock and a fake Resource Graph, and through the pages, the cards, the letters and the ladder with invented requests. The daily cost of a real resource over many weeks, and the late days of a real bill, were not read.
- Nothing was run in Azure; the real size of the stored records, the run time of a round with many waiting requests, and the delivery of a letter to an address outside the list are untried.
