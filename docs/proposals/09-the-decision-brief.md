# The decision brief: one page, one letter, one answer — a decision record

**What this document answers.** Why every page and letter was rebuilt around one object, the decision brief; what a person sees now; how a finding that Meternaut may not change gets an answer; what was taken out and what is not done. A change is still made by the approver, with the approver's own rights ([run as the approver](05-run-as-the-approver.md)).

## The problem

The first pages and letters showed what was easy to show: runs, cases, switches, explanations of how Meternaut works, and the same request said three times. A stranger could not tell from one page what they were asked or what would happen if they said yes. A finding about a resource that Meternaut may not change had no plan, so a person could read it and could not answer it.

## The test

Every screen and message must let a stranger, who does not know the resource, answer these eight questions in under a minute, without opening anything else:

1. What am I asked to decide?
2. What do I get?
3. How sure is it, and on what evidence?
4. What exactly happens if I say yes, who does it, and with whose rights?
5. Can it be undone?
6. What could go wrong?
7. Whose resource is it?
8. What are my choices?

Anything that helps none of the eight leaves the default view: it sits under one Details disclosure, or on a page for the platform team.

A test fails when a request cannot be decided (`tests/test_decidable.py`). For 31 kinds of request (a switch, a reduction, a removal, a schedule, a commitment, a deletion, a group, the two kinds of Azure Advisor, the six older checks, the seven families of change, and eight kinds that are asked about and state no saving) it makes the page, the Teams card and the approval letter, and asks of each in words: what is it about, what is the finding, what is asked of the reader, what would it save and what does that rest on, what did Meternaut not check, and what changes in Azure. Each check reads its own sentence, and a mutation takes out only that sentence and requires the failure. A finding with no figure is not asked about (`why_not_ask`), and the test says that too. Gaps are known and left, each named in the test with its reason: the family `change-disk-tier` states no saving, because it reads no price list, and the six older checks when the bill could not be read, and Azure Advisor's own recommendation with its figure in euros or none, are still asked about and state no saving, because the rule of asking does not reach them yet. The test also keeps every approval letter to the words that it had, except four named by 1 to 5 words.

## The decision brief

One object, made by code from a case, its plan and its action, in `src/case-functions/cases/brief.py`: pure functions, no input or output, no clock. The page, the letter and, later, the Teams card are renders of it, so the three say the same thing, and an answer is recorded once.

| Part | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Headline | A verb, an object and a place, written by code: "Delete the unattached disk disk-x in group rg-y", or "Check whether the private endpoint x is still used" when nothing measured shows that it can go. Never a model's sentence |
| Saving | One sentence of what the request is worth, with the month and the year: "Meternaut worked out that this could save about $1,682 a year ($140 a month).", and one sentence of what the figure rests on ("It is based on your bill for the last 30 days.", "It assumes ...", or Azure Advisor's own estimate). Its level (Verified, Expected or Scenario) decides the words ("saves" only for a confirmed change) and is kept in the record. Without a backed figure the page says what the finding costs ("It costs $848 a month, about $10,180 a year. Nobody has worked out what a change would save.") or why nothing can be shown ("Cost not visible", with the reason: "Azure has not reported a cost for this yet.", "Meternaut could not read what this costs just now.", "The cost is not in US dollars, so Meternaut shows no amount." or "Meternaut could not read what this costs."), and never "No figure" and never a cost of nothing for a cost that Azure does not have (below). Amounts are written in whole dollars from 100 up and with cents below, and no note says that money is in US dollars |
| Confidence | High, Medium or Low, derived by code from the second reviewer's verdict, the guards and how many cited numbers code re-checked |
| Facts | At most five rows (label, value with unit, source and period), chosen by code from the typed facts |
| How the saving was worked out | For a finding that states the change it proposes: the charges that the change would shrink as one fact, the figure and the words of what it rests on, and the AI's own words for a person in a quote that says "What the AI says. Meternaut did not check the wording." (below, and [proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md)) |
| Group | For a finding that asks for a look at many resources: one case and one request. The headline says how many, of what kind and in how many groups, and names the first; the rows say what they cover, what they cost together and which they are (below) |
| Reasoning, doubt | The first AI's reason and the second AI's strongest objection, each in a labelled quote ("What the AI says. Meternaut did not check the wording." and "What the second AI doubts."). Between them one short paragraph says why Meternaut is sure or not sure: how sure the first AI is, whether the second AI doubted it, and how many of the numbers that the AI quoted Meternaut checked against Azure |
| Change | What changes, who makes it (the approver, with their own permissions, so Azure checks their rights) and what is read again just before |
| Undo, risk | Whether it can be undone and how; one sentence of what could go wrong, with a level shown as a chip. For advice the page has one short paragraph instead of the four blocks ("Meternaut changes nothing in Azure. Your answer is recorded with your name. If you decide to act, you do it yourself in Azure with your own rights."), and, when the AI named a risk, a sentence about doing what the title suggests, derived from the kind of resource and the evidence; answering changes nothing in Azure and says so in its own chip |
| Check first | For advice about a resource that others share, or that may not be used any more: what it connects to, who owns it and its traffic (the page has three lines, a card and a letter one) |
| Owner | Who the resource probably belongs to, as plain facts from the tags and the Activity Log. A missing owner is said |
| Where, age | Group, kind of resource in words, the subscription by its name, one link to the Azure portal (web page only) and one to Cost analysis for the scope of the finding (web page only); "Found 3 days ago", and for a request that has waited a block "Waiting" (since when, with whom, what waiting costs), with no urgency |
| Answers, footer | The choices that exist now for this person; one footer line: an AI wrote it and which models, why this person got it, whom to talk to (a name and an address as text) |

Text from the estate, from a tag, from a person or from a model passes one function, `plain()`, in every render: one line, no hidden or direction character, no Markdown link, no address with a scheme (it is split from its slashes), and a length limit. A link is made from an id, never from text. A mail or chat client may still turn a bare host name in such text into a link: that is a known limit, and the one link that matters, to the page of the decision, is made from an id. Tests put the worst texts into every field of every page, letter and card.

## What a person sees

- **Decisions**, the start page. "Waiting for you (N)": the larger saving first, then the oldest; each row has the headline, saving and level, risk, age and one button, Open. Below it, five one-line rows of what was decided lately.
- **A decision** (`/ok/<id>`; `/why/<id>` shows the same page). The headline, one sentence of saving, the facts, the two boxes of AI words, change, undo and risk, the owner, and one answer form. Everything else (steps, models, tokens, guards, ids, the log of the action) sits in one collapsed Details.
- **History**, "What happened": 30 days of decisions, changes made and checked, and the number of new findings each day. The old addresses of My decisions and What I did here answer with it.
- **Settings:** channel, quiet hours (from, until and the time zone that they are read in; none by default), the most messages a week, pause. **Operations**, for the platform team: the switches, the runs and cases, the permissions, and the rule for [changes that need two people](04-the-loop.md#going-through-the-requests-quiet-hours-and-two-people); moved from the start page, not extended.
- **Go through the requests.** With two or more requests waiting, the Decisions page has the link "Go through the N requests one by one". Each request is its own page with its own answer, and after an answer the page of the result offers "Next request". No button answers for several.
- **A request that two people approve.** The page, the letter and the card say "Two people approve this: one confirms the request, then another approves it." The page also says where the request stands for its reader: the first person is offered "Confirm this request" (which changes nothing) and the others' buttons as usual; the second is told who confirmed and when, and is offered the usual approval; the person who confirmed can decline or say that it is not theirs, and cannot approve; a confirmation of an older form of the request is said not to count. A request that fewer than two people can approve says so and offers no approval. The Decisions page has a chip that says where it stands, and the History shows "the first of two people" and "the second of two people". A card never confirms or approves by itself: its buttons open the page that sends the answer, and the first of the two people has "Confirm".
- **Letters.** The decision letter (about 160 words): the title and the kind and group under it, the risk, the confidence and the undo as words, the figure a year and a month with its level and its basis (and once that money is in US dollars), the top facts, change, undo and risk in a line each, the footer (one sentence that an AI wrote it, why the person got it and who answers for Meternaut, in about twenty words). The result letter: what was done, what it saved so far, how to undo it. The weekly letter: the decisions that wait, counted and grouped as the Decisions page does it (findings of one kind about several resources are one line, and the number in the subject is the page's number), at most five lines, one for each decision with its year figure and level and no total, sent only when something waits. The first letter: three sentences. Each has one link, to the page of the decision (or the start page), no resource name or amount in its subject, and a plain-text copy that is ASCII in the words of the code (a name from Azure is shown as written). They draw the figure and the title with the code of the pages (`cases/stake.py` over `cases/web/figures.py`), and a test holds the three together.

The buttons Explain, Challenge and Teach became one answer, "Ask or tell": a text box for a question, an objection or something to remember. It is recorded with the person's name and approves nothing.

## The cost that Cost Management shows, names, and a finding that says nothing

A request once read "Review the resource <id>", where the id was that of a subscription, drew a flat line at zero and said "No cost line in the last 30 days" for a subscription that Cost Management showed a bill for. Three things were wrong, and each is fixed in code and in the words.

**The cost is what Cost Management shows for the scope of the finding** (the actual cost, the 30 closed days; `cases/costs.py` holds the words and the shapes, `cases/arm.py` the reads). A finding about one resource is the cost lines of its own id: Cost Management gives ids in lower case, so every match is made in lower case. A finding about a resource group is every cost line in it. A finding about a subscription is every cost line of the subscription, also the lines that have no resource and no resource group (a plan of Microsoft Defender for Cloud, a purchase), read by service in one query, and the line under the figure says what it is made of: its three largest services and what the others cost together. That is the whole bill of the scope, not what a change could save, so no total, no order and no bar of the Decisions page counts it. The query follows `nextLink` (a link to any other host, subscription or service is refused), and when Cost Management says "too many requests" the client waits as long as it says (`Retry-After`, or one of the `x-ms-ratelimit-microsoft.costmanagement-*-retry-after` headers) and repeats the query, at most four times and never longer than a minute for one wait. When that is not enough the page says "Cost could not be read just now."; a request that had a figure from an earlier search keeps it.

**Nothing says a cost of nothing for a cost that Azure does not have.** A cost has a state (`CaseRecord.cost_state`): lines, none ("Azure has no cost line for this yet."), failed, currency, or not read. A chart is drawn only from cost that Cost Management showed: a series of nothing but zeros is no series, and the chart of a request with no cost line shows no line and no axis, only the words that say why (a disk that Azure had no cost line for even a week after it was made shows that this is no delay). For a managed disk that Azure has no cost line for, the page, the card and the letter also show an estimate at list price (a small table in `cases/estimate.py`, by the tier of the disk's type and its size, with the date of the table) in a line of its own that starts "Estimate at list price, not Azure's bill". The estimate is not a cost: it is kept apart (`CaseRecord.estimate`), and it is never put in a figure, added to a total, counted as a saving, given to the learning or used to check a change. Only a cost that Cost Management showed counts as measured.

**A person reads a name before an id.** The display name of a subscription is read once in a run (the list of the subscriptions that the Watch reads has it already, and a subscription that is not in that list is read with `GET /subscriptions/{id}`, the one more call of the app, with the identity that reads the rest), kept with the case (`CaseRecord.subscription_name`) and shown in titles, cards, letters, pages and plan texts, with the start of the id only where the name was not read. A finding that is an Azure Advisor recommendation says it in Advisor's own words (its short problem and solution, cut to one line each: `CaseRecord.advice`): the title is the problem and what it is about, and the first fact, "What to look at", is the solution. A finding that still cannot say what to look at (it names no group, no analysis and no Advisor words, and it is about a whole subscription or resource group, or about a thing whose name is an id) is held back: no request is made for it, a request made before is taken back, the History says `held back: nothing to say` for each (`case.held`), and the run counts them (`heldBack`, and the column "Held back" of the runs page). A person never sees "Review the resource <id>".

**Words on buttons.** A button says what it does in a word or two: "Approve", "Decline", "Yes, delete it". The person signs in to Azure when Azure asks, and that is on no button. A page, a letter and a card say once that the change is made with the person's own permissions (the row "Who does it" of the page) and do not say it again.

**An irreversible change asks twice.** A person once pressed the wrong answer and a delete ran after one press and the sign-in. Now "Approve" on a request whose undo says it cannot be undone (a delete) opens a second page first, "Confirm: <short title>": that this cannot be undone, what would be lost and where, and two buttons, "No, go back" (the main one, first) and "Yes, <delete> it". Nothing is recorded and nothing is changed before that second press and the sign-in. A change that can be undone (stop, resize, tier) and advice never ask, so the question keeps its weight. The one-time token and the note travel to the second page and come back with it. A person who posts the form with `confirm=yes` straight away skips the page; that is accepted, because the page guards against a slip and not against a client, and the token, the sign-in, the checks and the switches are unchanged.

## A finding that Meternaut may not change

Such a finding (outside the one group where a change may be made, or of a kind with no change) now gets a plan of the family `advise`. It changes nothing in Azure and uses the same plan code, one-time token and decision record as every plan, and, as every request, no expiry ([proposal 30](30-escalation.md)).

| Answer | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| Worth acting on | The plan closes and the case becomes `acknowledged`, with the person's note |
| Not worth it | The plan and the case are declined, with a reason: still needed, the figure is wrong, already handled, other |
| This is not mine | The plan closes and the case goes back to needing a person |
| Ask or tell | The words, with the name; the plan stays open |
| Decide later | A snooze that only changes the order; the plan stays open |

A person named on the plan with the role owner or approver may answer it; a change still needs the role approver. The full stop switch holds "worth acting on", as it holds every approval; the other answers still go through. A request that nobody answered never closes: it waits, visibly, and is passed on to the next person ([proposal 30](30-escalation.md)). A finding that a person answered is not put to them again for 30 days. Advice gets no letter of its own: it waits on the page and in the weekly letter.

A person's time is the scarce thing, so code decides whether a finding is worth a request (`brief.why_not_ask`, which `Approvals` applies; `UNPRICED`, `ADVICE_OPEN_MAX`). **A finding of the investigation gets a request only when code priced a saving for it:** the saving that its case records (`case.saving_usd_month`), which is what `cases/pricing.py` works out from the change that the finding states and from the bill, what a resource that a measured zero shows unused cost in 30 days, or Azure Advisor's own figure when a finding cites one for a change. What a resource costs is not what a change saves. A finding with one number behind it ("2,929 requests in 30 days, so capacity may exceed use") that names no change and prices nothing is the AI's unfinished work: it is no request, whatever the resource costs and whatever else backs it. **And only when something was measured of the resource's use, and, for a switch, when both AIs are sure** (`UNMEASURED`, `UNCONFIRMED`): a finding whose case carries the flag `NO_MEASUREMENT` (no measurement that the tools confirmed backs it) is no request, whatever it saves, because a price list and an assumption are not a use. A saving that is a switch to another price of the price list (a change whose action is `switch` in `cases/pricing.py`, whether it is priced from an alternative that the tool showed (the form `alternative`) or from a share that the AI assumes (the form `assumed`), or a sum of rules that holds an alternative) is a request only when the second AI agreed (`case.learned["skeptic"]`) and the first AI was sure enough (its own confidence, `case.confidence`, is at least 0.7, the gate of a proposal, `brief.SURE_ENOUGH`; none counts as not sure): the list says what another tier costs and not whether this resource can move to it, nothing in the bill or in a measurement can check that, so both AIs must be sure, and the second AI is shown the alternative. `brief.why_not_ask` says the first reason that holds, in this order (`NOT_ASKED`): `unpriced`, `unmeasured`, `unconfirmed`. A group is judged by the fields of the group's case, as its page shows them. Azure Advisor's own claims, the findings of the older detectors, every plan of a change and the waste that Meternaut planted are not touched by these rules.

**There is no floor on what a resource costs.** A finding with a priced saving is a request however little the resource costs, and one with none is no request however much it costs: the first live Watch opened 49 requests for findings of the second kind, 46 of them for private endpoints with the one doubt "missing telemetry, not measured inactivity", and each said "no figure". Only 30 requests for findings that the AIs did not settle wait at the same time (ten until 2026-11-18, see [proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)), and a finding that is worth more than the smallest of them is not held by that limit, up to 45 open requests in all (`ADVICE_OPEN_HARD`); a finding that both AIs agree on and that code re-checked is not held by it either. What is not asked stays a case and an open question: the Investigator gets it again through `recall_open_questions`, a request is made when a saving is priced (or a place is free), the start page says, in a sentence for each cause (no saving was priced, nothing was measured, a switch that both AIs were not sure of, the finding cannot say what to look at, the places are full or nobody is named), how many findings are held back for it, and the notes at the end of the investigation count, as numbers only, the cases that no request was made for (a case whose request is open is not one of them), one note for each reason (no saving was priced from the bill, nothing was measured of the resource's use, the second AI did not agree that the switch can be made or the first was not sure, each said so that its words agree with the number; a note keeps 300 characters, so none is cut), so that nothing is dropped silently, and `recall_open_questions` gives the Investigator each of them again with the reason as one typed word (`why`: `unpriced`, `unmeasured`, `unconfirmed` or `none`; a word of the rules and never a text, so that it says why and never what to find). **Not proved:** that nobody wants to be asked about a finding that the AI could not price or that nothing measured (it stays an open question until it can be priced or something is measured), that the second AI's agreement means that a switch can be made (it is an opinion that was shown the alternative, and not a check of the features of the other tier), and a resource whose use cannot be measured at all is not asked about until a question for it exists.

`advise` never reaches Azure: the sign-in to Azure refuses an advise plan, the runner has no call for it, and a test pins that the two modules do not know the family.

Records stay readable: new fields have defaults and unknown fields are dropped. `CaseRecord.owner` is new: a dictionary with the keys `tagOwner`, `tagName`, `createdBy`, `createdAt`, `changedBy`, `changedAt` and `lookedUp`. Another part of the system has to fill it from the tags and the Activity Log; until then the page says that nobody has looked up the owner.

## Advice says no more than it knows

A request once read "Consider deleting the private endpoint x", with "Low risk", "Low confidence", "Changes nothing in Azure" and a saving, for a private endpoint of an API Management instance that belongs to another team. Its only evidence was that the endpoint was Disconnected and that no data came back for its traffic. Nothing had shown that nothing used it, and a person who acted on it would have broken what sits behind the instance. Eight rules came out of it. Each is in code, and none is left to the words of a model.

**Missing data is not zero.** A claim that a resource is not used rests on a measurement that exists, had a value on at least 20 of the last 30 days (the same 20 that the chart of use asks for) and is nil in every total that the tools recorded. No data, a read that failed, a metric that the type does not offer and a few days of values are unknown. Under the basis no-usage code drops the resources whose measurement is not one (`_quiet` and `_verify` in `cases/investigate.py`), and a finding that asks to delete is to delete only when every resource of it has a measured zero (`_hold_delete`): otherwise it asks for a look, with a note, and the case says "Not measured". A state that a resource reports about itself, such as a connection that is Disconnected, says how it is now and not whether anything uses it. The Investigator and the Skeptic are told so in their instructions.

**A resource that others share is never to be deleted.** Private endpoints, private DNS zones and their links, the gateways of virtual networks, firewalls, route tables, NAT gateways, API Management, Bastion, ExpressRoute and VPN objects, virtual networks with their peerings, network security groups, application gateways, DNS resolvers, DDoS plans, network managers and Key Vaults are on a list (`SHARED` in `cases/brief.py`). A finding about one of them may ask for a look and never for a deletion, whatever it measured, because what depends on it cannot be seen from here. A workspace that stores data is not on it: a finding about one is priced from the bill lines that it names ([proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md)).

**The title asks what is not known.** When no measured zero lets a finding ask to delete, its title is "Check whether the private endpoint x is still used" and not "Consider deleting ...", it claims no saving (the figure says what it costs and that nobody has worked out what a change would save), its facts say "Use: Not measured" when nothing was measured, and a "Check first" list follows: what it connects to, who owns it and its traffic. A card and a letter carry the list in one line. A request that an earlier version made keeps its record, and the page reads it by the same rules.

**The risk of acting is not the risk of answering.** Advice has two risks, and the page, the card and the letter say them apart. "Answering changes nothing in Azure" is a chip of its own. The risk chip ("High risk if you act") and the sentence under it are about doing what the title suggests: "Nothing proves that it is unused, so acting on it could break something that still uses it." when no measured zero shows it, "Traffic that you cannot see from here may depend on it." for a resource that others share, and "High risk" for the two together (`acting_risk` in `cases/brief.py`). The level is never low: a change is at least a medium risk, and the word "Low risk" never describes an answer.

**A resource that an automation manages is held back.** When the Activity Log names a write within 14 days by an identity that is no person (a sign-in name has an @) and is not one of Meternaut's own, somebody else's automation manages the resource. Every write of the log counts (`automatedAt` of the owner record), not only the newest: a person's edit yesterday does not hide an automation's write three days ago. The case stays, no request is made for it, one that nobody answered is taken back, the History says `held back: managed by an automation` (`case.held`) and the run counts it with the findings that say nothing (`heldBack`). Waste that Meternaut planted itself, in a group where a change may be made, is never held: its deploy changes it. *Since 2026-11-17* a finding that is held back is not hidden: it has a request of its own that nobody is asked to approve, and a page, a section of Savings found and a line in the weekly letter say why ([proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)). *Since 2026-11-18* an automation holds nothing back: the finding is an ordinary request of advice with a "Heads-up" line ("change it where it is defined"), and the words above are what a held finding said before.

**A log that was not read shows nothing.** A finding that asks to delete a resource, and whose Activity Log was not read (a read that failed or was refused, a Watch whose few reads were used up, or an older detector, which never looks it up) is held back too, with `held back: not checked who changed it`. A finding that only asks for a look is shown, and its owner line says "Not checked: who changed it." on the page, the card and the letter. A case that nobody will see takes no lookup.

**One place holds them all.** The older detectors judge every lead on the days that the investigation does not run, so the rules are not kept in the investigation alone: `Approvals.held_why` (with `held_reason` in `cases/brief.py`) is the one place that every path to an advise plan passes, whichever detector or AI made the case, and a deletion needs that none of the cited totals of the resource is above nil, whatever the basis. The waste that Meternaut planted is the one exemption (the planted gate of `cases/families/` stays as it is).

**A request that was made under the old rules is taken back by Meternaut.** At every Watch `Approvals.hold_back_empty` closes an open advise request that nobody has answered when the case would be held back now, when its title asks to delete (`Consider deleting ...`) and the case is a resource that others share or has no measured zero, or when it is the request of a finding of the investigation whose case has no saving that code priced and whose plan states none either (`UNPRICED`, above), or that nothing measured or whose switch the AIs were not both sure of (`UNMEASURED`, `UNCONFIRMED`) and that was made before these rules. A request that is made or opened under them keeps `preconditions["askedUnder"]` (`ASKING_RULES`, written by `build_advice`, so also when a held request becomes an open one), and a finding that is judged again later, with no measurement or with another verdict, never takes such a request back with a false "Meternaut's rules changed": only a request that has no such key is taken back for these two reasons. A request that was made before the key, and whose finding meets the rules at the first Watch that finds it, gets the key then (`Approvals._keep_asked`, in `hold_back_empty`: only when nobody has answered it, sealed again with the code that it had), so that a later judgment cannot take it back for them either; one whose finding does not meet them at that Watch is taken back. The last three take the reason `rules`. A request that was asked with a saving is not taken back when its finding is judged again later and no price is found: the case keeps the figure and the price of the open request (`Approvals.asked_saving`: the saving of the request, and the typed price of the earlier case with its level and basis), so its page says what the person was shown, and not that nothing was priced or that Azure Advisor gave it, and its wait does not start again. The take-back runs at the Watch and not at the minute tick, after the requests that a clock closed before are opened again (`escalation.revive`), so that a request that comes back and that no saving backs is taken back in the same Watch and the ladder of [proposal 30](30-escalation.md), which reads the open requests, makes no step for it. The plan keeps why (`preconditions["takenBack"]`, written in the one write that ends it, so a case that is judged again a week later cannot lose it), the History says why (`held back: Meternaut's rules changed` or `held back: not checked who changed it`, one line for each request and one count for the Watch) and the page, the card and the letter say "Taken back" and what is true: "Meternaut's rules changed, so it took this request back: ..." only when the rules changed, and for the other reason what changed in the facts ("nobody has looked up who changed the resource"). A resource that an automation manages is no reason to take a request back (it is asked about, with a warning: [proposal 29](29-held-findings.md)); a request that was taken back for it in the days before keeps the words that it had. "Nothing was changed in Azure." A request that a person answered in any way (approved, declined, asked about, put off, "not mine"), a request of Azure Advisor's own claim, a request of an older detector, a plan of a change, a request that is not open and the waste that Meternaut planted are never touched, and a request that was taken back is no unanswered request in the lessons (`cases/learning.py`), whenever the lessons are written, except one that the bill closed, which is written as a request that was acted on ([proposal 30](30-escalation.md), item 20).

## What removing it would do

The owner asked about the deletion advice for a Disconnected private endpoint of an API Management instance whether it is safe, and the honest answer needed things that nobody had read: what the state means, what else depends on it, what the DNS does when it is gone, whether another endpoint carries the traffic and who changed it. Meternaut must find that out and say it when it announces a change, and nobody should have to work it out beside the system. Before the page of a request about the use of a resource is made, code reads what depends on the resource and gives one verdict (`cases/impact.py`; the words of the page, the card and the letter are in `cases/brief.py`). Advice still changes nothing in Azure.

| Part | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Which kinds | A private endpoint is checked, and no other kind yet. The checks are a registry (`CHECKS` in `cases/impact.py`): one function for each kind, which reads and returns a digest. A finding about the use of any other kind says "Meternaut did not look at what depends on it." on the page, the card and the letter. The unattached disk, the public IP address, the network interface, the NAT gateway, the load balancer and the App Service plan are the next kinds to add. |
| What is read | Azure Resource Graph: the endpoint with the state, the description and the actions required of each of its connections (`privateLinkServiceConnections` and `manualPrivateLinkServiceConnections`); the service that it points at, with its own list of private endpoint connections and its public network access; the other endpoints to the same service and their states; and what else mentions the addresses of the endpoint. Azure Resource Manager: the DNS zone groups of the endpoint and the address records of the private DNS zones that they name, which are the two calls that `http.allowed_calls` gained (both GET, each an exact address). The Activity Log: every write of the last 90 days, by whom and when. Azure Monitor: the bytes in and out of the last 30 days. |
| With whose rights | The reader identity of the cases app, the one that reads the estate for the Watch: the Reader role on the subscriptions that it reads, with no new role (AGENTS.md rules 24 and 29). The client of `cases/http.py` holds reads and queries only, and the two new addresses are on its list by name. Nothing is written, no AI sees what was read, and a run checks at most six resources (`MAX_IMPACTS`) inside one budget of reads. |

**The verdict is code's, in three levels.** `_judge` in `cases/impact.py` derives it from the facts and from nothing else. "Do not remove": the endpoint is approved and carries traffic (Azure Monitor counted some in the 30 days before Meternaut read it), or it is the only approved private way to a service that takes no public traffic. "Safe to remove": the endpoint is Disconnected or Rejected (Learn, "Private endpoint overview": the connection was removed by the owner of the private-link resource, so the endpoint becomes informative and should be deleted for clean-up), every read worked, the service does not list it as approved or pending, nothing that Azure Resource Graph lists mentions its addresses, no application or service identity changed it, and no traffic was counted. "Check first": everything else, with each thing that is unsettled as a plain sentence (it is pending or approved and quiet, the readings disagree, something mentions its addresses, an automation may manage it). The AIs never see the digest, and the Skeptic can object to a fact on the page, not to the verdict.

**A read that failed is not "nothing found", and neither is a read that was not made.** Each read that failed, or that the identity may not make, is named in the line "Not checked: ...", together with what read-only reads cannot see: whether a policy or a template makes the endpoint again, name servers and firewall rules that Azure Resource Graph does not list, and anything in a subscription that Meternaut cannot read. So is a fact that was left unread although no read failed: an Activity Log that stopped after its pages (only part of the 90 days was read), an endpoint with no address that could be read (nothing could be searched for: the row says "Not searched"), more addresses than the search takes, and a service that Resource Graph does not show (it may be in a subscription that Meternaut cannot read). The check is then not complete, and a check that is not complete never gives "Safe to remove". The traffic follows the rule that missing data is not zero: only a nil on at least 20 days of both metrics is a measured zero.

**The search for what mentions the addresses has a positive control.** A miss of that search (a `has` on an address in Azure Resource Graph) turns "Check first" into "Safe to remove", so a miss counts only from a search that works. The query leaves no network interface out, and the endpoint's own interface holds its address (Learn, "What is a private endpoint?": the interface lives as long as the endpoint and keeps its address), so a search that works lists it. When the answer does not hold it, the search cannot be trusted: that is named in "Not checked", and "nothing mentions its addresses" is not given as a reason. The interfaces are not counted as things that mention the addresses. Nothing was run against real Azure: that the search finds the interface is the first thing that a real run shows.

**When it was read.** The digest says when it was read (`readAt`, UTC), and the page, the card and the letter say "Read on 12 November 2026.", because a request may be opened days after the read. The words hold the date of a write and never "yesterday" or "3 days ago", and the traffic is the 30 days before Meternaut read it. What a person reads of the state of a connection is what Learn says of it ("What is a private endpoint?" and "Manage Azure private endpoints"): only an approved endpoint can send traffic, and the owner of the service can change the state later, so a rejected endpoint may have carried traffic before. Learn does not settle whether the owner can approve a rejected connection again: "Manage Azure private endpoints" says that a connection that was denied cannot be approved and has to be made again, and the troubleshooting pages for connectivity failures and for access denied errors say, for a Rejected connection, to ask the resource owner to approve it. Meternaut therefore says neither. When traffic was counted for an endpoint that is Rejected or Disconnected now, the reason names that state and says that the connection may have changed inside the 30 days of the reading: it is "Check first", and it is no disagreement of the readings.

**The page, the card and the letter.** The page has a block "Impact": the verdict, the day of the check, its reasons, the table "What was read, and where it was read", what happens to traffic and DNS if it is removed, how to undo it, and two sentences on what was not checked. The card and the letter have one line, "Check what depends on it before you remove it. ..." with the first sentence of the first reason and "Meternaut checked this on 12 November.", and "Meternaut could not read all of it." when something was not read. The general list "Check first" is not shown beside an analysis. The risk chip follows the verdict ("Do not remove" is high, "Safe to remove" is medium, and a change is never low, because the reads cannot see everything), and a shared kind of resource still asks "Check whether ... is still used", never to delete it. The table of the details names no digest as a fact that the AIs saw: the digest is made by code after both AIs have judged, and no input of an AI is made from the facts of a case.

**A deletion of the network without a finished check is held back.** A finding that still asks to delete a resource of the network that others do not share (a measured zero, and a kind such as a public IP address or a network interface) needs a check that read everything. Until its kind has one, the finding is held back with `held back: impact not checked` (`case.held`, counted with the findings that say nothing), and an open request of that kind from before is taken back with the reason "nobody checked what depends on the resource" (`held_reason` in `cases/brief.py`).

## What a finding that states its change shows

A finding states the change that it proposes (the bill lines that it would shrink and what remains of them), and code works out the saving from the real bill lines ([proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md)). The page, the card and the letter show the figure in one sentence and say what it rests on in the next ("Over the last 7 days this resource paid about $100 a month for what the change would shrink. The AI assumes that 30 to 60 percent of that would go away. That is the AI's own assumption, and Meternaut did not check it."; for a switch to an alternative that a price list prices, the sentences say what the bill pays now, what the alternative would cost for the same use (when that and what is paid add up to the saving) and that nobody checked that the resource can be switched to it, [proposal 28](28-alternative-prices.md#the-form-alternative-a-switch-priced-by-code)); the level (Expected when the change removes everything that the named meters bill, Scenario for every other form) stays in the record and decides whether the sentence says "could save" or, for a confirmed change, "saves". The page has one more fact, "Charges it would shrink", and a quote with what the first AI says that a person would do ("What the AI says. Meternaut did not check the wording."). A switch is titled from what to what ("Consider switching the firewall x from Standard Deployment to Basic Deployment") when its figure keeps the plain names of both ends; a switch whose alternative cannot be named in plain words is not priced. A request of advice that waits says in one sentence what the person is asked, right after the chips on the page and in the card and the letters too: "You are asked whether it is worth switching the firewall from Standard Deployment to Basic Deployment." (`brief.ASKED`, one sentence in the words of the verb of the title, and "You are asked to find out whether the disk is still used." for the question of use; a finding of one of the checks of Meternaut adds what the check found, from `brief.DETECTOR_STATE`: "a closer look at the disk, which is not attached to any virtual machine"; a statement, because a page, a card and a letter ask the reader nothing; that an answer changes nothing in Azure and that a person who says yes acts alone are said once, by the chip and by the paragraph "What happens if you answer"). A finding that states a change that code cannot price says why in one sentence and shows no figure. Nothing in the decision brief is written for a kind of resource any more.

Two kinds were written for before: the data that a workspace stores and nothing reads, and the gateways that carry more scale units than anybody uses. Each had a module that worked out its own figure, rows of evidence, and two or three choices in code's words, and a block in the page, the card and the weekly letter. They are gone. The same findings are priced by the general path, from the meters that the Investigator names and what remains of them; the choices a person reads are now the AI's own words, and the second AI is asked whether a smaller size would still carry the highest value that its rows show. What a person lost is code's own list of choices and Microsoft's table of sizes for a gateway: the figure no longer proves that the smaller size carries what was seen, which the second AI judges and a person decides. The family `scale-vpn-gateway` still lowers a gateway that Meternaut planted by one step ([proposal 14](14-families-of-change.md)).

## A finding that covers many resources

The first live Watch made one case and one request for each of 60 private endpoints that had no traffic data: dozens of look-alike rows that no person can use, when the unit of the decision is the finding. Now a finding that asks for a look (the proposal investigate) and has more than five resources left after the checks of code is one case and one request ("60 private endpoints without traffic data, together 357 US dollars in 30 days: worth looking at?"). A delete or a change is never grouped, because each resource needs its own decision, and neither is a finding of five resources or fewer.

- **One case, owned by one resource.** It belongs to the dearest of the resources (the first of them when the cost is the same), so the link to the portal, a person's do-not-touch note, the quiet days after an answer and the open questions of the Investigator work on that resource. A Watch that finds the group again takes the case that it made before, even when the costs have moved, so that no second case and no second request are made while the first waits or was answered. The answers mean what they mean for one resource and are recorded on that case.
- **Which resources it stands for.** The case keeps the short ids of its resources, and their resource ids in lower case (at most 200) for its daily cost, which is the sum of theirs. A case that was judged lately stands for a later finding only when it stands for at least 80 percent of that finding's resources, whether or not the resource that the case belongs to is one that the finding names (the Investigator's lists differ from run to run, and the dearest resource may be missing from one); the resources that it does stand for are not asked about again, and the others are a group of their own, whose first resource is the dearest of them. So a later finding that names the first resource of an old group together with six new ones of 500 US dollars each reaches a person.
- **Do-not-touch.** A resource that a person marked do-not-touch is never one of a group: it is left out before the group is made, and the case says how many. A mark on the dearest resource does not silence the group, which then belongs to the next one. When five or fewer resources that nobody marked are left, each is a case as ever, and a marked one gets no request. A mark on the first resource of a group that was judged lately holds nothing back until the days of the recheck have passed. The marks are read before the agents run: when they cannot be read, the investigation does not run, nothing is spent, and the History says so.
- **What is at stake.** The cost of the case is what all of them cost. It has no figure of its own unless the finding states a change that code could price: then the figure is the sum of the figures of its resources, each by its own meters and counted once, and the basis says how many of how many are priced ([proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md)). The rule of a request is the same as for one resource: when no saving could be priced for the resources together, no request is made and the case stays an open question.
- **Still per resource.** A resource that a guard holds is not part of the group, the resources that no measurement backs are counted one by one, and the status comes from the first AI's confidence and the second AI's verdict as for any resource. The cap of 60 cases a run counts resources, and a group that it cut says in its first row how many more it left out.
- **The older requests.** The first version asked about each resource of such a finding on its own, and those requests were still open when the request of the group was made (49 of them, on the first real run). After the investigation the Watch closes the open advice requests for the other resources of a group whose request is open, as taken back (the reason is "group"), since they only ask the same question again, and the History says how many. A request that a person answered, put off or asked about, the request of a change and the request of another group are never closed, and neither is anything when the request of the group is not open. The closed requests end at the time of closing. An advice that nobody answered is no row of "Recently decided" any more (a change that nobody answered is, and so is an advice that the bill closed, taken by the time of its closing however old the request is: [proposal 30](30-escalation.md), item 20), and the History says "Closed with no answer: N requests to look at a finding" once a day.
- **What a person sees.** The headline says how many, of what kind and in how many groups, and names the first ("Look at 60 private endpoints in 14 groups, the first is pe-60"). The rows say what it covers ("60 private endpoints in 14 resource groups", and "and 10 more that the limit of one run left out" when the cap cut it), what they cost together and which they are ("pe-60, pe-59, ... and 54 more"). The weekly letter adds what they cost together. Nothing else on the page changes.

## What is not done

- **Built and tested against fakes, not run in Azure:** the walk through the requests, quiet hours and the request that two people approve ([the loop](04-the-loop.md#going-through-the-requests-quiet-hours-and-two-people)). By rule 32 none of them is done until it has run there.
- **The Teams cards** are rebuilt from the same decision brief ([the Teams record](13-teams-and-outlook.md)) and say what the pages and the letters say (the figure a year and a month with its level and basis, the title, the chips as words, one footer line), and since 2026-11-12 they carry the answers as buttons that are links to a page that sends the answer ([proposal 19](19-teams-cards-through-power-automate.md)). Built and tested; the first ones were seen in Teams on 2026-11-11 and the buttons not yet. The older templates in [the web pages](../experience/06-web-pages.md) and the messages design still show three answer buttons and a link to a person.
- **The owner** is looked up for a case that the investigation opens (a tag of a closed set and the Activity Log: [the toolbox record](12-investigation-toolbox.md)); a case of the fixed detectors, the planted waste, has none, and the page says so.
- **The saving of a finding that states its change** is worked out by code from the bill lines that the Investigator names, and has not been tried against a real estate or a real Investigator: tests run it over a fake estate with the real tools ([proposal 24](24-priced-findings.md) says what is not proved). A person no longer gets code's own rows for the data that nothing reads, or for gateways, or the choices that were written for them, and no longer gets Microsoft's table of sizes for a gateway.
- **Nothing learns from the answers yet.** They are stored; nothing reads them.
- **The cases of a group that older runs made one by one** stay as they were: their requests expire, and they stay open questions until a person answers them. A group whose dearest resource has left the finding gets a new case. A group of resources of different types is called resources.
- **Forwarding a finding to its likely owner** is not built; it needs the owner's word on contacting people outside the list.
- **The link to the Azure portal** is `https://portal.azure.com/#resource` and the resource id, made only from an id of the shape of one resource. The portal's documentation also shows a shape with the directory in it. Neither was tried against the real portal.
- **The check in a browser after the deployment** needs the deployed page.
