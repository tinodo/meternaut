Meternaut style guide

Fluent 2 tokens, one stylesheet, no script. Drawn from the Decisions page, which is the first page built from it.

This is the one place that says how a Meternaut page looks and reads. Every page is made from the parts below, with the tokens below, and nothing else. A rule that is not written here is not a rule. When a page needs something new, it is drawn here first.

See it all together in the Decisions preview. The stylesheet is meternaut-ui.css, next to this file. The pages of the app carry the same text as cases/web/style.py, because the pages read no file, and a test fails when the two differ.

1 Principles

Eight rules. Each has a test that a person can apply in a second.

RuleWhat it meansThe test
1 Answer firstThe first line of a decision says what to do and what it is worth.A stranger can say what to do after one second.
2 One thing in focusOne decision is shown large. The rest are ranked by what is at stake, and a bar shows the difference.You can name the most important item without reading.
3 Money has a unit, a period and a source"$1,682 a year", "$100.74 a month at list price". Never a bare number.Every figure answers: how much, per what, from where.
4 Say what is not knownNever "No figure". Show what is known, and say why the rest is missing.An empty slot always carries a reason.
5 One row per decisionFindings of the same kind are one row that opens into its members. The same resource is never shown twice.No two rows have the same headline.
6 Less text, more shapeA row has a headline, one figure, one chart and one verdict. The reasoning is one click away.A row fits on one screen line pair, with no paragraph.
7 Colour never works aloneEvery colour has a word or a shape beside it.The page still reads in greyscale.
8 Calm by defaultNeutral surfaces. Blue for actions and data, green for money saved, orange and red only for risk.Nothing on the page shouts that is not urgent.

5 Layout

The Decisions page, top to bottom. Every page of figures is built in this order, and leaves out what it does not have.

  1. Header and tabs, as on every page.
  2. Title and one sentence that says what is at stake, and how to sort.
  3. Summary tiles: at most three, each a figure with a unit and a note.
  4. The decision in focus: the one with the most at stake (the largest saving, or failing that the largest cost), with its figure, what changes and why, and a chart of its cost.
  5. Ranked rows below it, with a bar that shows how much each is worth against the first.
  6. A second list for what looks wasteful but is not measured, headed by its total cost.
  7. What is held back, and what was decided lately, both quiet.
  8. The footer: who wrote this, and a way to find who answers for it.
WidthWhat changes
Wider than 52remA row has five columns: rank, headline, figure, chart, button. The focus card has text on the left and the cost chart on the right.
40em to 52remA row has two lines: the headline and figure above, the chart and button below. The focus card is one column.
Narrower than 40emEverything is one column, in the same order. Buttons are as wide as the screen.

The request page

The page behind "Review": one request, in this order. It leaves out what it does not have. It is wide, like the Decisions page, so that the answer can stand beside what is read.

  1. Header and tabs, as on every page, and a plain link back to the decisions.
  2. The title: the action and its target, as in the row of the list. One line under it: the kind of resource, its group, when it was found and until when the request is open.
  3. Three chips: the risk, how sure the AIs are, and what a change leaves behind (it can or cannot be undone), or that an answer changes nothing in Azure.
  4. When the request is not open any more, one notice says what became of it.
  5. The decision card, headed "If you approve" (a change) or "If you answer" (advice): the figure a year, then the figure a month with its level and its basis, and the line that says money is in US dollars; the chart of daily cost beside it; and under both, the facts: what changes, who does it and with whose rights, what is checked again, the undo and what could go wrong.
  6. On the left, what code read (a table of fact, value and where it was read), what a person could do about it (only for data that nothing reads), why the AIs think so (how sure, then the words of the AIs in boxes that say that no code checked them) and whose resource it is.
  7. On the right, in a panel, the answer: a note, the reason for a decline, the main button and "Decline" one above the other, and the quiet answers (not mine, ask or tell, decide later) below them.
  8. Details, closed: ids, steps, models, guards and the log of the change. Then the footer.

6 Components

Summary tile

  • Ready to save

    $3,255 a year

    3 decisions · Scenario, Expected. The basis is in each row.

  • A label, one figure with its unit, one note. The Decisions page has three: Ready to save, Cost under review and Saving measured.
  • The figure is green only for a saving. Saving measured counts what Cost Management confirmed after a change, and nothing that was only expected.
  • No figure: show a dash, and put the reason in the note.
  • A page of counts (What happened, Operations) uses the same tile with a count or a short word in place of a figure: a label, the number, one note. A tile of counts never shows money (Meternaut is not a cost report), and it is never green.

Feed line

  • DeclinedReview the disk build-disk-02Tino · reason: it is already handled
  • Change madeStop virtual machine build-agent-03Stopped and released (deallocated).
  • Search3 new findings
  • A line is a chip that says what kind of thing it was, the title of the request as a link (or a count, for findings) and one quiet part: who, why, or what came of it. Nothing else, no sentence that repeats the chip.
  • Lines are grouped by day under a heading of that day, newest first. The title is the one that the row and the request page show.

Panel and grid

Changes

State: Running

What it stops: No approval is accepted and no change is made in Azure.

All messages

State: Paused by Otto

What it stops: No message is sent to anybody.

  • A panel is one thing that a person does or reads together: a group of settings, one switch, the answer to a request. It has a heading of its own, so a screen reader can reach it.
  • Panels that stand side by side are a grid: as many columns of at least 20rem as fit, one column on a narrow screen.
  • State is a chip (a word and a shape) and then, when there is more to say, who, when and why in plain words.

Impact figure

The most important part of a row. It has three states, and a row is always in one of them. The yearly figure is large, the monthly figure is small, and the label says what kind of figure it is.

  • Saves, Expected: a concrete saving with its level, which is Expected (from the cost lines of the last 30 days), Scenario (an estimate: Azure Advisor's own, or the list price) or Verified (measured after the change). Green. The basis is one line under the title ("Based on the cost lines of the last 30 days.").
  • Costs, saving not measured: the cost is known and the saving is not. Neutral. The cost is the most that could be saved. For a finding about a whole subscription or resource group it is the sum of every cost line in it (also the lines that have no resource and no resource group, such as a plan of Microsoft Defender for Cloud), as Cost analysis shows it: the note says "All the cost lines of the subscription in the last 30 days. What a change would save was not worked out.", and the line under it says what it is made of (see below). That is the whole bill of the scope, not what a change could save, so no total, no order and no bar counts it. A request that only asks whether a resource is still used is in this state too: no saving is claimed for a resource that nothing measured shows can go.
  • Cost not visible: Cost Management shows no cost for it, and the reason is said in plain words: "Azure has no cost line for this yet." (the read worked and Azure has none), "Cost could not be read just now." (Azure said too many requests for as long as the app was willing to wait, or answered with an error; the next search reads it again), "The cost lines are not in US dollars." or "No cost line was read yet." Grey. This is the only place a dash may stand in for a figure, and a cost of nothing is never written or drawn for it: no "$0.00", and no line along the floor of a chart.
  • Rows are ranked by the larger of the saving (when known) or the cost, and the bar under the figure shows it against the first row.

  • Made of: for a finding about a whole subscription or resource group, its three largest services and what the others cost together, from the same lines as the figure. It is a sentence of words and numbers, never a chart.
  • The estimate: for a managed disk that Azure has no cost line for, an estimate at list price from a small table in the code (by the tier of its type and its size: the East US prices that the Azure Retail Prices API gave on the date that the line says). It is on the page of a request only (a letter and a card have one link, the page of the request), it opens a new place and says so with rel="noopener", and it is made from ids that code checked, never from text.

Chips

A chip says one thing in one or two words, with a shape, so that colour is never alone.

Low risk Medium risk High risk High confidence Needs measuring

A request that only asks for an answer says two things apart: what answering does, and what acting on it risks.

High risk if you act on it Low confidence Answering changes nothing in Azure

  • Risk is a circle (low), a triangle (medium) or a diamond (high). Confidence and state are squares.
  • At most three chips in a row, and never one that repeats the label next to it.
  • For advice the risk chip is about doing what the title suggests, and it says so: "High risk if you act on it". The chip "Answering changes nothing in Azure" is its own. "Low risk" never stands for an answer: acting is at least a medium risk, and a high one for a resource that others share (a private endpoint, a firewall, a gateway of a network) when nothing measured shows that nothing uses it.
  • The same two things are in words on the page ("What changes" and "What could go wrong: If you act on it: ..."), in the card and in the letter. The words of the risk come from the kind of resource and the evidence: "Unproven: nothing shows that nothing uses it." when no measurement shows it, and "Could break traffic that you cannot see from here." for a resource that others share.
  • A resource that others share is never offered for deletion. Its title is "Check whether the private endpoint x is still used". When its impact was not analysed a short list "Check first" follows (what it connects to, who owns it and its traffic); when it was, the block "Impact" below says what to check, and no list stands beside it.

Impact

Before Meternaut asks anybody to remove a resource, code reads what depends on it (a private endpoint first) and gives one verdict. The verdict is never an AI's: no AI makes it, sees it or argues with it, and the Skeptic can only object to a fact.

Safe to remove Check first Do not remove

  • The block "Impact" on the page is, in this order: the verdict, when it was read ("Read on 12 Nov 2026 UTC."), its reasons (each a plain sentence), the table "What was read, and where it was read" (each fact with the place that it was read: Azure Resource Graph, Azure Resource Manager, the Activity Log or Azure Monitor), "If it is removed" (what happens to traffic and to DNS), "How to undo it", and the line "Not checked: ..." in muted text.
  • "Not checked" names every read that failed or that this identity may not make, every fact that was not read although no read failed (an Activity Log that was read in part, an endpoint with no address to search for, a service that Azure Resource Graph does not show, a search that did not find the endpoint's own network interface), and what the read-only reads cannot see (whether a policy or a template makes it again, name servers and firewall rules that Azure Resource Graph does not list, anything in a subscription that Meternaut cannot read). A read that failed is never "nothing found": the check is then not complete, and the verdict is never "Safe to remove".
  • The card and the letter carry one line: "Impact: Check first. Read on 12 Nov 2026 UTC. the first sentence of the first reason", and "Part of it could not be read." when something was not read. The risk chip follows the verdict: "Do not remove" is a high risk if you act on it, and "Safe to remove" is a medium one, because Meternaut could not read everything. A change is never a low risk.
  • A finding whose kind of resource has no check says so in one line, on the page, the card and the letter: "Impact not analysed: Meternaut did not look at what depends on it."

Buttons

Review and decideDecide laterShow detailsDeclineReview

  • A button starts with a verb and says what it does in one or two words: "Review and decide", "Approve", "Decline", never "OK" or "Open".
  • One primary button for each card or row. The rest are plain or subtle.
  • Height 40 pixels, or 32 in a row.
  • The sign-in to Azure is never on a button. The person signs in when Azure asks. A page, a letter and a card say once that the change is made with the person's own permissions (the line "Who does it" of the page), and nowhere else.

Charts in a row

With data

No data yet

  • 30 daily points, a 1.5 pixel line, a tint under it and a dot on today. No axes: the figure beside it is the scale.
  • It starts at zero, so a flat line means a steady cost.
  • No data is the words "No daily costs yet" and nothing else: no line, no dashed line, no line along the floor, because a person reads any line at zero as a cost of nothing. The reason is said in words where the figure is (a screen reader has it in the label). A series with no cost on any day is no series, so a chart is never drawn from nothing but zeros.

Decision row and group row

A row is one decision. Findings of one kind about one resource each are one row that opens into a table, and each member has its own Review button, because nothing answers several requests at once. A finding that stands for many resources is one request already: its row opens into the first few of them and one button, "Review all 50". A change is never consolidated: a person approves one change at a time.

The decision in focus

The decision with the most at stake, shown large: the rank, the title, the figure, the chips, four facts (what changes, why, checked, undo), one button, and a chart of its cost. A page has one. It is drawn in full in the Decisions preview, with a second chart (CPU use, with the 5% idle line). The app draws that chart now, under the cost chart, for the request of one resource of a kind that has a use (a machine's CPU, an App Service plan's CPU, the traffic of a private endpoint, a public IP address or a load balancer, the transactions of a storage account, the calls of an AI account, the connections of a point-to-site gateway); a resource that has none shows one line that says why. The request page uses the same card (class focus) with the figure and the chart side by side and the facts under them, so that the row, the card and the page show one figure that comes from one piece of code.

The answer panel

Your answer

ApproveDecline
This is not mineAsk or tellDecide later
  • One panel, one form. The note and the reason come first; then the main button and "Decline" stacked, full width; then the quiet answers, which are small and look like links.
  • The main button says what happens: for a change it is "Approve", for advice "Worth acting on". Nothing in the panel approves anything on its own.
  • While a switch holds changes, the main button is left out and a notice says so; the other answers stay.

Notices

Information

Cost lines arrive 8 to 72 hours after use.

Done

The machine is stopped and released.

Needs a look

The check before the change could not read the machine.

Nothing was changed

Azure refused the request.

  • A notice says what kind of news it is in words (its title) and in its edge and colour. The kinds: information (an answer recorded, a change that was not needed), success (a change made), warning (a check stopped a change, a sign-in was not completed, an answer cannot be taken now) and error (Azure refused, or the change failed).
  • The page that says what came of an answer is the title, one notice of the kind of news it is (with no title of its own, because the title of the page is it), and one main button back.

Tables

  • A table is for numbers and records that a person looks things up in (runs, findings, what was measured). It stands in a box that scrolls sideways and can be reached with the keyboard, it has a caption, header cells with a scope, and its first column is the row header.
  • A date in a table stays on one line (the class nw), so that a column of dates is not a tall row of short lines. A status is a chip. A money figure is the figure of a request: a year, then its level, the month and its basis.

The confirmation before a change that cannot be undone

This cannot be undone

The disk and the data on it are deleted, and no snapshot was taken.

No, go backYes, delete it

  • Approving a change that cannot be undone, such as a delete, does not start the sign-in at once. A second page, titled "Confirm: " and the short title of the request, says that nothing has happened yet, what would be lost, where, and asks once. A person who comes from a Teams card was asked on the card, with the same words and the same two buttons, so that page does not ask again; a person who comes from a letter or the list gets it.
  • The safe answer is the main button and stands first: "No, go back". The other is red and says what it does, with the verb of the request: "Yes, delete it". Nothing is recorded and nothing is changed until that button is pressed and the person has signed in to Azure.
  • A change that can be undone (stop a machine, resize, change a tier) and advice never ask. Asking every time teaches people to press through.
  • The page has no question mark, because plain pages state and do not ask; it carries the same one-time token and the note of the page before.

Recommendations written by AI. A person approves every change.

  • Money. A dollar sign before the figure and a thousands separator: $4,297. A year is in whole dollars and is twelve times the month, a month has its cents ($358.11). "a year" and "a month" in words, never "/yr". Every saving carries its level (Expected, Scenario or Verified) and its basis in one line, and the page says once that money is in US dollars.
  • Time. "today", "yesterday", "3 days ago", then the weekday and date. The exact time is for the hover text and the history.
  • Names. A resource is in the monospace font, with its kind in words in front of it.
  • Words we do not use on a page for people. Family, plan (for a change), case, lead, gate, digest, re-check. Say kind, request, finding, "checked again". A test reads every page of the gallery, the Details and the pages of Operations included, and fails on any of these words, on "No figure" and on "Talk to a person" (an Azure product name such as "App Service plan" is the one exception).
  • Length. A headline is at most 70 characters. A note under it is one line. Reasoning is at most four short facts.

8 Charts

  • A chart answers one question, and its title says which: the thing, the period, the unit. "Cost per day, last 30 days".
  • A cost chart starts at zero. A usage chart (CPU, bytes, calls, connections) starts at zero too, breaks at a day that has no value, and has a dashed line to read it against where its kind has one ("5% idle line" for a machine's CPU, the default of Azure Advisor's low-use check; "no connection" for a point-to-site gateway). The line is for the person who decides: no AI is given it and no rule of waste uses it.
  • Blue is the line. Orange is a threshold. Green is only for money saved. No second data colour until a chart needs one, and then it is a grey.
  • Missing data is a gap or a dashed line with a reason, never a zero.
  • No pie, no 3-D, no two vertical scales on one chart, no legend that a label can replace.
  • Every chart has a text alternative that says what it shows in a sentence ("flat at about $4.67 a day"), not "chart".
  • A row has one small chart. The focus card has at most two large ones. A chart is drawn by the page itself, as inline SVG, so it needs no script and no outside file.

9 Access

  • Text 4.5 to 1 and meaningful shapes 3 to 1, in light and in dark. The numbers are in the colour section.
  • A focus ring of 3 pixels on everything that takes the keyboard, never removed.
  • A button is at least 32 pixels high; the main one is 40.
  • Headings in order, landmarks (header, nav, main, footer), a skip link, a list for a list and a table for a table.
  • Colour is never alone: risk has a shape and a word; a chart has a text alternative.
  • Readable at 200% and at 320 pixels wide without sideways scrolling, except inside a table.
  • Forced colours are handled: borders, lines and shapes use system colours.
  • No animation, no script, no autoplay. The page works with the keyboard alone.
