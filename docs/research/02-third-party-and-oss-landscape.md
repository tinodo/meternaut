## 2. Ten ideas worth stealing

1. **Query-usage-gated retention** — Grafana Adaptive Logs decides what's safe to drop from *measured query frequency per log pattern*, not volume alone. (grafana.com/docs/.../adaptive-logs)
2. **In-channel chat approval before execution** — Datadog posts a resource summary to Slack/Teams and only runs the automation after a human replies/approves in that channel. (docs.datadoghq.com/cloud_cost_management)
3. **Published, auditable "trust scorecard"** gating unattended writes — nOps requires a 7-day observation window, forecast-error ceiling and zero simulated-budget violations before Auto mode is even selectable. (help.nops.io)
4. **Graduated trust ladders** rather than binary on/off automation — Sedai's Datapilot/Copilot/Autopilot and Edge Delta's 5-level Guardrails both default new accounts to a conservative tier and require earned promotion. (docs.sedai.io; docs.edgedelta.com)
5. **ITSM-anchored approval** — Turbonomic's "Automated when approved" mode only exists once a real ServiceNow change-record target is configured — the approval is a governed business record, not a UI click. (ibm.com/products/turbonomic)
6. **PR-based remediation** instead of direct auto-apply — CAST AI's Application Performance Automation opens a pull request for risky changes rather than merging directly. (docs.cast.ai)
7. **Insuring the optimization itself** — Archera backs its guaranteed commitments with a real third-party insurance policy (Crum & Forster, A+ rated), not just a software SLA. (docs.archera.ai)
8. **Auto-built ownership graph from existing tools** — Edge Delta's Knowledge Graph infers Team/Person/Service "owns" relationships from GitHub/Slack/PagerDuty/Jira connections rather than relying on static tags. (docs.edgedelta.com)
9. **Governing AI/agent spend as its own optimization target** — CloudZero's Model Right Sizer recommends model/effort/token-budget choices with a read-only-by-default architecture and exactly one narrowly-scoped write skill. (docs.cloudzero.com)
10. **Live dry-run with projected impact before commit** — Grafana's "Apply recommended drop rates" dialog and Mezmo Flow both show projected volume/savings per segment before the human clicks apply. (grafana.com; docs.mezmo.com)

## 3. What exists vs. what we did not find (the owner-routed, owner-approved, verified-savings loop)

**Exists today, in pieces, across different vendors:** tag-based owner notification (near-universal); Slack/Teams/email alerting (most vendors — Teams support is inconsistent: native at Datadog/Grafana/Sedai/PerfectScale/Turbonomic-adjacent, workaround-only at CloudZero/Cribl/Mezmo); pre-execution approval gates (Datadog CCM's channel approval, Turbonomic's ServiceNow mode, Sedai's Copilot tier, nOps' trust-scorecard, Archera's opt-in policies, Grafana's apply-dialog); genuinely usage-aware filtering (Grafana Adaptive Logs; narrowly, Datadog's manual Audit-Trail report); autonomous commitment execution that is Azure-capable today (ProsperOps, nOps, Zesty, Archera).

**Not found in any of the 22 products researched:**
- **Identity-directory/manager-chain owner resolution.** Every vendor that touches identity (Entra ID/AD/Okta) uses it only for SSO/RBAC/access control. None resolves "who owns this resource" via directory attributes, org chart, or manager-chain escalation — all owner mapping is tag/metadata-based.
- **A single end-to-end chain** that (a) identifies the human owner via directory/activity history, (b) sends an actionable Teams adaptive-card-style approval, (c) executes the specific Azure change on approval, and (d) verifies realized savings — no product combines all four; pieces exist in different vendors (approval UX in Datadog; execution in Zesty/ProsperOps; notification in Sedai/Grafana).
- **Replay-based safety testing** of a proposed log-filter/drop rule against real historical queries before go-live. Grafana/Cribl/Edge Delta/Mezmo all preview a *sample* of data or a *volume* impact estimate, but none replays actual historical dashboard/alert queries against the proposed rule to confirm nothing breaks.
- **Counterfactual savings verification.** Every vendor shows "cost before vs. after" or an "estimated savings" figure; none documents a control-group/counterfactual methodology (e.g., what the resource would have cost absent the change, isolating confounds like usage growth or price changes) — evidence here is thin everywhere, not just absent at one vendor.
- **Usefulness analysis of ingested log *content*** (is this specific field/table ever read by a human, not just "is this pattern queried") — Grafana gets closest at pattern-level; none analyzes at a semantic/business-usefulness level (e.g., "this table backs a decommissioned dashboard").

## 5. Implications for our idea

- The **owner-via-directory/manager-chain** mechanism is a genuine, unclaimed gap across all 22 products — tags are the ceiling everywhere today.
- **Teams-native adaptive-card approval that executes an Azure change** has no direct precedent found; Datadog's Slack/Teams channel-approval is the closest analogue and a pattern worth emulating, not copying wholesale.
- **Query/usage-aware log filtering** is proven technology (Grafana), not a novel concept — our differentiation is extending that reasoning beyond logs to *all* underused Azure resources (gateways, reservations) and to Azure-native telemetry.
- **Graduated trust ladders with published, auditable promotion criteria** (nOps, Sedai, Edge Delta) are the current state of the art for safe autonomy — our HITL design should adopt this rather than a binary advisory/autonomous split.
- **Counterfactual savings verification is an industry-wide thin spot** — if we build it rigorously, it is a clear, defensible differentiator versus every product reviewed.
- **Replay-based safety checks before a risky change** (e.g., simulate a log-filter or gateway scale-down against real historical traffic) were not found anywhere — worth prototyping early as a trust-building feature.
- Azure depth is **uneven even among Azure-capable vendors** (nOps and Archera are deepest; several majors lag AWS materially) — real differentiation is available simply by being Azure-first and WAF/CAF-aligned rather than Azure-as-an-afterthought.
- Nearly every vendor's "agentic/AI" claim is **either a read-only chat assistant or a rebranded pre-existing deterministic/RL engine** — genuine LLM-driven reasoning that decides *and safely executes* infrastructure change is rare; this is a credible wedge if we're honest about today's safety bar.
- Commitment-automation (RI/SP) is the **most mature, most autonomous** corner of this market and already Azure-capable (ProsperOps, nOps, Zesty, Archera) — not worth rebuilding; consider integrating rather than competing here.
- Given Flexera's 2026-01-06 roll-up of ProsperOps + Chaos Genius, expect continued **consolidation toward "agentic FinOps" positioning by acquisition**, not organic LLM engineering — real technical moats will be judged by execution-safety design, not AI messaging.

### C. What changes in our package

1. **Positioning is unchanged and slightly sharper.** The first report's "not found" list holds up as *searched, not found*: directory- or manager-chain owner resolution, replay-based safety testing of a filter against real historical queries, counterfactual savings verification, and usefulness analysis of log *content*. These are stated everywhere as "not found" with the search limits, never as "does not exist".
2. **Our graduated Autonomy Ladder is the market's state of the art**, not an outlier (Sedai's three tiers, Edge Delta's five-level guardrails, Turbonomic's four modes, nOps' trust scorecard). We keep it, and add to the plan the idea of **published, auditable promotion criteria** between levels.
3. **Our stance on commitments is deliberately more conservative than the market.** At least four vendors (ProsperOps, nOps, Zesty, Archera) already run *fully autonomous* Azure-capable commitment purchasing. We do not compete there: transactions are never L3–L5 ([06 §6](../ideation/06-closed-loop-actions-and-autonomy.md)), the commitment family is parked, and if a customer already uses such an engine its outcomes are simply ledger inputs. This is a trade-off, named honestly: a competitor's autonomous engine will save more on rate optimisation than our signed-manifest flow.
4. **Ideas worth borrowing (design patterns, not code)** and where they land:

   | Idea (source) | Where it lands |
   |---|---|
   | Usage-gated filtering from measured query frequency (Grafana) | The log-usefulness playbook ([03 §1](../ideation/03-deep-dives-seed-examples.md)); note Grafana uses a 15-day window, we plan a longer, calibrated evidence horizon |
   | In-channel approval before execution (Datadog) | Decision cards ([05](../ideation/05-human-interaction-concepts.md)) — with our authority check and artefact binding added |
   | Published trust scorecard before an automation can be switched on (nOps) | Promotion criteria between ladder levels ([plan/02](../plan/02-guardrails-workplan.md) autonomy policy) |
   | ITSM-anchored approval: the approval *is* a change record (Turbonomic, PV) | Assist-level approvals attach to the customer's change record ([07 §2](../ideation/07-ownership-and-organization.md) CAB/ITSM counterpart) |
   | Pull-request remediation instead of direct apply (CAST AI) | Already the IaC-first path ([06 §4](../ideation/06-closed-loop-actions-and-autonomy.md), spikes S1b and S13) |
   | An ownership graph built from existing tools (Edge Delta) | Owner Graph signals ([07 §4](../ideation/07-ownership-and-organization.md)): repositories, work tracking, on-call — restricted to Microsoft sources in our runtime |
   | Dry-run with projected impact before commit (Grafana, Mezmo) | Shadow measurement and the preview step |
   | Governing AI spend as its own optimisation target (CloudZero) | The AI family (AI-04, AI-16, AI-20) |
   | Insuring the optimisation (Archera) | Related to the blameless-regret idea ([09 M-27](../ideation/09-outer-rims-moonshots.md)); real insurance is out of scope |

5. **Where vendors over-claim** (useful when we talk about ourselves): most "AI" is a read-only chat assistant or a rebranded deterministic or reinforcement-learning engine; "agentic" is often positioning by acquisition (Flexera's 2026 roll-up of ProsperOps and Chaos Genius); Datadog's Azure auto-actions are two; Komiser's "open source" is source-available. Our wording should keep the same discipline: *AI-composed, figures computed*.
6. **A third-party equivalent does not trigger kill criterion K4** (we cannot contribute to a proprietary product), but it is logged at the quarterly re-check and may reshape positioning ([plan/01 §5](../plan/01-assumptions-risks-decisions.md)).
