## 1. Verdict

**Mostly yes.** Read alone, both files are unusually disciplined idea-level writing: no technology is picked, there is a consistent "say what the evidence is, say what it isn't" ethic, and — checked specifically, as instructed — none of the retired vocabulary (*lab, exam, Terrarium, Move, Brief, mechanism-verified, commercially verified*) survives into either file. Trust is dented by one checkable factual overstatement sitting in the single most important competitive claim (10, vs. Azure Advisor), and by the fact that 09's own promise of "one vocabulary" is broken against its own named cross-reference set: "the ladder" means two different things between 01 and 09, and 09's rule that a claim never carries the bare word "verified" is broken by 07.

## 4. Fact checks

1. **Claim:** Azure Advisor's cost recommendations are "hand-written" (10:36). **Checked:** `learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/advisor/advisor-cost-recommendations` (page dated 2026-02-18, last updated 2026-04-01). **Result: wrong as stated.** Advisor's VM/VMSS resize and shutdown recommendations are explicitly machine-learning-driven ("Advisor uses machine-learning algorithms to identify low utilization and to identify the ideal recommendation"); other Advisor cost checks (e.g. unattached disks) are rule-based. "Hand-written" overstates the case for the feature most readers think of first.
2. **Claim (supporting context for 09/10's "list prices" framing):** Advisor's own shutdown/resize savings estimates use retail, not negotiated, rates. **Checked:** same URL. **Result: confirmed.** Resize savings are explicitly "based on retail rates" — consistent with, and supporting, 10's "estimated at list prices — your rates are invisible to me."
3. **Claim (supporting context for 09 §6 / 10's procurement scene):** buying a commitment (e.g. a savings plan) requires a specific billing role, distinct from ordinary subscription access. **Checked:** `learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/savings-plan/permission-buy-savings-plan` (updated 2026-10-29). **Result: confirmed.** Requires EA-admin-write, MCA billing-profile-contributor, or a subscription Owner/"Savings plan purchaser" role.
4. **Claim (implicit in 09's "Contract type" and throughout 10):** Enterprise Agreement, Microsoft Customer Agreement and pay-as-you-go remain current Azure commercial offer types. **Checked:** `learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/manage/view-all-accounts` (updated 2026-06-25). **Result: confirmed current**, no deprecation notice for any of the three.
