# Round 2 — Lens A (Architecture) — Raw Report

> Reviewed at commit `7baddcf` · model `gemini-4-argon` · date `2026-10-31`. Assumed: one EU Entra tenant, shared landing-zone connectivity hub, business-hours operations by a three-person crew (or single-operator mode initially).

## 1. Verdict — five lines

The Round-1 revisions fixed the major conceptual seams—the spine now calls leaves through the hub, Durable Functions replaces custom checkpointing, and Z3 splits authority across three parties—making the high-level architecture substantially stronger.
However, the large edit introduced **new mechanical contradictions** in the W4 execution path, the Cosmos container and RBAC tables, the `snet-exec` network rules, and the computed contract quotas.
It also inflated operational complexity (9 Key Vaults, 28–40 private endpoints, 12 contracts per environment, 19 drift layers, 20 detections, 15 runbooks) beyond what a crew of three can sustainably operate.
The neglected Well-Architected pillar is now **Operational Excellence** (with **Reliability** degraded by synchronous fail-closed coupling across six Z3/Z4 components).
**One thing that must change:** reconcile the W4 authority-chain mechanics (job-start parameter passing without template overrides, the missing `leases` store, internal `snet-exec` ingress, and spine queue-event paths) and collapse per-producer Key Vaults and excess contract splits into a single operable baseline.

### A-01 — Container Apps job dispatch with "no template" cannot pass `approval_id`, and parallel dispatch races the lease
**Critical · File and heading:** `<repo>\docs\design\07-security-architecture.md` `### 3.3 The execution identity: just-in-time, without PIM` (and `<repo>\docs\design\03-logical-architecture.md` `### A commitment purchase`).
**Quote:** `"its call carries no template, and any other start, or one with a template, raises D16"` and `"APR->>MVR: dispatch the runner job, fixed template, approval id only"`.
**Why it matters:** (Tasks 1, 2, 6 — R1 A-05/ADR-07; W4 trace.) In Azure Container Apps (`POST .../jobs/{jobName}/start`), the *only* request body is `JobExecutionTemplate`. Sending **no template** means `approval_id` cannot be passed in the start call. Nor can the runner query Cosmos DB: `07 §3.1` grants execution identities only `"container-scoped create on the start marker in \`approvals-index\` and read on \`approval-records\`"`—no read/query on `approvals-index` and no `leases` container in `06 §7.2`. Worse, `07 §3.3` starts the runner **in parallel** (`par dispatch by the Approval service`) with calling the broker, launching a second replica under the *same* active managed identity before the broker acquires the lease.
**Fix:** Acquire the lease in a declared `leases` container *before* job start, or trigger the runner as an event-driven job from a queue after the broker writes the lease and grant.
**Vendor:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/rest/api/resource-manager/containerapps/jobs/start?view=rest-resource-manager-containerapps-2026-07-01 — `"containers | JobExecutionContainer[] | List of container definitions for the Container Apps Job."`

### A-02 — `snet-exec` network rules and "no ingress" forbid the W4/W5 calls between Z3 services and the spine
**Critical · File and heading:** `<repo>\docs\design\04-landing-zone-and-citadel.md` `## 6. Network` (and `<repo>\docs\design\05-agents-and-orchestration.md` `## 4. How the spine runs`).
**Quote:** `"The broker, the runner and the Verifier have no ingress."` and `"senders hold *Storage Queue Data Message Sender* on that one queue"`.
**Why it matters:** (Tasks 1, 2 — R1 A-04; W4 trace.) Four W4/W5 paths fail under `04 §6` and `07 §3.1`: (1) `03 §2/§4` (`AP -->|"decision signal"| SP`) and `05 §4` require Approval service and runner to enqueue `decision` and `execution finished` events to the spine's Storage queue, but `04 §6` denies `snet-exec` outbound access to Storage and `07 §3.1` grants no Z3 identity `Storage Queue Data Message Sender`; (2) `07 §3.3` requires `APR->>BRK` and `RUN->>BRK`, needing internal ingress on the broker in `snet-exec`; (3) `03 §4` and `09 §4` show `MVR->>VER`, but Verifier has `"no ingress"` (`04 §6`, `07 §3.1`) and the runner `"cannot start a job"` (`07 §3.1`); (4) `09 §4` states `"the Policy service signs its decision first, with a key of its own, and the decision's id sits **inside the signed record**"`, and `07 §4` puts `"the **policy decision id**"` inside `target_hash` before approval, yet `03 §4` calls `APR->>POL` *after* approval and `04 §6` blocks pre-approval Policy calls.
**Fix:** Allow internal ingress to Policy service and broker in `snet-exec`, trigger Verifier via spine/Approval service, unify Policy ordering across `03 §4`, `05 §3`, and `09 §4`, and grant `snet-exec` outbound access plus `Storage Queue Data Message Sender` to the spine's event queue.

### A-03 — Compromised Approval service still completes unauthorized Moves by using Policy service and Broker as confused deputies
**Major · File and heading:** `<repo>\docs\design\07-security-architecture.md` `### 3.3 The execution identity: just-in-time, without PIM` (and `<repo>\docs\design\13-decisions.md` `### ADR-07 Execution identities are granted just in time by a broker on a signed approval`).
**Quote:** `"A compromised *Approval service* can sign — forge — an approval for anything the Policy service permits"` and `"Three parties, none able to complete a change alone."`
**Why it matters:** (Task 6 — Attack on ADR-07.) The claim in `03 §3`, `09 §4`, and `ADR-07` that no single Z3 party can complete a change is false for the Approval service. Because the Policy service `"reads the Move catalogue and the rules and credentials it evaluates, and **nothing about any estate**"` (`07 §3.1`) and does not authenticate humans, a compromised Approval service can request a signed policy decision for *any* catalogue Move on *any* action-onboarded resource, sign a fabricated approval with its own key, log it through the Audit Writer (bypassing D18), and drive both broker and runner to execute it. Without Entra ID P1/P2 (`07 §11`), `11 §7.1` admits D13 `"falls back to the Approval service's token record, which is weaker"`—written by the compromised service itself.
**Fix:** Store the human's Entra ID token (with `nonce` binding `target_hash`) in `approval-records` and require **both Credential broker and Move runner** to verify it directly against Entra's JWKS keys.

### A-04 — Immediate-return `POST /invocations` with a 2-minute idle timeout kills long-running leaves, and intermediate record writes wake the spine prematurely
**Critical · File and heading:** `<repo>\docs\design\05-agents-and-orchestration.md` `## 4. How the spine runs` and `## 5. Anatomy of a leaf` (and `<repo>\docs\design\09-safeguards-and-drift-controls.md` `## 5. The preview register and the dependency register`).
**Quote:** `"it returns **at once with a run id**"` and `"idle timeout **2 minutes** for batch-style roles, 15 for the Diplomat"`.
**Why it matters:** (Tasks 1, 2, 6 — R1 A-10/ADR-33; W4 trace.) Three platform interactions break leaf invocation: (1) On Foundry Hosted Agents, `"If no request arrives within that window, the platform deprovisions the compute and persists the session state"` (Learn). Because `POST /invocations` returns immediately (`05 §4`), the spine waits on a queue event rather than polling `GET /invocations/{id}`, and resilient tasks are disabled (`"the long-running APIs stay off, because the spine owns long-running work"`, `09 §5`), no request arrives during a 5-to-12-minute Detective run (`05 §4` `seconds: 300`; `tools/cost-model.py`: 12 active minutes)—so a 2-minute idle timeout (`05 §5`) evicts the container mid-run. (2) In `05 §1/§4`, the spine awaits a `"record written"` event, yet Detective writes multiple records per run (`"hypotheses, evidence, a *prediction*"`, `05 §2`), waking the spine on the first write; if a leaf fails before writing, the spine waits until the deadline expires. (3) `05 §5` specifies `0.5 vCPU / 1 GiB` and `2 minutes` idle timeout, whereas `tools/cost-model.py` lines 62/222 price `"1 vCPU and 2 GiB"` and `5` minutes idle timeout.
**Fix:** Have the activity await the synchronous `POST /invocations` response (or poll `GET /invocations/{id}`), emit one `run-completed` event at handler exit, and align `05 §5` with `tools/cost-model.py`.
**Vendor:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/agents/concepts/hosted-agents — `"If no request arrives within that window, the platform deprovisions the compute and persists the session state."`

### A-05 — Non-restored Durable Functions task hub loses in-flight case budgets and long timers, and contradicts `11 §8`
**Major · File and heading:** `<repo>\docs\design\06-data-and-knowledge.md` `### 7.6 Recovery` (and `<repo>\docs\design\11-delivery-and-operations.md` `## 8. Resilience and recovery`).
**Quote:** `"Spine state (the task hub) | **not restored**: a new task hub is created and the open cases are re-opened from the case store"` vs `11 §8`: `"their deterministic ids make the replay idempotent"`.
**Why it matters:** (Tasks 1, 6 — R1 A-10, A-12; ADR-33.) In Durable Functions, replay reads `<TaskHubName>History` in the task hub storage account (Learn). If the task hub is **not restored** (`06 §7.6`), a new task hub has *no* history to replay. Moreover, `05 §4` states per-case budget tracking is `"arithmetic on the orchestration's own state"`, while only group/contract budgets live in Cosmos DB (`budgets`). Discarding the task hub wipes every open case's accumulated spend and resets multi-day timers (W6 Lab waits, reminder ladders). Also, `05 §4` states `"A failed instance is **re-opened from the case store under a new instance id**"`, contradicting `11 §8`'s `"their deterministic ids"`.
**Fix:** Persist per-case cumulative spend and timer deadlines in `cases` or `budgets` in Cosmos DB, and update `11 §8` to describe reconstructing state from the case store under a new instance ID.
**Vendor:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/durable-task/durable-functions/durable-functions-azure-storage-provider — `"These history events replay into your orchestrator function code, restoring it to its previously checkpointed state."`

### A-10 — Synchronous fail-closed coupling across six components and 9 Key Vaults creates fragile availability and unverified public-key distribution
**Major · File and heading:** `<repo>\docs\design\03-logical-architecture.md` `## 6. Degraded modes` (and `<repo>\docs\design\07-security-architecture.md` `### 6.1 Secrets`).
**Quote:** `"**fails closed: no change without a ledger entry** — no approval is issued, no role granted"` and `"(a) **a vault of its own** — the default for the chain (Policy service, broker, runner, Verifier)"`.
**Why it matters:** (Task 3 — New SPOFs and coupling.) Three new single points of failure are not covered by `03 §6` or `09 §4`: (1) **Audit Writer SPOF:** Every step of Policy, Approval, Broker, Runner, and Verifier synchronously calls the single Audit Writer (`snet-aca`), which appends to one active append blob using `x-ms-blob-condition-appendpos` (`06 §7.3`). If the Audit Writer restarts, **even capturing a human's approval click fails (`"no approval is issued"`, `03 §6`)**, and in-flight Moves abort mid-execution—where the rollback step itself cannot run if it also requires a ledger entry. (2) **Six signing Key Vaults (`KEY_VAULTS = 9` in `tools/cost-model.py`):** A single Move depends synchronously on six vaults and six private endpoints, and `04 §6` admits `"how the public keys reach each party is *to confirm* in spike S-ID-3"`. (3) **Durable Functions storage account:** One storage account holds the task hub for all 11 workflows; storage throttling under 10-second polling + fan-out stalls every workflow simultaneously.
**Fix:** Persist signed human approvals in `approval-records` during an Audit Writer restart (blocking *dispatch/grant* until the ledger entry commits), exempt **rollback steps** from Audit Writer unavailability, and consolidate the four ledger-entry signing keys (`broker`, `runner`, `Verifier`, `Audit Writer`) into one vault with key-scoped RBAC.
**Vendor:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/key-vault/general/rbac-guide — `"Azure RBAC for key vault also allows users to have separate permissions on individual keys, secrets, and certificates."`

### A-11 — Over-engineering inflates the footprint beyond what a three-person crew can operate (Operational Excellence neglected)
**Major · File and heading:** `<repo>\docs\design\11-delivery-and-operations.md` `### 7.2 Support model` (and `<repo>\docs\design\04-landing-zone-and-citadel.md` `### 4.3 The access contracts`).
**Quote:** `"**The minimum crew is three roles**: the **operator**, a **second reviewer and approver** (a different person, so that the two-person rule can be met) and a **security reviewer**; one of the three is the sponsor of record."`
**Why it matters:** (Tasks 4, 5 — Over-engineering; neglected WAF pillar.) Counting artifacts across the revised design shows severe operational inflation for a 3-person crew: **30+ managed/agent identities** per environment; **9 Key Vaults** and **28–40 private endpoints** (`12 §2` / `tools/cost-model.py`, €180–€257/month idle); **6 subnets** and **3 Container Apps environments** per spoke; **12 Citadel access contracts per environment** (36 across dev/test/prod); **7 GitHub Actions workflows**, **6 Deployment Stacks**, **19 drift layers**, **20 detections (D1–D20)**, **15 runbooks (RB-01–RB-15)**, and **22 spikes (61 working days)**. Among the five Well-Architected pillars, **Operational Excellence is now neglected**: controls were layered on without a complexity budget, pushing key rotation, contract upkeep, and monthly chaos/drift drills past the 20–40 h/month labour seed in `12 §2`.
**Fix — what to cut and what it costs/saves:** (1) **Cut 5 of the 9 Key Vaults** (keep app, hub, Approval-signing, and 1 shared Policy/ledger-signing vault with key-scoped RBAC): saves **5 private endpoints + 5 Defender meters (~€33/month)** and 5× rotation toil (`RB-12`). (2) **Merge same-data-class contracts (`study` + `curate`; `survey` + `investigate`) at the APIM product level** (12 → 8 per environment): saves **12 contract stacks across 3 environments** with zero security loss (`04 §4.3` already enforces per-agent `azp` and `Tool.<name>`). (3) **Consolidate the 14 Python MCP tool services into 2 Container Apps** (`public-tools`, `estate-and-knowledge-tools`): cuts container build/deploy toil by >60%.

## 4. What Is Strong — What to Keep

1. **Honest platform engineering over marketing claims:** The revisions openly document Azure RBAC's 10-minute propagation delay (`07 §3.3`), ABAC delegation's inability to constrain target scope (`04 §3.1`, `07 §3.3`), Content Safety's 1,000-character Foundry guardrail limit (`07 §5`), Sweden Central's AI Search high-demand block (`04 §3.2`), Deployment Stack resource-move bypasses (`09 §2.1`), and GitHub environment single-reviewer semantics (`11 §3`).
2. **Estate-clustered statistical evaluation protocol (`10 §4`):** Treating the *estate* as the unit of independence (`DE = 1 + (m − 1)·ρ`), pairing against both the previous release (`δ`) and a frozen anchor (`Δ`) to prevent non-inferiority chaining, and physically isolating the training, public, and credential exams (`10 §3`) is gold-standard evaluation design.
3. **Layered defence against prompt injection (`07 §5`):** Combining architectural read-only isolation (Layer 0), universal source-sanitiser chunking at 10,000 characters (Layer 1), APIM sliding-window response screening (Layer 2), session-level taint (Layer 4), and per-tool app roles keyed on verified `azp` (Layer 6) ensures no single guardrail failure compromises an estate.
4. **Recovery ordering that preserves erasures and revocations (`06 §7.6`, `RB-10`):** Restoring `controls` first, re-applying delete tombstones and the erasure register, and raising the `approval epoch` so pre-restore approvals cannot be replayed directly solves the resurrection flaws of naive change-feed replay.
5. **Reproducible, self-checking cost model (`12`, `tools/cost-model.py`):** Resolving live retail meters by strict selectors, separating marginal token cost from all-in cloud + labour cost (`Q-COST-1`), and shrinking Foundry from 9 projects per account to 2 (`04 §7`) keeps the financial model grounded.

## 5. What Is Missing — Five Most Important Omissions for Lens A

1. **A working parameter-passing and lease-acquisition protocol for the Move runner (`A-01`, `A-07`):** Because manual Container Apps jobs started with no template override accept no per-run arguments, and `06 §7.2` has no `leases` container, the mechanism by which a started runner replica discovers its `approval_id` and `lease_token` without racing another replica is missing.
2. **Internal communication topology inside `snet-exec` and outbound queue signaling to `snet-spine` (`A-02`):** `04 §6` denies ingress to Policy service, Credential broker, and Verifier and omits outbound access from `snet-exec` to the spine's Azure Storage event queue, leaving the W4/W5 authority chain and spine notifications unroutable.
3. **Cryptographic verification of the human's Entra token by the Broker and Runner (`A-03`):** Without Broker and Runner verification of an Entra-issued JWT, the Approval service remains a single point of compromise that can forge approvals and drive the other two parties.
4. **Long-running leaf execution lifecycle and task-hub state durability (`A-04`, `A-05`):** The design turns off `azure-ai-agentserver` resilient tasks (`09 §5`) and sets a 2-minute session idle timeout (`05 §5`) while making `POST /invocations` return immediately (`05 §4`), and discards the Durable Functions task hub on recovery (`06 §7.6`) where per-case budgets and multi-day timers live.
5. **Complete contract quotas (`12 §2`) and W10/Examiner/Conductor tool grants (`04 §4.4`) (`A-06`, `A-08`):** Four of the twelve contracts (`experiment`, `engineer`, `forge`, `examine-eu`) and two workflows (`W3b`, `W10`) are missing from `tools/cost-model.py`, and `04 §4.4` omits the tool roles needed by Examiner, Conductor, Skeptic, and W10.
