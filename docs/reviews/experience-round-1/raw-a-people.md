# Decision review: people, attention and trust

## 1. Verdict

Good: explicit approval, visible uncertainty, editable knowledge and permission to disagree form a strong foundation.  
Not ready: the ordinary path demands too much attention, while several promises contradict the actual cards.  
Adoption remains unproven: optimise for informed decisions and safe disagreement, not response or approval rates.

## 2. Top 5 changes I would make to the decisions

1. **Simplify C3, not its safety information (§7, X10).** The populated sample has **19 visible text blocks and 11 controls**, about 175 words before Details or input forms; native Teams labels/feedback add more. My ten-second prediction: “€710, shutdown, Choose A,” not the unresolved month-end job, approval boundary or undo deadline. Show one proposed change, scope, range/basis, unresolved risk, timing/undo; keep alternatives behind Details. Use “Review exact schedule,” “Keep as is,” “Later.” The job question must precede an unsafe shutdown option. **Cost:** less immediate comparison; another interaction for alternatives.

2. **Prioritise the fresh-plan R1 path (X3/X22).** Separate messages risk another interruption and lost context. Once freshness and approval binding are proven, show the complete, stored R1 plan initially with an explicit verb–object approval button. Never turn “Choose A” into hidden approval. Keep separate confirmation for changed plans and R2/R3. **Cost:** speculative planning; loses a second deliberation opportunity, not plan-bound authorisation. Until validated, say “Prepare a plan—nothing changes yet.”

3. **Budget interruptions, not Cases (X7).** Three new asks can generate reminders, read-backs, approvals, outcomes and another five-item desk. Consolidate routine receipts in the same thread/desk; count every unsolicited non-critical push. Adopt the pilot settings below, then measure burden and decision quality [S2–S5]. **Cost:** slower discovery of some savings; additional notification accounting.

4. **Replace disciplinary escalation with coverage routing (X7).** A directory manager is not necessarily a resource custodian. An opaque request still reveals unanswered work, especially in small teams. Owner wording: “Nothing changes without approval. I will park this on [date]; you can choose a delegate.” Custodian wording: “Ownership or cover needs confirming. Please route this decision; no action is authorised.” Use only appointed, entitled cover; managers only when explicitly appointed. **Cost:** fewer forced responses; ownership gaps need a real organisational queue.

5. **Make trust repair a designed transaction (J3/J5/J6/J12, X19).** Specify wrong advice, failed changes, first undo and recovery—not merely “I could not.” Say: “This recommendation was wrong. [Impact/status]. Further automatic changes here are paused. [Human contact] will update you by [time].” Distinguish restored, restoring and unknown. Report what changed in the system before inviting reuse [S6]. **Cost:** human incident capacity, recovery states and follow-through.

## 4. Decisions I agree with—and their strongest counterargument

- **X2:** one labelled AI voice prevents impersonation; repeated labels can still become invisible.
- **X4:** free text never approves; sign-in/channel friction may strand legitimate owners.
- **X8:** basis plus uncertainty prevents false comparison; chips can become undeserved authority signals.
- **X9:** inspectable Notes enable correction; expiry can resurrect previously rejected advice.
- **X12:** versioned templates enable testing; model comprehension cannot establish human comprehension.
- **X18:** no calendar/presence inference protects privacy; people forget to configure leave.
- **X19:** prompt receipts plus narrowing create control; an unread receipt is not meaningful advance awareness.
- **X20:** independent approvers reduce shared error; visible prior approval undermines independence.

## 5. What is missing entirely

J1–J8 name the journeys; their people-specific transition rules remain missing:

- **J1/J8, joiner:** verify responsibility, not identity or resource creation; show what is retained, who sees it, current permissions, channel preferences and a real human contact.
- **J8, leaver/mover:** invalidate pending personal approvals; transfer resource context with provenance, not private conversation; reset the successor’s introduction and attention settings.
- **J1/J8, contractor/shared mailbox:** distinguish delivery contact from named accountable approver; provide expiry, access-denied and individual claim/handoff paths. A mailbox must not become “the person.”
- **J4, absent/unowned:** appointed cover or custodian queue; no silent reassignment, implied consent or manager-chain pursuit. Return-from-leave reconciliation must not dump accumulated asks.
- **J3/J7, teaching:** confirm the exact resources/expiry, then show “Note saved; these recommendations changed.” Explain who can see it and how retained audit history differs from deletable context.
- **J5/J6, first failure/undo:** teach Undo before first approval; schedule within available cover. An expiring rollback must not vanish during quiet hours without an accessible recovery path. Include partial/failed undo and irreversible residual costs.
- **J7, team lead:** design a scoped capacity/ownership view, not an unanswered-person list. Suppress identifiable small-group response statistics; prohibit HR/performance reuse and expose what managers receive [S7].
- **Across journeys:** native-speaker tests for “Your OK,” “Challenge,” “Teach” and “Not mine”; neutral alternatives such as “Approve,” “Correct this,” “Add context,” “Not responsible.” Do not infer culture from names.

## 6. What I verified myself

Read the full spec, 15 templates/payloads and six SVG sources; counted C3 without hidden content/fallbacks. Read the named technical/idea documents.

- **[S1] [FinOps playbook](https://www.finops.org/wg/encouraging-engineers-to-take-action/):** confirms 40% action challenge; does not validate these attention defaults.
- **[S2] [Mark et al., 2008](https://ics.uci.edu/~gmark/chi08-mark.pdf):** original experiment found faster interrupted work with greater stress/effort, not a universal recovery-time rule.
- **[S3] [van der Sijs et al., 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1447540/):** confirms 49–96% overrides in clinical systems; not Azure engagement evidence.
- **[S4] [Parasuraman/Manzey, abstract via Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:21077562%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json):** experts and teams also show automation bias; training alone is insufficient.
- **[S5] [Nielsen response-time guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/):** one-second flow/ten-second attention heuristics; not a two-minute answer guarantee.
- **[S6] [Microsoft HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/):** covers initial interaction, inevitable errors and adaptation over time.
- **[S7] [BetrVG §87(1)(6)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html):** confirms employee-monitoring technology is a co-determination topic; local applicability needs review.

**Not verified:** live Teams/mobile/screen-reader rendering, actual ten-second comprehension, approval-code comparison behaviour, or any optimal numeric attention threshold. SVG inspection and this cognitive walkthrough are not user research. Publisher full text for S4 was unavailable; only its indexed abstract was checked.
