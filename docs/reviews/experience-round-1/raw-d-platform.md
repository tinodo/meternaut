# Review 1-D — Platform feasibility and accessibility

## 1. Verdict

The platform research (R1–R9) is careful, and most of its hard facts hold up under independent re-checking. But the cards built from it do not obey their own stated rule: nothing caps the *total* number of buttons one Teams message renders, so a populated Recommendation can show 11–14 live buttons — the opposite of "one decision, one minute." Two other load-bearing facts are unverified where it matters most (whether `Action.OpenUrl` opens the system browser on every Teams client, and what text a phone notification previews), and accessibility claims outrun what Adaptive Cards can structurally deliver.

## 2. Top 5 changes

1. **Cap total visible actions per rendered card, not just per `ActionSet`.** Why: Teams' own "six actions, then overflow" rule applies per action array (confirmed below) — spreading buttons across many small `ActionSet`s, as C3/C5 do, legally dodges the cap while still rendering 7–14 buttons. Cost: one new `cardlint.py` check (sum `Action.*` nodes across the body) plus a content rework — cap options at 3 (route a 4th to the page), fold "Not now"/"Not mine" into Challenge's reason chips, cutting ~4 buttons per card.
2. **Treat X25 (system browser, phishing-resistant step-up) as unverified until device-tested.** Why: no Learn page states what a bot-sent card's `Action.OpenUrl` does per Teams client; R4 already flags this gap. Cost: a half-day manual pass on Teams iOS/Android/desktop/web before launch; zero engineering, but a release gate.
3. **Ban inline images, SVG and charts in e-mail explicitly, in section 8 and the register.** Why: classic Outlook's Word engine drops `<svg>` outright and blocks remote images by default; nothing currently stops a future writer embedding the calibration scatter in a letter. Cost: one sentence.
4. **Set the Bot Framework `Activity.summary`/`text` on every send, not only the card's `fallbackText`.** Why: these are two different properties at two different protocol layers; only one of them is documented as the "channel cannot render cards" fallback, and neither is confirmed to drive a phone's toast/lock-screen preview. Cost: one extra string per send in the send step.
5. **Name retention for the Logs Ingestion custom table before trusting multi-quarter trends.** Why: the Analytics plan's default retention (31/90 days, R5) is shorter than the report card's own "learning delta frozen vs updated" and the quarterly commitment review (J13) need. Cost: a named per-GB/month line item, currently absent from the "negligible cost" claim.

## 4. Decisions I agree with, and the strongest reason each might still be wrong

- **X25** (page opens in the system browser): right in principle — Microsoft's own identity guidance says so — but the one Teams-specific fact this whole security chain depends on (what `Action.OpenUrl` does per client) is unverified anywhere, including by me.
- **X5** (server-rendered pages, no framework, Flex Consumption): right call for simplicity and cost, but "no declarative headers" and the step-up logic mean more hand-written code than "no client framework" suggests is being saved.
- **X11** (WCAG 2.2 AA and EN 301 549 everywhere): right as a bar, but Adaptive Cards structurally cannot reach it (flat headings, no landmarks) — claiming the *same* bar across Teams and the page overstates what a card can deliver next to a page.
- **X26** (review card = GitHub check run): right surface, but its 3-button cap and lack of a PR-native form mean the "ten-minute checklist" will spill into the mirrored comment, diluting the "visible without an extra click" advantage that justified picking it.

## 5. What is missing entirely

No journey covers a tenant that has not granted `TeamsAppInstallation.ReadWriteSelfForUser.All`, has no setup policy, or lacks Entra ID P1/P2 (contexts and strengths need it) — "Teams is the default channel" silently becomes "e-mail only" there, with no stated detection. No device/client test plan is named (Teams iOS, Android, desktop, web, each in light/dark/high-contrast, with a screen reader) despite Microsoft's own advice that Teams support for cards "slightly differs" — §16 names only the parser and five-person cold reads. No failure state exists for an R2/R3 approver who hits a dead approval page mid-flow (Pause's runbook only covers the platform team). No journey covers the partner contact (Gerrit) or a guest account in Teams — §7's self-install and proactive-message rules assume a licensed tenant member throughout.

## 6. What I verified myself, and what I could not verify

**Confirmed, with URL:** six primary actions per action array before overflow, behaviour differing for root `actions` vs. `ActionSet` (learn.microsoft.com/microsoftteams/platform/task-modules-and-cards/cards/cards-format#adaptive-cards-overflow-menu); a `ShowCard` submit collects its own inputs plus parent-card inputs, never sibling/child cards (learn.microsoft.com/adaptive-cards/authoring-cards/input-validation#fields-to-be-validated-and-submitted); `ShowCard`'s default `actionMode` is inline, generic default `maxActions` is 5 (learn.microsoft.com/adaptive-cards/rendering-cards/host-config#actionsconfig); `TextBlock.style:"heading"` has no level, and `Action.ToggleVisibility.tooltip` aids narration (learn.microsoft.com/microsoft-copilot-studio/adaptive-card-accessibility-tips); Flex Consumption supports built-in authentication (learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-functions/migration/migrate-plan-consumption-to-flex) and "always ready" instances remove cold starts at a cost (learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-functions/flex-consumption-plan#always-ready-instances); `AdaptiveCards.Templating` exists on NuGet (v2.0.6), the .NET analogue of the npm package named in §7; classic Outlook's Word engine does not render inline SVG (non-Microsoft, consistent with R2's "weak CSS" finding).

**Could not verify:** which Teams client(s) open `Action.OpenUrl` in the system browser versus an embedded view (R4 already flags this; nothing further found); whether a phone's notification preview comes from the card's `fallbackText`, the Activity's `summary`/`text`, or neither; whether in-place card replacement after a tap behaves the same on Teams mobile as on desktop.
