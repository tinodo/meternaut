# Cost and FinOps

**What this document answers.** What the system costs to run at three scales; what it pays when nobody is using it; what moves the cost; what it must save to pay for itself; and what the numbers leave out.

## How to read the numbers

Every figure in the block below is generated by `tools/cost-model.py` from a price snapshot (Azure Retail Prices API, euro, Sweden Central) and from seeded volumes (calls per case, dead ends per case, tokens per call, minutes per session), which the block lists. The prices are real list prices. The volumes are **seeds**, starting estimates replaced by measurement in proof point P10 ([roadmap](13-roadmap.md)). The three scenarios are sizing parameters, not forecasts. In these tables a case is a Case that reaches an owner; a dead end is a Case that ends before any message to an owner.

- **Watch:** 10 leads triaged per case, one `triage` call each (3,000 input tokens, 1,500 of them cached, 300 output).
- **Dead ends (seed):** investigations that end refuted, or parked before any message to an owner, number 1 for every case that reaches an owner. - **Study:** one `triage` call per public document (8,000 input, 500 output tokens); 15% of documents are relevant and get a decode (30,000 input, 10,000 of them cached, 3,000 output) and a challenge (10,000 input, 1,000 output); 30% of those also get a curation call (10,000 input, 1,500 output).
- **Estate refresh:** 0.8 `triage` calls a month per resource (5,000 input tokens, 2,000 of them cached, 500 output).
- **Sampled scoring:** 5% of cases, one judge call each (20,000 input, 1,000 output).
- **Evaluation:** 40 component cases per change (4 calls each of 20,000 input tokens, 12,000 of them cached, and 1,500 output); 30 full cases per release; 1,000 attacks per safety run, each a target call (3,000 input, 600 output tokens), an attacker call (1,500, 400) and an evaluator call (3,000, 200).
- **Model aliases:** `reason` is GPT-6.1 Sol, `triage` is GPT-6 Luna and `judge` is Mistral Large 3, a different model family from the one it grades.
- **Prompt caching:** a cached prefix is paid at the cache-write price once per session and at the cached-input price on the other calls of that session. A case opens one session per agent, the Watch and Study jobs open 30 sessions a month, the estate refresh 60, and each evaluation case is its own session.
- **Hosted-agent time:** 1.4 hours per case including idle tails; a Watch run takes 4 minutes (730 runs a month), a study document 3.5 minutes and an estate call 0.4 minutes.

## What the numbers say

- **The plumbing costs more than the thinking at small scale.** At Pilot size the idle floor — AI Search Basic, private endpoints, the always-ready Functions instance that Learn recommends for Durable Functions, the Durable state storage — is most of the Operate profile; production tokens are small. At Enterprise size production tokens and logging dominate. Cost per case falls as the floor is spread over more cases.
- **Evaluation is the largest variable cost of the dev environment**, and a large share of all token spend at Pilot size. It is kept small by running suites on change rather than continuously, on the Flex tier where it is available, with sampled production scoring ([testing and evaluation](08-testing-and-evaluation.md)).
- **The data boundary is a policy choice with a price.** Data Zone deployment for estate data costs about a fifth more per token than Global Standard on current models. Public documents and synthetic data use Global Standard.
- **The hub is shared platform cost** and not in the totals. An organisation without one would build one: the last sensitivity row prices a production gateway (API Management Standard v2; the cheaper Developer tier has no SLA), and most of the rest is the hub's always-on usage pipeline, which a fork can replace with log queries ([architecture](02-architecture.md)).
- **The largest fixed line is the Atlas search service at Team and Enterprise size; at Pilot size the private endpoints cost slightly more.** AI Search Basic is about a fifth of the Pilot total and bills while idle. An embedded index in the `tools` app (DuckDB full-text and vector search) would remove it; proof point P5 compares it with Basic, Serverless (preview) and Foundry IQ.
- **The experience layer adds no meter of its own.** The web pages run in the `approvals` app: one of the five on the "Functions on Flex Consumption" line, already behind a private endpoint, scaling to zero (an always-ready instance, if first loads are slow, would add a fixed line). Preferences are small records on the "Cosmos DB serverless" line. Report events, a sliver of a gigabyte a month in a Log Analytics custom table kept at least 13 months, are inside the "Log Analytics ingestion" line; retention past the 31 included days bills per gigabyte a month and is not modelled. Mail through Microsoft Graph has no per-message charge, and Workbooks have none of their own. Teams cards pass through Azure Bot Service, which connects the `bot` app to Teams; its standard channels, Teams among them, carry unlimited messages ([pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/pricing/details/bot-services/)).
- **Break-even is a threshold to beat, not a forecast.** It divides the cloud cost by all managed spend, and most of that spend cannot be saved: if a quarter can, the share to save is four times the figure above. Judge the system on *verified net savings*: savings the Auditor confirmed in the bill, less this cost, people's time (the hours above, at the organisation's own hourly rate) and the cost of wrong advice that was applied, such as a rollback or an incident. The report card tracks it ([testing and evaluation](08-testing-and-evaluation.md)).

## The levers

| Lever | What it does | What it trades |
|---|---|---|
| Scale to zero | Functions, Cosmos DB serverless and hosted agents bill only while in use | Cold starts; the one always-ready instance Learn recommends for Durable Functions is the exception |
| Detectors first | Cheap compiled checks nominate leads; the model works on novel or ambiguous cases | Detectors need back-testing, shadow runs and expiry |
| Small model first | `triage` handles volume; `reason` is escalated on objective signals | Needs evaluation to set the thresholds. The headline prices every Detective and Economist call on `reason`, so it is the high estimate for tokens; the sensitivity table shows the savings from keeping half on `triage` or using a cheaper `reason` tier, which evaluation decides |
| Flex and Batch | About half price for study, evaluation and consolidation | Flex is preview; latency; Global processing only |
| Prompt caching | Stable prefixes are read at a fraction of the input price | Cache writes cost more; breakpoints only on prefixes reused several times a session |
| Evaluation on change plus sampling | Suites run when something changes, not all the time | Slower detection of slow drift; covered by sampled production scoring |
| Platform-managed Foundry data | Removes Foundry's own Cosmos DB and Storage and two private endpoints | Agent data is not in the organisation's own resources (a parameter turns it on) |
| One search service, one vault, no registry | Fewer fixed meters | Shared blast radius for the search index; mitigated by scoped roles |
| Log Analytics table plans | Bulky tables on cheaper plans | Basic and auxiliary plans limit queries |
| Reuse the organisation's hub | No second gateway | Depends on the hub's roadmap |

## What the numbers leave out

- **People's time.** Owner time on cases and reviewer time on Atlas pull requests are given in hours, not money; learning one new service costs its Study tokens plus one pull request to review. Platform, FinOps and security time (the rota, the quarterly reviews and onboarding in [delivery and operations](09-delivery-and-operations.md); the device test, translation review and accessibility audits) is not modelled at all, and at Pilot size a single platform day a month can cost more than the whole cloud bill; the report card measures those hours ([testing and evaluation](08-testing-and-evaluation.md)). The cost of a wrong recommendation that is applied (a rollback, an incident) is not priced either; the report card counts regrets.
- **Licences.** Microsoft Entra P1 or P2, Microsoft 365, Agent 365 and Defender plans are the organisation's own ([goals and scope](01-goals-and-scope.md)); Defender for AI Services appears only as a sensitivity.
- **Foundry's own evaluation meters.** The model assumes the default path: PyRIT and our own graders, billed as tokens. Safety evaluations, red-team runs and agent-playground evaluations bill on their own meter ([pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/pricing/details/foundryobservability/)); they are not in the model, and the playground ones, on by default in Foundry, are switched off here ([testing and evaluation](08-testing-and-evaluation.md)).
- **Landing-zone services.** Private DNS zones and the monitor private-link scope belong to the adopting organisation's landing zone.
- **A deployment runner inside the network.** Deploying to a private Foundry account needs a runner that can reach it ([delivery and operations](09-delivery-and-operations.md)); its cost depends on the organisation's choice.
- **A second region.** Recovery is by redeployment in phase 1; a standing second region is phase 5.
- **Free grants** (Functions, Log Analytics, Key Vault, the semantic ranker) are applied; taxes, support plans and negotiated discounts are not.
- **Shadow investigations.** The weekly sample of resources a Detector did not flag, investigated in shadow and graded ([knowledge and learning](04-knowledge-and-learning.md)), is not priced: its size is not set, and each one costs about as much as a dead end.

## The solution watches its own bill

The platform is onboarded as one more estate. It reads its own cost the way it reads any other: Azure budgets and anomaly alerts raise a warning, and because budgets only alert, the hard stops are the hub's token quotas per contract, the bounds on meters that are not tokens, and a run-rate trip in the workflows that flips the global kill switch ([safeguards and drift](07-safeguards-and-drift.md)). Cost per case is computed from hub logs, so a regression in tokens per case shows up as drift ([technology register](11-technology-register.md) lists the pricing behaviour of each service).
