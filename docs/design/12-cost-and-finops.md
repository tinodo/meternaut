# Design 12 — Cost and FinOps

> **What this answers:** *What does the system cost to run at three scales, where does the money go, which parts are always on and why, and how is the system's own spend governed?* The numbers are not typed into this document: they are produced by [`tools/cost-model.py`](../../tools/cost-model.py) from a reproducible [price snapshot](cost/prices-snapshot.json), so a changed price list changes the document, not our memory of it. Cost is a design constraint ([C7](01-requirements-and-principles.md#1-constraints--what-is-not-negotiable), [Q-COST-1](01-requirements-and-principles.md#4-quality-targets)); the choices it shaped are in [13](13-decisions.md). Part of the [design](README.md).

---

## 1. How the model works

1. **Prices.** `tools/fetch-prices.py` reads the Azure Retail Prices API with the queries in [`cost/price-queries.json`](cost/price-queries.json) and writes the [snapshot](cost/prices-snapshot.json): about 2,500 meters in euros and dollars, for Sweden Central, West Europe, North Europe and global meters, plus allowances that only a pricing page states, each **checked against the pricing page it came from**. The method and its checks are in the [price research](research/prices.md).
2. **Selectors, not memory.** Every line of the model names its meter with an exact selector. A selector that matches nothing, or matches two different prices, **stops the run** — so a renamed meter can never silently change a number.
3. **Seeds.** The quantities — cases a month, tokens per call, hours of compute — are **seeds** in the script, chosen to be plausible and *wrong in the useful direction* (generous). Spike **S-COST-1** replaces them with measurements ([14](14-next-phases.md#3-spike-register)).
4. **Scenarios** are those of [A6 in 01](01-requirements-and-principles.md#2-assumptions): **Pilot** (5 subscriptions, about 500 resources, 5 people), **Team** (50, about 10,000, 100) and **Enterprise** (1,000, about 200,000, 2,000).
5. **Reproducible and checked.** `python tools/cost-model.py --check docs/design/12-cost-and-finops.md` fails if the table below is stale; the pipeline runs it with the release gate for cost (G6, [10](10-test-and-evaluation.md#4-release-gates)).

**List prices.** These are retail prices. Enterprise-agreement or customer price levels, commitments and credits will be lower; the system itself learns the operator's real price levels ([02 of the north star](../02-learning-azure.md#9-learning-the-commercial-reality)) and can re-price its own bill.

## 3. Reading the numbers

**Tokens dominate beyond the pilot; the private-network floor dominates below it.** At Team scale tokens are about two-thirds of the bill and at Enterprise scale more than four-fifths; hosted compute is a small line in every scenario. At Pilot scale a third of the money is the *floor* of a private design — private endpoints, a Premium registry, a search service — that is paid whether or not a single case is open.

**Evaluation costs as much as production.** At Pilot scale the exams and release gates cost *more than the cases they protect*, because synthetic exams run regardless of how many real cases exist. This is the system's largest discretionary lever, and it is why the design says: a small exam nightly *and only when something changed*, the full exam weekly and at release, judge prompts with a static prefix so caching applies, composite evaluators to cut judge input by about two-thirds, and Flex or Batch deployments for the synthetic estates ([10 §9](10-test-and-evaluation.md#9-platform-mapping)).

**One case costs well under a euro.** On the strongest tier in the EU Data Zone a whole case — Detective, Skeptic, Economist, Diplomat and triage — costs about €0.63; the Detective alone is two-thirds of it. The Skeptic is cheap because its judge model is from another family at a lower price, so independence is not a luxury here.

**Residency has a price, and it is small.** Running the same case on a Global deployment would cost about 16 % less; that difference is what **C9** costs, and it is paid only on estate and personal data because the hub routes public work to cheaper deployment types ([ADR-09](13-decisions.md#adr-09-models-are-aliases-and-the-data-class-chooses-the-deployment-type)).

**Caching helps less than people expect** in this workload (a few percent), because most of a case's input is fresh evidence; the *judge* side, whose prompts are static, is where it pays.

**Break-even is a small share of what is monitored.** To cover its own cost the system must verify savings of about **3 %** of monitored spend at Pilot scale, **0.45 %** at Team scale and **0.2 %** at Enterprise scale. The claim of Q-COST-1 — after the first quarter the all-in cost is below the savings the system has *verified* — is therefore a claim about measurement discipline more than about ambition, and the system tracks it from week one as **cost per verified euro saved**.

## 5. Levers, in order

Estimates use the model; each is a decision with an owner, not a wish.

| # | Lever | Effect | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Share a hub** rather than deploying one | −€440 to −€1,200 a month | the landing zone offers one |
| 2 | **Trim the hub's usage pipeline** if we run it | up to −€320 a month | a fork change |
| 3 | **Run exams only when something changed**; Flex and caching on the rest | about a third to a half of exam spend | path filters in the pipeline; exam still runs weekly and at release |
| 4 | **Terra instead of Sol** for the investigation tier | about −48 % per case | the exam shows no loss of quality (spike S-MOD-1) |
| 5 | **A cheaper shape for dev** | −€150 to −€250 a month | spike S-NET-2 |
| 6 | **Serverless AI Search** | −€65 a month | general availability with private indexing |
| 7 | **Reuse the landing zone's firewall** | −€800 a month versus a spoke-local one | the landing zone has one |
| 8 | **Log content capture off; Basic and Auxiliary table plans** | a large share of ingestion | already the default |
| 9 | **Route public work to Global, Flex, Batch** | avoids the 20 % premium on that work | already the design |
| 10 | **Typed query tools instead of model-written code** | no sandbox bill; fewer tokens | already preferred |

## 6. How the system's own spend is governed

Five layers, from the one nearest the work to the one furthest from it — each *independent* of the others:

1. **The envelope** of every invocation: tokens, euros, seconds and tool calls ([05 §4](05-agents-and-orchestration.md#4-how-the-spine-runs)).
2. **The agent's middleware**: stops the loop at the envelope's limits.
3. **The contract at the hub**: token limits per minute and per month, with `estimate-prompt-tokens` on so a limit is a *hard* budget ([04 §4.3](04-landing-zone-and-citadel.md#43-the-access-contracts)). This is the real cap: Azure budgets evaluate once a day on data eight to twenty-four hours old and stop nothing.
4. **Detection**: spend or tokens per contract above three times the trailing median raises D4 ([07 §10](07-security-architecture.md#102-detections-we-ship-as-code)); Azure budgets alert as a backstop.
5. **The Agenda**: the platform's own cost is the *first* item the Conductor ranks (FR-X4), with the question "what would this cost less if it were done differently?"

**Unit economics, tracked weekly:** cost per case; **cost per verified euro saved**; tokens as a share of cost; evaluation cost as a share of cost; the always-on floor; the break-even share of monitored spend. The **FinOps Framework** structure applies to ourselves: *Understand* (allocation by contract, agent, case and environment, from the hub's dimensions and tags), *Quantify* (the unit economics above), *Optimise* (the levers of §5), and *Manage the practice* (the weekly cost report is a pipeline output, not a meeting).

## 7. What the model leaves out

GitHub minutes and storage; the landing zone's shared services (connectivity, firewall, DNS, SIEM); Microsoft 365, Teams and Exchange licences; Agent 365 and other optional licences (priced in the table above on their own); data egress and peering (cents per gigabyte, small here); model deployments of partner families that the price API does not list (a Claude model is billed through the Marketplace); voice, SMS and avatar modules (off by default); reserved capacity and committed-use discounts; and people's time. Taxes and negotiated discounts change the absolute numbers, not the proportions.

## 8. Keeping it true

The snapshot is refreshed monthly by re-running `tools/fetch-prices.py` and reviewing the diff; the model is re-run, and `--check` fails the build if this document is stale. Seeds are replaced by measurements as soon as the first hundred synthetic cases have run on the pinned stack (S-COST-1), and every phase exit in [14](14-next-phases.md#4-phases) reports *measured* cost per case next to the model's.
