# Design 12 — Cost and FinOps

> **What this answers:** *What does the system cost to run at three scales, where does the money go, which parts are always on and why, and how is the system's own spend governed?* The numbers are not typed into this document: they are produced by [`tools/cost-model.py`](../../tools/cost-model.py) from a reproducible [price snapshot](cost/prices-snapshot.json), so a changed price list changes the document, not our memory of it. Cost is a design constraint ([C7](01-requirements-and-principles.md#1-constraints--what-is-not-negotiable), [Q-COST-1](01-requirements-and-principles.md#4-quality-targets)); the choices it shaped are in [13](13-decisions.md). Part of the [design](README.md).

---

## 1. How the model works

1. **Prices.** `tools/fetch-prices.py` reads the Azure Retail Prices API with the queries in [`cost/price-queries.json`](cost/price-queries.json) and writes the [snapshot](cost/prices-snapshot.json): about 2,500 meters in euros and dollars, for Sweden Central, West Europe, North Europe and global meters, plus allowances that only a pricing page states, each **checked against the pricing page it came from**. The method and its checks are in the [price research](research/prices.md).
2. **Selectors, not memory.** Every line of the model names its meter with an exact selector. A selector that matches nothing, or matches two different prices, **stops the run** — so a renamed meter can never silently change a number.
3. **Seeds.** The quantities — cases a month, tokens per call, hours of compute — are **seeds** in the script, chosen to be plausible and *wrong in the useful direction* (generous). Spike **S-COST-1** replaces them with measurements ([14](14-next-phases.md#3-spike-register)).
4. **Scenarios** are those of [A6 in 01](01-requirements-and-principles.md#2-assumptions): **Pilot** (5 subscriptions, about 500 resources, 5 people), **Team** (50, about 10,000, 100) and **Enterprise** (1,000, about 200,000, 2,000). Each scenario also fixes the number of **private Foundry accounts** (Pilot 2, Team 3, Enterprise 4). Each account brings its own standard-setup trio — an AI Search service, a Cosmos DB account and a Storage account — so **each is priced as a floor of its own**; accounts are created in the phase that needs them ([14 §4](14-next-phases.md#4-phases)), never in advance.
5. **Quotas come from the same plan.** The model lists every token workload with its contract group and environment, prices it, and derives each group's monthly quota from the same list (plan × 1.5); the hub's caps in [04 §4.3](04-landing-zone-and-citadel.md#43-the-access-contracts) are therefore *outputs* of the model, not seeds typed next to it. `--check` fails if a quota would block planned work or the summed cap exceeds twice the plan.
6. **Reproducible and checked.** `python tools/cost-model.py --check docs/design/12-cost-and-finops.md` fails if the table below is stale or a quota diverges from the plan; the pipeline runs it with the release gate for cost (G6, [10](10-test-and-evaluation.md#4-release-gates)).

**List prices.** These are retail prices. Enterprise-agreement or customer price levels, commitments and credits will be lower; the system itself learns the operator's real price levels ([02 of the north star](../02-learning-azure.md#9-learning-the-commercial-reality)) and can re-price its own bill. **Unverified inputs** are marked in the line notes: the Language PII rate (the vendor page rendered no amount), whether hosted-agent idle time is billed (priced as billed), the sandbox's hour rounding, the red-teaming token volumes, and whether orchestrator replays on Flex Consumption bill more executions than the 150 a case the model counts (S-ORCH-1).

## 3. Reading the numbers

**Tokens are the largest line from Team scale upward; below that, the private-network floor is.** The shares line under the table gives the split: at Pilot scale half of the money is *fixed and platform* — private endpoints, one AI Search service for each private Foundry account, a Premium registry — paid whether or not a single case is open; at Team scale tokens are the largest group and at Enterprise scale nearly two-thirds of the bill. Hosted compute grows with cases (sessions are billed for their idle timeout too, priced here as billed because the pricing page is silent) and the sandbox, the Durable Functions spine and the Container Apps tool services are small beside it.

**Evaluation costs more than production at the pilot, about as much at Team scale and a tenth at Enterprise scale.** Exams, release gates and red teaming cost about four times what the cases they protect cost at Pilot scale, because synthetic exams run regardless of how many real cases exist. This is the system's largest discretionary lever, and it is why the design says: a small exam nightly *and only when something changed*, the full exam weekly and at release, judge prompts with a static prefix so caching applies, composite evaluators to cut judge input by about two-thirds, and Flex (a per-request tier on Global Standard, preview) for the synthetic estates, with Batch once it has an adapter ([10 §9](10-test-and-evaluation.md#9-platform-mapping)). Red-teaming volumes are an unverified seed until the first scans are measured; the self-hosted harness pays the same tokens as the cloud agent.

**A case's marginal tokens cost well under a euro; the all-in cloud cost per case is a different number.** On the strongest tier in the EU Data Zone a whole case — Detective, Skeptic, Economist, Diplomat and triage — costs about €0.44 of tokens when the cache is written once per session, about €0.63 if it is written on every call; the Detective alone is nearly two-thirds of it. The Skeptic is cheap because its judge model is from another family at a lower price, so independence is not a luxury here. Dividing *every* cloud line by the cases gives about €8, €2 and €0.9 at the three scales: that is the number that must fall as cases per subscription grow, and it is what the claim in [Q-COST-1](01-requirements-and-principles.md#4-quality-targets) is about.

**Residency has a price, and it is small.** Running the same case on a Global deployment would cost about 16 % less; that difference is what **C9** costs. For the GPT-6 family the Data Zone list price is about 20 % above Global (about 10 % on earlier models, and it varies by meter), and it is paid only on estate and personal data because the hub routes public work to cheaper deployment types ([ADR-09](13-decisions.md#adr-09-models-are-aliases-and-the-data-class-chooses-the-deployment-type)).

**How much caching helps depends on how cache writes are billed.** Written once per session, as [05 §7](05-agents-and-orchestration.md#7-the-token-economy) assumes, caching takes a case from about €0.68 to about €0.44; written on every call it saves only about 6 %. Spike S-COST-1 measures which is true. The *judge* side, whose prompts are static, benefits either way, and cache lifetime is minutes, so a prefix is not "written once per release".

**Break-even depends on what is counted, and the denominator is a seed.** Counting only the cloud bill, the system must verify savings of about 4 % of monitored spend at Pilot scale, 0.5 % at Team scale and 0.2 % at Enterprise scale. Counting named labour changes the Pilot story completely: at 40 hours a month the all-in break-even at Pilot scale is nearly a fifth of monitored spend, so **a pilot proves capability, not payback**; from Team scale it is about 1.3 % and at Enterprise scale a quarter of a percent (labour table above). The monitored-spend figures are seeds until the first estate's cost export replaces them, and the system tracks the claim from week one as **cost per verified euro saved**, with cloud and all-in side by side ([Q-COST-1](01-requirements-and-principles.md#4-quality-targets); labour is a named, operator-declared number of hours at a declared rate, assumption A8).

## 5. Levers, in order

Estimates use the model; each is a decision with an owner, not a wish.

| # | Lever | Effect | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Share a hub** rather than deploying one | −€440 to −€1,200 a month | the landing zone offers one |
| 2 | **Trim the hub's usage pipeline** if we run it | up to −€320 a month | a fork change |
| 3 | **Run exams only when something changed**; Flex and caching on the rest | about a third to a half of exam spend | path filters in the pipeline; exam still runs weekly and at release |
| 4 | **Terra instead of Sol** for the investigation tier | about −46 % per case | the exam shows no loss of quality (spike S-MOD-3) |
| 5 | **A cheaper shape for dev** | about −€95 a month for each account that needs no private trio (AI Search €65, four private endpoints €26, Cosmos DB and Storage) | spike S-NET-2, extended to production |
| 6 | **Create environments in the phase that needs them** | the same about €95 a month for each of the test and Lab accounts until their phase | staged creation ([14 §4](14-next-phases.md#4-phases)) |
| 7 | **Reuse the landing zone's firewall** | −€803 a month versus a spoke-local Standard firewall | the landing zone has one |
| 8 | **Log content capture off; Basic and Auxiliary table plans** | a large share of ingestion | already the default |
| 9 | **Route public work to Global, Flex, Batch** | avoids the Data Zone premium (about 20 % on the GPT-6 family) on that work | already the design |
| 10 | **Typed query tools instead of model-written code** | removes the sandbox line and the tokens of writing the code | already preferred |
| 11 | **Drop the always-ready instance of the spine** | −€21 a month per instance | accept a cold start of seconds on a spine step (Learn recommends the instance; S-ORCH-1 measures the effect) |
| 12 | **Shorter idle timeouts for hosted agents** | cuts the idle part of every session (idle is billed here because the page is silent) | spike S-COST-1 measures what is billed |
| 13 | **Semantic ranker only where it earns its price** | up to the whole semantic-ranker line (about €23 at Team scale, about €400 at Enterprise scale) | the exam shows no retrieval gain without it ([06 §7.5](06-data-and-knowledge.md#75-atlas-retrieval-and-vectors)) |

Serverless AI Search is **not** on this list as a dated saving: it is preview, has no SLA and no shared private links, so it can serve at most a development Atlas index, not the standard setup of a private account.

## 6. How the system's own spend is governed

Five layers, from the one nearest the work to the one furthest from it — each *independent* of the others:

1. **The envelope** of every invocation: tokens, euros, seconds and tool calls ([05 §4](05-agents-and-orchestration.md#4-how-the-spine-runs)). Before each leaf call the spine **reserves the call's worst-case cost atomically** against the case's and the group's budget and reconciles when the hub's usage record arrives, so a case cannot start work it has not been allowed to pay for.
2. **The agent's middleware**: stops the loop at the envelope's limits.
3. **The contract at the hub**: token limits per minute and per month, with `estimate-prompt-tokens` on ([04 §4.3](04-landing-zone-and-citadel.md#43-the-access-contracts)). This is the real cap, but **not an exact one**: `llm-token-limit` can be overrun by the requests that are in flight when the counter trips, so the residual overrun is bounded by the concurrency of a contract times its largest call, and the quotas are *computed* from the plan (`tools/cost-model.py --quotas`) rather than typed. Azure budgets evaluate once a day on data eight to twenty-four hours old and stop nothing.
4. **Detection**: spend or tokens per contract above three times the trailing median raises D4 ([07 §10](07-security-architecture.md#102-detections-we-ship-as-code)); Azure budgets alert as a backstop.
5. **The Agenda**: the platform's own cost is the *first* item the Conductor ranks (FR-X4), with the question "what would this cost less if it were done differently?"

**Unit economics, tracked weekly:** cost per case (marginal tokens and all-in, kept apart); **cost per verified euro saved**, cloud and all-in with named labour side by side ([Q-COST-1](01-requirements-and-principles.md#4-quality-targets)); tokens as a share of cost; evaluation cost as a share of cost; the always-on floor; the break-even share of monitored spend. The **FinOps Framework** structure applies to ourselves: *Understand* (allocation by contract, agent, case and environment, from the hub's dimensions and tags), *Quantify* (the unit economics above), *Optimise* (the levers of §5), and *Manage the practice* (the weekly cost report is a pipeline output, not a meeting).

## 7. What the model leaves out

GitHub minutes and storage; the landing zone's shared services (connectivity, firewall, DNS, SIEM); Microsoft 365, Teams and Exchange licences; **Entra ID P1 or P2**, which the Auditor's cross-check of approvals against the sign-in log (detection D13) needs for the Graph sign-in log, and which is a licence dependency, not a meter; Agent 365 and other optional licences (priced in the table above on their own); internet egress and Key Vault operations (cents); peering is a line, but only to a hub in another region; model deployments of partner families that the price API does not list (a Claude model is billed through the Marketplace); voice, SMS and avatar modules (off by default); reserved capacity and committed-use discounts; AI Search features we do not use (agentic retrieval). **People's time is not a line** but a sensitivity: the labour table in §2 shows the all-in break-even for 20 to 160 hours a month at a stated rate. Taxes and negotiated discounts change the absolute numbers, not the proportions.

## 8. Keeping it true

The snapshot is refreshed monthly by re-running `tools/fetch-prices.py` and reviewing the diff; the model is re-run, and `--check` fails the build if this document is stale or a quota has drifted from the plan. Seeds are replaced by measurements as soon as the first hundred synthetic cases have run on the pinned stack (S-COST-1), and every phase exit in [14](14-next-phases.md#4-phases) reports *measured* cost per case next to the model's.
