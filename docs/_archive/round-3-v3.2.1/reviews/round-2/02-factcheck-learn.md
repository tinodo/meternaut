## (c) Relevant Microsoft capabilities or changes the package misses

1. The reservation exchange cutoff on 1 Feb 2027 ("one final exchange"), the unchanged savings-plan trade-in route, and unchanged ISF. This bears directly on the commitment playbooks and the R3 treatment of exchanges.
2. `advisor_recommendation_update` (state write-back) and `advisor_remediation_get`. The latter carries destructive/reversible/confidence/effort flags that overlap the package's reversibility classes and playbook anatomy, so it is a "ride" candidate. It also bears on spike S2 (dismissal state is writable). Service Groups are exposed via `advisor_servicegroupintelligence_get`.
3. **SRE Agent economics.** Learn: always-on is "4 AAUs per agent-hour", plus token-based active flow. RPA meter "SRE Agent Unit" is USD 0.10 per AAU in East US (effective 2026-01-01). That is about USD 0.40/h, roughly USD 292/month per agent before tokens. The trial (up to 30 days) waives the always-on charge. This matters if the SRE Agent is the "optional execution runtime".
4. **Budgets now include reservation purchase data** (budgets tutorial, "Costs in budget evaluations"). Budget→action-group "cost brake" automation can therefore fire on commitment purchases.
5. **PTU and reservations.** A reservation stays at its original PTU quantity after a scale-down, so scaling down does not cut reserved cost. Scale-up depends on capacity.
6. **Log Analytics.** There is a 6-hour undo window on commitment tiers, and the real discount range runs ~15% to ~36%. The RPA also shows a Sentinel "50 GB Commitment Tier" meter (effective 2025-10-01), while the Sentinel billing page says tiers start at 100 GB/day. I could not reconcile these. The Sentinel meters "Data lake ingestion" and "Classic Auxiliary Logs Analysis" are both €0.0572/GB.
7. Minor: NAT Gateway StandardV2 (same price); the Sentinel "only used by Azure Monitor" observation.
