## Verdict

1. **Strong ideation:** the package is unusually candid about uncertainty and already anticipates many real governance failures.
2. **Not yet a coherent safety contract for write-capable autonomy:** L5 commitments, L3 silence, and R3 telemetry handling create routes around meaningful human approval.
3. **The main control gap is indirect influence:** tags, mutable thresholds, shared models/data, and evidence can change outcomes without formally changing a guardrail.
4. **Privacy, tenancy, audit retention, and responsibility need enforceable invariants — not only excellent questions.**
5. **Priority:** treat Critical/High findings as blockers before presenting policy autopilot, "safe one-tap", or cost brakes as trustworthy product commitments.

## Contradictions between documents

1. **L5 commitments:** the vision permits L5 commitment portfolios, while doc 06 limits R3 to L1–L2/two-key and doc 08 requires named approval and rollback.
2. **Hard stops:** doc 06 calls locks/legal holds/protected tags "hard stops", but doc 08 and the FAQ allow a "recorded exception".
3. **Owner-first:** the catalog says "No action without a confirmed owner", while doc 07 permits a quarantine ladder after silence.
4. **Undo claims:** doc 06 correctly classifies log dropping, retention reduction and commitments as R3; the FAQ nevertheless says "Everything has an undo window."
5. **Numeric provenance:** the vision says every number is computed, never generated; the cards in docs 05 and 03 label numerical card content "AI-generated". Use "AI-explained; calculation/evidence available" instead.
6. **No urgent bypass:** the brake is described as a separate narrow policy, but its timeout behaviour in doc 05 still needs explicit precedence over incident freezes and protected scopes.
7. **No-blame culture:** M-27 promises no team penalty, while the FAQ assigns accountability to the individual approver.

## What the package does especially well — keep

- It correctly treats static IP release, retention reduction, ingestion drops, redundancy reduction and commitments as materially irreversible/economically irreversible.
- The log-ingestion deep dive is excellent: audit blind spots, security consumers, shadow measurement, replay and protected sources are unusually thoughtful.
- The P2S example foregrounds shared ownership, a maintenance window, stale-approval checks and post-change monitoring.
- The deterministic-numbers / typed-tools / human-authored-policy spine is the right foundational direction.
- Read-back confirmation, AI disclosure, accessible channels, attention budgets and the explicit rejection of dark patterns are strong human-centred choices.
- The GDR structure and the insistence that a guardrail needs an owner, an enforcement mechanism and a test are excellent foundations.

## Questions the operator must answer

1. Are commitment purchases, exchanges, renewals and cancellations **permanently excluded** from L3–L5?
2. What exact affirmative enrolment makes L3 compatible with HITL, and when is silence never acceptable?
3. Which authority may accept stewardship of an orphan, and can it authorise quarantine or only escalation?
4. Which exact actions qualify as a cost brake, and which constraints can **never** be bypassed?
5. Which tags are control-plane inputs, and who may change them?
6. Is v1 strictly customer-owned single-tenant deployment, including all model prompts, traces, memories and audit data?
7. Who owns the trust root and emergency revocation process for playbooks, models, prompts and evaluators?
8. What organisation-level RACI, liability posture and incident process replaces "the human approver is accountable"?
