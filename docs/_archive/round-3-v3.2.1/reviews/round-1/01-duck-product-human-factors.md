## Verdict

1. This is an unusually strong ideation package: it has a real product thesis, not a feature dump or "AI chatbot" veneer.
2. Two **Critical** claims are unsafe as written: the vWAN P2S sizing model is factually wrong, and the promise of universal undo conflicts with irreversible actions.
3. The "last mile" thesis is plausible, but **"detection is mostly solved"** overstates the evidence and blurs a core differentiator.
4. The communication design is thoughtful, yet silence-based escalation, individual accountability language, and cost-emergency automation could erode the trust it seeks to create.
5. The guardrail-first / Wizard-of-Oz / thin-slice sequence is sound, but the pilot and catalog need sharper decision rules to produce credible evidence rather than a polished demo.

## Contradictions between documents

1. **Confirmed ownership vs silence-based retirement:** the catalog says "No action without a confirmed owner", while the orphan flow reaches restriction, stop and deletion after silence.
2. **Undo promise vs R3 reality:** the customer promise and FAQ say everything is undoable, while the action model correctly identifies irreversible data, retention, redundancy, address and commitment changes.
3. **Detection solved vs novel detection:** the headline says detection is mostly solved, while both seed examples depend on missing usage/usefulness detection and synthesis.
4. **Respectful attention vs escalation pressure:** "mute", "don't nag", "never shame" conflict with escalating a silent individual into team and manager channels.
5. **HITL language vs actual autonomy:** L3 veto-by-silence and L4/L5 policy autonomy may be defensible, but they are human-*on/over*-the-loop, not per-action HITL. Define the distinction plainly in public-facing language.
6. **Scope cut vs nothing dropped:** Phase 1 promises an H1 scope cut, while the plan says every idea has a home and nothing is dropped. A credible product needs a visible kill/park decision.

## What the package does especially well — keep

- The **Case** is an excellent product primitive: evidence, challenger, owner, decision, execution, verification and sustainment form a coherent long-running workflow.
- The **deterministic spine** is the right answer to "FULL AI": AI reasons and communicates; calculations, gates, identities and writes stay constrained.
- The log deep dive is notably mature in recognising evidence horizons, unknown consumers, protected sources, replay, shadowing and R3 loss.
- The interaction concepts are far more humane than typical alerting proposals: progressive disclosure, accessibility, language, AI disclosure, quiet hours and a "talk to a human" path.
- The staged plan — guardrails, human validation, playbook specification, feasibility, then thin slices — is disciplined and avoids premature architecture.
- The research's **Verified / Partial / Unverified** labelling and evidence map are valuable habits worth preserving.

## Questions the operator must answer

1. Is the first product bet **workflow closure**, **novel usage intelligence**, or deliberately both — and how will success distinguish them?
2. Who is the named organisational custodian permitted to decide about a genuinely orphaned resource, and what actions are forbidden without an identified service owner?
3. Is policy-authorised L3/L4 autonomy acceptable under the project's definition of HITL, and which classes are permanently limited to advise or explicit per-action approval?
4. What is the hard rule for public/team escalation, mute, and manager involvement so that cost optimisation does not become employee pressure or surveillance?
5. Will the Wizard-of-Oz pilot use a pre-registered sample and comparison condition, and what outcome would genuinely refute the central hypothesis?
6. Should vWAN P2S remain an early **advisory** seed until the corrected sizing table, downtime model and customer change process are proven?
7. Who funds and governs a savings dividend, and can it be honoured across shared costs, commitments, chargeback models and annual budget cycles?
8. Which two or three initial playbooks are important enough to select — and which attractive ideas are explicitly parked or retired?

| **[1] P2S capacity is banded, not linear** | **CONFIRMED — and it exposes an error in our work.** The FAQ table reads: scale units **1–3 → 500** concurrent connections; **4–6 → 1,000**; **7–10 → 5,000**; **11–20 → 10,000**; then one more pair of gateway instances per extra 20 units, each pair adding 10,000 (**40 → 20,000 … 100 → 50,000 … 200 → 100,000**).
