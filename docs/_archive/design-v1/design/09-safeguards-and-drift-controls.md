# Design 09 — Safeguards and drift controls

> **What this answers:** *What can silently change under us, in us and around us — and for each of those, what prevents it, what notices it and what puts it right?* And: *which safeguards stand between an AI mistake and an irreversible action?* Platform facts come from the [Foundry](research/foundry-agent-service.md), [governance and Agent Framework](research/foundry-governance-and-framework.md), [Citadel](research/citadel.md) and [security](research/security-and-compliance.md) research, checked on 2026-10-31. Part of the [design](README.md); principle **P9**, "drift is a first-class risk", is made concrete here.

---

## 1. What drift means here

**Drift is any divergence between three states that are supposed to agree:** what is **declared** (the repository and the release manifest), what was **evaluated** (the version the exam and the gates passed) and what is **actual** (what is serving traffic right now).

An AI system drifts on three clocks, and a design that watches only one is blind to the others:

| Clock | Who changes it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Ours** | our people and pipelines — or someone bypassing them | a portal edit to a gateway policy; a role assignment that was never removed |
| **The platform's** | Microsoft, under an unchanged label | the model router's pool grows inside version `2025-11-18`; a preview feature changes its schema; a service retires |
| **The world's** | prices, billing data, people, attackers | a new meter; a changed export schema; a poisoned tag; an approver who stops reading |

The model router is the instructive case: its **pool lost five models between July and August 2026 under a version label that did not change**, and Standard deployments auto-upgrade unless told otherwise. "We pinned the version" is therefore not enough; every drift control below checks the *behaviour and the actual state*, not the label.

**Each layer has three controls** — one that **prevents**, one that **detects**, one that **corrects** — and each control says whether the correction is **automatic** (the declared state is authoritative, the correction is reversible and the blast radius is small) or **human-approved**. A layer with no detective control is not controlled; a layer with a detective control and no corrective one is a pager.

Drift also has a *downstream* side. When something that a claim, a Detector, a Move, a piece of evidence or a credential **depends on** changes, everything that relied on it stops relying on it *first* and is re-examined *after*. That is a dependency graph, not a habit ([§3](#3-the-drift-controller)).

#### The lock procedure for stack resource groups

Learn's interim mitigation for the move bypass is a **read-only lock on the resource group**: it blocks moves in and out while stack operations keep working, and is lifted for a stack update and re-applied ([known issues](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-resource-manager/bicep/deployment-stacks-known-issues)). A read-only lock also blocks every ARM `POST` ([lock guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-resource-manager/management/lock-resources)), so the procedure is applied **per resource group, by class** (to confirm per resource type):

1. **Groups the runtime does not touch through ARM** (network, registry, contracts, policy, ledger storage) carry the lock. Only the *bootstrap* stage lifts and re-applies it, each event is a ledger entry, and a lock found missing outside a pipeline run is a drift finding that a person approves re-applying.
2. **Groups the runtime starts or scales through ARM** cannot take it without blocking that call. Routine identities there use a Contributor-style custom role **without** `Microsoft.Resources/subscriptions/resourceGroups/moveResources/action`, which a move needs on its source group ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-resource-manager/management/move-resource-group-and-subscription)); D17 raises the alarm.
3. Creating or deleting locks (`Microsoft.Authorization/locks/*`) belongs to the bootstrap identity only.
4. Each month the planted-drift job moves a harmless resource out of a test stack, with the right and the scope that [§6](#6-testing-the-safeguards) states: D17 must fire within its service level and, in a locked group, the move must be refused.

**On people.** DR-17 watches the *role*, not the person: **no individual score**, no per-person latency or ratio, **no inference about a named person's working rhythm**. The aggregate job reads an approval view with no actor column, and a cohort under five is not reported. The only personal data kept about approvers is their **contact preferences** (working hours and language, stated by the person, opt-in), used to choose *when and how to ask* and never to evaluate anyone ([06 §5](06-data-and-knowledge.md#5-estate-knowledge-privacy-by-design), [08 §2.2](08-governance-and-compliance.md#22-what-it-means-for-the-design); whether any remaining use counts as monitoring of workers stays *to confirm with counsel*). The price is stated in [§4](#4-the-safeguards-between-a-mistake-and-an-irreversible-action): one approver's habit shows only through the uniform friction and sampled case review. A **contract test** keeps the line: the build fails if the estate service returns a per-person field other than contact preferences ([10 §2](10-test-and-evaluation.md#2-the-layers), L2).

## 3. The drift controller

Detectors are **small, deterministic jobs**, not agents. Together they are the *drift controller*: a set of Container Apps jobs that each compare *declared* with *actual* for one layer and emit a **drift finding**. The jobs run in `snet-aca`, the internal tools environment, beside the Estate service ([04 §6](04-landing-zone-and-citadel.md#6-network)).

```yaml
finding: 1
id: df-2026-10-31-0007
layer: DR-5                     # agent runtime
expected: skeptic@sha256:aa… 
actual:   skeptic@sha256:bb…
severity: high
reversible: true
auto_correct: allowed           # declared state is authoritative
invalidates: [ev-g4-0412, cred-0012]   # consumers found through the dependency graph (below)
```

1. **Findings are typed records** in the Ledger and the audit ledger, never free text.
2. A **correction policy** (deterministic) decides: *auto-correct* only when the declared state is authoritative, the correction is reversible and its blast radius is in the *small* class; otherwise a **case** is opened for a person.
3. The **Auditor** agent reads the stream of findings to find *patterns* (the same layer drifting weekly means a process is wrong), and the **Conductor** puts the patterns on the Agenda.
4. **The controller is not switched off by what it watches.** It has its own identities, runs from the governance plane, and each detector sends a **heartbeat** to a dead-man's-switch alert in Azure Monitor: silence is an alert.
5. The controller measures itself: **mean time to detect**, **mean time to correct**, the share of findings corrected automatically, and findings older than their service level. These are design targets for the first quarter, not promises.
6. **Every detector, and every automatic correction claimed in §2, is fault-injected** in a test estate ([§6](#6-testing-the-safeguards)); a detector that has never found a planted fault does not count as a control.
7. **The controller also watches approvers' standing.** Every five minutes it polls the Estate service's standing endpoint for every **in-flight approval** (approved, not done) and every **active lease**; the jobs call it from `snet-aca` under the app role `Standing.Read` and their own Entra token, and the endpoint takes VNet ingress from the named caller subnets only ([04 §6](04-landing-zone-and-citadel.md#6-network)). When an approver's standing is gone it creates a **deny entry for that approval** in `controls`, a narrowing item, and the runner's fresh read before its next step stops the Move. The Estate service keeps its read-only Graph permission and gains no write right. Which Graph call gives a consistent read is *to confirm* in S-ID-3 ([07 §3.4](07-security-architecture.md#34-conditional-access-and-the-licence-line)).

#### Evidence, invalidation and withdrawal

Every release gate ([10 §4](10-test-and-evaluation.md#4-release-gates)) emits **signed, time-bounded evidence** keyed to the release manifest. Every record that something else relies on (a claim, a Detector, a Move, an evidence record, a credential) carries `depends_on` edges to what it was built from: source hashes, data snapshots, tool, evaluator and judge versions, the manifest, other claims. The producing service writes them when it creates the record; the graph is their union, kept in the Ledger. When a detector reports a change in a node, the controller **invalidates every consumer at once** (a consumer refuses an invalidated record); only then does the slower work begin: repair or roll back, re-run the exam or re-check and, for a credential, a person confirms.

```mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Valid: evidence signed
    Valid --> Blocked: a node it depends on changed
    Valid --> Expired: evidence reaches its time limit
    Expired --> Retesting: scheduled re-check
    Blocked --> Repairing: repair or roll back
    Repairing --> Retesting: fix ready
    Retesting --> Valid: passes and for a credential a person confirms
    Retesting --> Blocked: fails
    Blocked --> Retired: cannot be repaired
    Retired --> [*]
```

A **credential** is the last node: bound to a manifest, a domain, a cap and an expiry, it is **withdrawn automatically** when a node its evidence rests on is invalidated, and granted again only by a person after a fresh exam (G9, [10 §4](10-test-and-evaluation.md#4-release-gates)). **A withdrawal has its own deny list.** It writes the credential's id to the **credential deny list** in `controls`, separate from the all-approvals epoch, and the broker checks the credential's **live status**, its own signed record (scope, cap, class and expiry, never the caps that a permit states) and that list at grant, before it takes the lease, and the runner checks them again: an approval that rests on a withdrawn credential and sits in the `dispatch` queue is refused, and no other approval is touched. The graph is only as good as its edges: the producing service refuses a record without `depends_on`, and every node type has a planted change in the drills ([§6](#6-testing-the-safeguards)).

## 4. The safeguards between a mistake and an irreversible action

The drift catalogue keeps the system *as designed*. The safeguards below keep a *wrong proposal* from becoming a *wrong change*. They are layered so that no single failure — a fooled model, a hurried human, a stale number, a compromised service — is enough, and one rule closes them: **no change without a ledger entry**. Every effect is preceded by a signed **intent** entry that its actor reads back, and followed by an **outcome** entry: the broker writes `grant-intent`, grants, then writes `grant-outcome`; the runner writes `step-intent`, acts, then writes `step-outcome`; the broker writes `revoke-intent`, revokes, then writes `revoke-outcome`. A job reconciles any intent that has no outcome after its window against the live state (D15 for a grant, D19 for a ledger gap). The one exemption is a rollback to the pre-state named in the signed record, which runs from a signed outbox and is flagged D19.

The order is that of W4 ([05 §3](05-agents-and-orchestration.md#3-the-workflows)): the **Policy service** signs a permit first; the Brief shows the target and the permit id; a **person** approves; the **Approval service** signs the approval record, records the position of the approval's own ledger entry in a signed `approval-position` marker and only then enqueues its id; the **broker** verifies everything itself, **reads that block from the ledger**, takes the lease and grants; the **runner**, an event-driven job, verifies again and acts; the **Verifier** compares the result with the prediction. *No single credential completes a change that needs a person's web-page approval*; the residual is stated below the table. The signer starts nothing because Learn says that *Jobs - Start* "accepts an optional execution template that can override the main and init container images, commands, and environment variables", and a job can use any managed identity configured for it ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/container-apps/jobs#permissions)): whoever may start a chain job can run any image as that job's identities, and whoever can write the job changes what its next execution runs. The write right sits with the pipeline that deploys the environment (`deploy` for dev and test, `release` for production). The production `release` pipeline holds `jobs/write` and **not** `jobs/start/action`, because event-driven and scheduled jobs need no manual start; the dev and test `deploy` pipeline may start a job for a smoke test. In production a queue message starts each execution of the broker, the runner and the Verifier, and the schedule starts the Verifier's observer.

#### The authority chain

Then it **takes the lease** (a conditional create in `leases`) and reads `controls` **fresh** for a deployment lock item of the environment: if one exists it deletes its own lease, grants nothing and puts the message back with a delay. Otherwise it writes a signed `grant-intent` **and reads it back**, grants a constrained role to the execution identity, writes `grant-outcome` and enqueues the id on `run`. It creates its signed `start` marker (`started(attempt n)`), writes `step-intent` **and reads it back**, acts as the leased identity, writes `step-outcome`, ends the Move by creating its signed *done* marker in `approvals-index` and enqueues `execution finished`. **What the chain claims, and what remains.** *No single credential completes a change that needs a person's web-page approval.* A compromised Approval service cannot start a job, fabricate a permit or fabricate a person's Entra-signed token. On the card path (reversible Moves of the lowest class only) the proof is the bot channel's assertion, which a compromised Approval service can fabricate; that is why the path ends at that class. A compromised Approval and Policy service can approve within an autonomy credential's constraints, which the broker and the runner check against the credential itself. A compromised broker can grant constrained Move roles without a lease; a compromised release pipeline can run code as a runner identity while it holds a role. Each is bounded and detected as stated in [07 §3.3](07-security-architecture.md#33-the-execution-identity-just-in-time-without-pim). A compromised Approval service can still withhold, delay or replay; the nonce marker and the expiry stop a replay.

The approval page is the person's display: a compromised page can show one transaction and submit another. The page therefore shows the target's short code (the first eight characters of the target hash and the permit id), the Brief in the second channel shows the same code, D13 cross-checks every approval against Entra sign-in logs, and cards approve only the lowest reversible class. The nonce proves that the token was minted for this request, target and decision, after the request existed, at the required authentication strength. It does not prove that a click preceded it, because the app chooses the nonce (Learn: "A value generated and sent by your app", [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/entra/identity-platform/v2-protocols-oidc)), so a compromised page can **induce** an approve sign-in without a click. The person compares the short code in the second channel before the click; the two-approver rule for the three web-only classes (irreversible Moves, purchases, credential grants), D13 and the autonomy cap bound the rest. No receipt is added, because the component that serves the page would send it.

The broker can grant the constrained Move roles to the execution identities at any scope where the estate opted in, whether or not one is leased, because the delegation cannot tell. A leftover is usable only by that class's runner job, which wakes for any message in its queue, believes nothing in it and verifies the approval, the permit, the person's token, the lease and the live state itself; D15 flags any grant that matches no signed approval, and the delete-only sweeper removes leftovers. The sweeper writes nothing and signs nothing: it holds no signing key, no ledger role and no create right on `approvals-index`, so the Verifier's **observer** job records each removal from the Activity Log (one signed `sweep-observed` entry per event id, from a 24-hour window that every run re-reads, so an Audit Writer outage or a late delivery only delays it) and raises the alert "a leftover grant was removed" (that the delete event names the sweeper's principal is *to confirm* in S-ID-3).

A compromised release pipeline, the only holder of `Microsoft.App/jobs/write`, can run code as a runner identity while that identity holds a role. That is bounded by the lease (one target, one Move class, minutes), by signed-image admission (images by digest from our private registry, built by the pinned pipeline) and by the two-approver release rule, and it is detected by D16. Two controls narrow it. The production `release` pipeline holds `jobs/write` and **not** `jobs/start/action`. A **deployment lock** keeps a deployment from overlapping a lease, on both sides. The pipeline reads `leases` and waits until none is held, creates a lock item that expires after 30 minutes, waits one cache period (60 seconds, which only the services that cache `controls` need), reads `leases` fresh and writes a job only if none is held; before every write it re-reads its lock item and creates a successor lock if less than the step's longest duration plus one cache period is left. Its `timeout-minutes` (25) is shorter than the lock's `ttl` (30), so a stalled deployment ends before its lock does, and a pipeline that loses its lock stops writing and fails the run. The broker, after it creates its lease and before it writes `grant-intent`, reads `controls` fresh for a lock item of the environment, and if one exists deletes its own lease, grants nothing and puts the message back with a delay. Either the broker's fresh read sees the lock, or the lock was created after that read and so after the lease, and the pipeline's fresh read sees the lease. That needs **no timing assumption** because the control state is read **linearizably**: the operational store's account default is **Strong** consistency (a single region), of which Learn says "Strong consistency offers a linearizability guarantee" ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cosmos-db/consistency-levels)), and only readers outside the control plane relax to Session per request. Under Session, reads to a partition that the client never wrote "behave as reads with Eventual Consistency", so a measured lag would be no guarantee. S-ID-3 confirms that Strong works on a serverless account (*to confirm*), measures its request units and latency, and runs both lock races under Strong and, as a control, under Session. The fallback is Bounded Staleness (single-region minimum: 10 write operations or 5 seconds), a wait of at least 60 seconds taken from that documented bound, and the claim that a Move stops at its next step within the staleness bound plus the step time; if neither level is available, the classes that need the lock or the kill switch stay disabled. The lock narrows the window; it does not stop a compromised pipeline.

**The queues.** One dedicated storage account, `stchain`, in each chain environment (ZRS, shared-key and public access off) holds the four queues `dispatch`, `run` (one per Move class), `verify` and `spine-events`, each with a `-poison` queue (`dispatch-poison` and so on); a message carries an **id only**. The account's private endpoint is in `snet-pe`: Learn's subnet delegation overview says a delegated subnet "Can't be used with a private endpoint if the subnet is delegated." ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/virtual-network/subnet-delegation-overview)), and the Container Apps environments of `snet-exec` sit in delegated subnets. Network security group rules allow `snet-exec` and `snet-spine` to the endpoint's address on 443 only. The senders are these exactly: `dispatch` takes the Approval service and, as its consumer, the broker; `run` (each class) takes the broker and, as its consumer, that class's runner; `verify` takes the spine only (the Verifier holds the plain Processor role and renews nothing); `spine-events` takes the Approval service, the runner and the Verifier and, as its consumer, the spine; `spine-signals`, in the task-hub account, takes the case-record facade and the Brief service and, as its consumer, the spine. A party outside a queue's sender set is **refused by RBAC** when it tries to send, so the one message that a non-sender can still put is a **consumer's own**: the broker into `dispatch`, a runner into its class's `run`, the spine into its own queues, because Put Message accepts `messages/write`, which the worker role holds ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/rest/api/storageservices/authorize-with-azure-active-directory#permissions-for-calling-data-operations)). That is safe because **a queue is a doorbell**: a job wakes for any message in its queue, believes nothing in it and verifies the record, the signatures, the lease and the live state itself, so such a message is at most a denial of service: the job refuses it (no record, no signature, no lease), counts it, and moves it to the `-poison` queue with an alert once its failures exceed three. The control is the verification, not the queue's access list. Each party's role assignment is **scoped to its own queue** (queue-level scope is supported for the built-in queue data roles, [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/storage/queues/secure-queues)); a **condition on the queue name** is the generally available alternative but covers only the five message actions ([attributes](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/storage/queues/queues-auth-abac-attributes)); that the queue trigger and the runner job's scale rule work with a queue-scoped role carrying the least data-plane right the scaler needs is *to confirm* in S-ID-3, and one storage account per queue pair is the fallback. The scale rule authenticates with a managed identity, which Learn documents for Azure Queue Storage scale rules ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/container-apps/managed-identity#use-managed-identity-for-scale-rules)); on an event-driven *job* that is *to confirm*. The spine reads `spine-events` through a queue-triggered function in its own app, so no chain service holds a Durable management key. Its identity holds what that trigger needs on `spine-events` and `spine-signals` (it consumes, deletes and updates a message to retry it: the `acia-queue-worker` role, whose minimal set is *to confirm* in S-ID-3) and **Sender on `spine-events-poison` and `spine-signals-poison`**, because the Functions runtime writes a poison message with the host's identity ("the functions runtime adds a message to a queue named `<originalqueuename>-poison`", [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-functions/functions-bindings-storage-queue-trigger#poison-messages)).

**Processing a message.** Each chain job runs with `parallelism` 1, `replicaCompletionCount` 1 and `replicaRetryLimit` 0. Learn says only that "Each execution of a job typically performs a single unit of work." ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/container-apps/jobs)), so **exactly one execution is not claimed**. The message's visibility timeout exceeds the longest Move plus the RBAC propagation wait, and the job renews it by updating the message while the Move runs. Storage queues have no dead-letter queue, so our code moves a message to the queue's `-poison` queue, with an alert, when its failures exceed three. The queue's `DequeueCount` rises on **every** dequeue (Learn: it "is incremented each time the message is subsequently dequeued", [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/rest/api/storageservices/get-messages)), so a wait would count as a failure; we count them apart: **`failures = DequeueCount − waits > 3`**, where `waits` is a counter in the message text (the ids, `waits`, `first_seen`) that the **broker**, the only party that waits, rewrites with Update Message each time it puts the message back to wait for a leased identity or a deployment lock, and **signs with its entry key** each time; it verifies the text on every dequeue. A message whose counter is missing or does not verify counts as `waits = 0`, so a counter injected by a sender (the Approval service can put to `dispatch`) changes nothing: the message's own `DequeueCount` applies, and the fourth dequeue moves it to `dispatch-poison`; an invalid counter on a message that was dequeued before raises a D14-class alert, "queue message tampered". Waiting is bounded by the decision's own validity: after the permit's `expiry` the message is closed as *expired, not run*, the spine is told, and nothing is poisoned. Marker ids are deterministic from their logical key, and **a create that returns HTTP 409 is never retried under another id**: the party reads the existing item. If it verifies and is this party's own earlier write for the same logical key, the delivery is a **redelivery**, and the party reconciles and resumes. If it verifies but belongs to another approval id, it is a **replay**, refused and raised as D20. If it does not verify, or belongs to another producer or content, it is a **preemption**: the step fails closed, the decision is refused and closed (a new proposal gets a new decision id, challenge and nonce), and D14 names the writer's principal, to whom the kill switch for that identity or key applies. Preemption can deny a decision; it cannot forge one, and the writers of `approvals-index` are a short list of named parties, so the squatter is identified and stopped. The broker's redelivery therefore **reconciles, then resumes**, as the runner's does: it reads the lease, the `grant-intent` and `grant-outcome` entries and the live RBAC state, and finishes or rolls back. The runner's `start` marker records `started(attempt n)`, and a redelivery that finds it without a `done` marker does the same. S-ID-3 kills the broker after its nonce marker and the runner after the nonce marker, after the intent, after the effect and before the done marker, and sends one message through ten waits, one through four crashes and one that carries an injected counter through four crashes.

**Leases.** An execution identity serves one Move at a time, fenced by a lease token that the **broker creates, replaces and deletes** in the `leases` container; the item is signed by the broker, and a reader ignores one that fails verification and raises D14. The broker takes the lease by a conditional create before it grants. The runner only **reads** the lease, before every step, ends its Move by creating a signed *done* marker in `approvals-index` and enqueues `execution finished`; the broker, still running, writes `revoke-intent`, removes the role and deletes the lease only after the **revocation probe** passes. The probe has two parts: the assignment list shows none, and after the propagation wait a call that the revoked role would have allowed, made as the execution identity, **fails**. An **activation probe** runs before the Move; until the revocation probe passes the identity is **quarantined**. RBAC changes can take up to ten minutes to take effect ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/role-based-access-control/troubleshooting)), so approval and purchase windows allow for it.

**If the Audit Writer is down**, no approval is dispatched and no grant is made, and a Move in flight stops at its next step because it cannot write its intent. A person's signed approval is **kept** in `approval-records` and dispatched when its ledger entry commits, and a **rollback to the pre-state named in the signed record is exempt**: it runs from a signed outbox, the gap is flagged D19 and it is reconciled afterwards. The Verifier's observer appends nothing while the writer is down, and its next run appends what it missed from its 24-hour window (the run at 03:00 UTC covers seven days, so a longer outage is caught too, with the gap alert), because the sweeper's removal was never gated.

**What stops when** ([07 §10.3](07-security-architecture.md#103-kill-switches)). A suspended contract stops in **seconds**. Disabling an agent identity stops **new tokens only**: an issued token lives until it expires (about an hour, possibly a day for managed identities; to confirm for agent identities), so the fast stop for one agent is its contract or the hub's **deny list on its `azp`**. The execution plane's switch stops **new grants and dispatches within the cache bound of at most 60 seconds** (a stale cache fails closed); role removal takes **minutes**. Every chain service reads `controls` with that cache, with one exception that matters: **the runner reads the epoch, the deny lists, the stop flag and its credential's grant record fresh, without the cache, before every step, and the broker reads them fresh at grant**, each a linearizable read at the account's default consistency (Strong, see the deployment lock above). So a Move in flight stops at its next step, in seconds, when the epoch is raised, a deny entry is added, the stop flag is set or its credential is withdrawn (under the Bounded Staleness fallback: at its next step, within the staleness bound plus the step time); the other parties take up to the cache bound. A switch pulled is an unsigned, create-only item that can only narrow, and every narrowing item alerts and names its creator; an item that widens authority (a credential grant, a flag set on, an epoch lowered) carries the signature of the party that may widen it, and readers ignore one that does not. Three switches guard the approvals: raising the **approval epoch** voids every earlier approval (a restore does the same, [06 §7.6](06-data-and-knowledge.md#76-recovery)), the **signing key's deny list** makes verifiers refuse that key's approvals, and the **credential deny list** withdraws one autonomy credential without touching any other approval ([§3](#evidence-invalidation-and-withdrawal)).

## 6. Testing the safeguards

A control that is not exercised is a hope. Every control here has a test in a named layer ([10 §2](10-test-and-evaluation.md#2-the-layers)):

- **Deterministic controls** (the target hash, the nonce, the signature checks, the leases, the sweeper, the correction policy, the ledger schema) have **property tests**: *an approval can never cover a target other than its own* is a property, and so is *a lease can never be held twice*. A forged, expired, replayed, re-targeted, pre-epoch or denied-key approval, a permit that the Policy service did not sign, a token with the wrong audience, nonce or `auth_time`, and an approval that rests on a withdrawn credential (the all-approvals epoch unchanged) are each refused by the broker **and** by the runner, tested on its own; a token obtained for a decline, a duplicated token, one person in both hats and the requester as an approver are refused the same way; an approve sign-in that starts before a click on Approve (when the page opens, in the background, after another action) fails the test, and a decline never yields a token with the approve nonce; two tokens with the same `oid`, and the requester or the preparer as an approver (by `SHA-256(decision id ‖ oid)`), are refused by the broker and again by the runner, from the tokens themselves; a stale `iat` against `appended_at`, and an approval appended after its permit's expiry, are refused; a permit whose caps exceed the credential's own signed record is refused; a marker or lease that its producer did not sign is ignored and raises D14, and so is a signed marker **copied to another id, partition or type**; a create that returns 409 for an item that does not verify is a **preemption** that fails closed (the decision is refused and closed, and D14 names the writer), for the party's own item a redelivery, and for another approval's item a replay (D20); an unsigned item in `controls` that widens authority is ignored; a grant for an approval with no signed `approval-position` marker, or whose ledger entry cannot be read from the block that the marker names, is refused, and no grant, step or revocation can be made without a read-back intent entry; every identity but the Approval service fails when it tries to sign with the approval key, and every identity but the Policy service with its key; **the ledger entry schema rejects any name, e-mail, UPN, `oid` or Teams-id field**; a producer that proceeds without reading its block back, and a writer that acknowledges without appending, are caught. **Role-assignment assertions** (L0) check that only the pipeline that deploys the environment (`deploy` for dev and test, `release` for production) holds `jobs/write` on the chain's jobs, the Verifier's observer included, that the production `release` pipeline holds no `jobs/start/action`, that the Approval service holds no `Microsoft.App/jobs/*` action, that no signing identity holds `keys/update/action`, that only the broker holds a write role on `leases`, that the pipeline holds read on `leases` and `controls` and create (not replace or delete) on `controls`, that the sweeper is delete-only and holds no signing key, no ledger role and no create right on `approvals-index`, that the spine holds Sender on `spine-events-poison` and `spine-signals-poison` and that only its identity holds the app role `Run.Manage`, that no identity holds `replace` or `delete` on `cases` or `case-notes` (the case-record facade holds create and read only, so evidence is create-only by RBAC) and that only the facade holds `replace` on `runs`, where no other identity holds any right (the export job's change-feed read apart, because `runs` is exported with the rest of the store), that every queue's senders are exactly the sets of [§4](#the-authority-chain), that the operational store's default consistency is Strong, and that the observer's template has `replicaTimeout` 600 seconds, below its 15-minute schedule, with `parallelism` 1, `replicaCompletionCount` 1 and `replicaRetryLimit` 0.
- **Drift detectors** have **planted drift**: a scheduled job introduces a harmless drift in a test spoke and the detector must find it within its service level. The set covers an extra role assignment, a changed alias and a public-access flip, and, added after the first review round: a resource **moved out of a test stack** (D17); an agent version registered **without `invocations_moderation`**; a pinned alias pointed at a **different model**; a **role assignment without a signed approval** (D15) and an **approval without an Entra sign-in** (D13); a lease violation, a **forged marker** or a signed marker **copied to another id** (D14), and a **preempted marker id** (the decision is refused and D14 names the writer); **a grant with no prior intent entry** (D15); **a leftover grant removed by the sweeper** (the Verifier's observer must record it once, append `sweep-observed` and alert; with the observer killed and the Audit Writer down, the next run must record it exactly once from its 24-hour window; a deletion that the Activity Log delivers after 40 minutes must still be recorded, and one delivered after 25 hours recorded with the D19-class gap alert; two runs must not overlap, because the 10-minute timeout is below the 15-minute schedule); **an approval entry that the writer acknowledged and did not append** (D19); **a start of a test chain job by another principal, or with an altered template** (D16); **a signing identity granted `keys/update/action`** (DR-19); **an exemption added to a high-risk policy** (DR-7: a finding in minutes, not at the next cycle); a deployment output that carries a key; an `x-policy-id` header on a request; a free-text field longer than the content-safety window with an injection after character 1,000, another after character 10,000 and one **across a 1,000-character response-window boundary** (all must be caught: Foundry guardrails see only the first 1,000 characters, so the sanitiser chunks and the hub screens each window, with its overlap set); and a **change to each node type of the dependency graph** (a source hash, a tool version, an evaluator), which must invalidate every consumer and withdraw a test credential within its service level.
- **Who may move the planted resource.** No routine identity holds the move right (`Microsoft.Resources/subscriptions/resourceGroups/moveResources/action`); the **bootstrap identity** (*Azure Deployment Stack Owner* plus `Microsoft.Authorization/locks/*`) is its only holder. The **planted-drift job** runs in the test spoke only, with a **time-boxed assignment** of the right that ends with the run, and never for a production scope ([07 §3.1](07-security-architecture.md#31-who-has-an-identity)).
- **Every detector and every claimed automatic correction is fault-injected**; one that has not found a planted fault is not counted as a control.
- **The chain is fault-injected too** (L6, [10 §2](10-test-and-evaluation.md#2-the-layers)): the broker is killed after its nonce marker, and the runner after the nonce marker, after the intent, after the effect and before the done marker, and each restart must reconcile and resume without a repeated or a lost effect; a message that waits ten times must not reach its `-poison` queue, and one that crashes four times must, with an alert, whether or not it carries an injected counter (`failures = DequeueCount − waits`); a send by a party outside the queue's sender set is refused by RBAC, and a consumer's own put (the broker into `dispatch`, a runner into its class's `run`, the spine into its own queues) is refused by the job, counted, moved to `-poison` after three failures and alerted; a deployment started while a lease is held must wait, and **both lock races** are forced: the broker is paused between its read and its lease while the pipeline takes the lock (the broker's fresh read after the lease, or the pipeline's fresh read after the wait, must catch it), and a deployment is stalled past its 25-minute `timeout-minutes` or left without a successor lock (the pipeline must stop writing and fail the run). Under Strong none of these races may overlap, and the **epoch race** (the epoch raised between two steps of a Move) must stop the Move at its next step; a **control run with the same reads relaxed to Session** shows that the setting matters.
- **The privacy line of DR-17 is a contract test** (L2): the estate service returns contact preferences only, and the aggregate job's approval view has no actor column.
- **Kill switches** are pulled in the monthly resilience drills and **timed**: each drill records the real latency of every level against [07 §10.3](07-security-architecture.md#103-kill-switches), including the **approval epoch** (an approval signed before the raise is refused by broker and runner), the **signing-key deny list** (verifiers refuse that key's approvals), the **credential deny list** (one credential's queued approval is refused) and the hub's **deny list on `azp`**; a switch pulled in `controls` must be honoured within the **60-second cache bound**, and an epoch raised, a deny entry added, the stop flag set or a credential withdrawn while a Move is running must stop it at its **next step**, because the runner and the broker read those fresh.
- **The canary** and the **planted injections** run continuously; a silent success fails the release gate.

## 7. Design targets

Targets for the first quarter, to be validated, not promised: **time to detect** a configuration, identity or gateway drift within 15 minutes; a **behavioural** drift within 24 hours; **100 % of hub and agent changes** arriving through the pipeline (anything else is a finding); **no preview feature in the path that is absent from the register**; an **invalidating change** reaching every consumer, and withdrawing any credential built on it, within one detection cycle (15 minutes for configuration nodes); **no forged, replayed or re-targeted approval accepted** in the property tests; **no change without a ledger entry**, and no grant, step or revocation without a prior intent entry; **no signing identity holding `keys/update/action`** and, in production, no start of a chain job by any principal but its trigger; and the **share of findings corrected automatically** reported every week.
